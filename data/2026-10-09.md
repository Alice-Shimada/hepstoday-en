### [1] [Tau and dark sector searches at Belle and Belle II](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11335)
*Wenzhe Li*

Main category: hep-ex

TL;DR: This paper summarizes several BSM searches conducted by the Belle and Belle II collaborations, focusing on CP violation in tau decays, lepton-flavor-violating tau decays, axion-like particle production, and invisible B decays.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To probe physics beyond the Standard Model through high-precision studies of tau lepton decays, rare processes, and dark sector signatures using e+e− collision data from Belle and Belle II.

Method: Experimental analysis of e+e− collision data: (i) CP asymmetry measurement in τ⁻ → π⁻K_S⁰ν_τ; (ii) search for τ⁻ → ℓ⁻η (ℓ = e, μ); (iii) search for axion-like particles a via e+e− → γa (a → γγ) at Belle II; (iv) search for feebly interacting particles X_inv in B → h X_inv (h = π, K) using Belle data.

Result: No significant deviations from SM predictions are reported; upper limits are set on CP-violating asymmetry, branching fractions for τ⁻ → ℓ⁻η, axion-photon coupling g_{aγγ}, and invisible B decay branching ratios.

Conclusion: The analyses place competitive constraints on BSM scenarios involving tau lepton CP violation, lepton flavor violation, light pseudoscalars, and dark sector mediators; they demonstrate the sensitivity of Belle/Belle II to diverse new physics signatures.

Abstract: The Belle and Belle II experiments provide a powerful platform for beyond-the-Standard-Model searches, particularly in tau and dark sectors. This summary reports include a measurement of CP violation in $τ^- \to π^- K_S^0 ν_τ$, a search for $τ^- \to \ell^- η$, a search for axion-like particles via $e^+ e^- \to γa (\to γγ)$ using Belle II data, and a search for feebly interacting particles in $B \to h X_{\rm inv}$ decays using Belle data.

### [2] [Observation of single top-quark production in the $s$-channel in proton--proton collisions at $\sqrt{s}=13$ TeV with the ATLAS detector](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11465)
*ATLAS Collaboration*

Main category: hep-ex

TL;DR: First observation of s-channel single top-quark production at ATLAS using 140 fb⁻¹ of 13 TeV pp data; achieved via GNN-based background discrimination, yielding 5.1σ significance and SM-consistent cross-section.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To directly observe the highly suppressed s-channel single top-quark production—a key probe of top-quark electroweak couplings and potential new physics—previously undetected due to low cross-section and large backgrounds.

Method: Analyzed 140 fb⁻¹ of √s = 13 TeV pp collisions with ATLAS; selected events with one lepton (e/μ), missing transverse momentum, and two b-jets; employed a Graph Neural Network to discriminate s-channel signal from tt̄, W+jets, and t-channel single-top backgrounds; performed a binned likelihood fit to the GNN output score.

Result: Observed significance of 5.1σ (expected 5.2σ); measured combined s-channel cross-section σ_{tb̄+t̄b} = 10.8 ± 1.5 (stat.) ± 1.6 (syst.) pb, consistent with SM prediction.

Conclusion: This constitutes the first direct, statistically significant observation of s-channel single top-quark production, validating the SM description of top-quark electroweak interactions and establishing a new channel for precision tests and BSM searches.

Abstract: The first single-experiment observation of single top-quark production in the $s$-channel is presented. Measurements of this process are essential to fully characterize the electroweak interactions of the top quark and probe for new physics, however its highly suppressed cross-section has so far caused it to evade direct detection at the Large Hadron Collider. The analysis uses proton--proton collision data collected at a center-of-mass energy of $\sqrt{s}=13$ TeV with the ATLAS detector, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of $140$ fb$^{-1}$. The selected events contain exactly one electron or muon, missing transverse momentum, and exactly two $b$-quark-initiated jets. A Graph Neural Network is utilized to separate the signal from the top-quark pair, $W$+jets, and $t$-channel single top-quark backgrounds. A likelihood fit to the network output yields an observed (expected) signal significance of $5.1$ ($5.2$) standard deviations. The measured cross-section of $σ_{t\bar{b}+\bar{t}b} = 10.8 \pm 1.5 \text{ (stat.)} \pm 1.6 \text{ (syst.)}\,\text{pb}$ is consistent with the Standard Model prediction.
