### [1] [Measurement of the Higgs boson decay to a low-mass dilepton system and a photon in $pp$ collisions at $\sqrt{s} =$ 13 and 13.6 TeV with the ATLAS detector](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03369)
*ATLAS Collaboration*

Main category: hep-ex

TL;DR: ATLAS reports a combined measurement of Higgs boson decay to γℓℓ (ℓ = e, μ) with low dilepton mass (mℓℓ < 30 GeV), yielding μ = 1.03 ± 0.34 (observed) and 3.4σ significance—consistent with SM prediction.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Probe rare Higgs decay H → γℓℓ (ℓ = e, μ) in the low-dilepton-mass region (mℓℓ < 30 GeV), sensitive to potential BSM contributions (e.g., light new particles or anomalous couplings) and test SM predictions with improved statistics.

Method: Search for H → γee/γμμ events using ATLAS data: 164 fb⁻¹ at √s = 13.6 TeV (Run 3) and 139 fb⁻¹ at √s = 13 TeV (Run 2); perform statistical inference via profile likelihood fit to extract signal strength μ; combine results using correlated systematic uncertainties.

Result: Combined observed signal strength μ = 1.03⁺⁰·³⁵₋₀·₃₂ (expected μ = 1.00⁺⁰·³⁴₋₀·₃₂); observed significance = 3.4σ, expected = 3.3σ; Run 3-only result yields μ = 0.64⁺⁰·⁴⁴₋₀·₃₉ with 1.7σ observed significance.

Conclusion: The combined measurement is consistent with the Standard Model prediction (μ = 1), representing the most precise test to date of H → γℓℓ in the low-mass dilepton regime; no evidence for BSM physics is found.

Abstract: A measurement is presented for the Higgs boson decaying into a photon and a pair of electrons or muons with an invariant mass $m_{\ell\ell} <$ 30 GeV, using $pp$ collision data at $\sqrt{s} =$ 13.6 TeV recorded by the ATLAS detector at the Large Hadron Collider during the years 2022-2024, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 164 fb$^{-1}$. The best-fit value of the signal strength parameter, defined as the ratio of the observed signal yield to the one expected in the Standard Model, is measured to be $μ= 0.64^{+0.44}_{-0.39}$ compared with the expected value of $μ= 1.00^{+0.47}_{-0.41}$. The corresponding observed (expected) signal significance is 1.7 (2.5) standard deviations under the background-only hypothesis. The result is combined with a similar search performed using 139 fb$^{-1}$ of $pp$ collision data at $\sqrt{s} =$ 13 TeV collected during the years 2015-2018. This combination gives an observed (expected) signal strength of $μ= 1.03^{+0.35}_{-0.32}\,(μ= 1.00^{+0.34}_{-0.32})$, corresponding to an observed (expected) significance of 3.4 (3.3) standard deviations.

</details>

TL;DR: This paper reports 4.5σ evidence for C-even resonance production via two-photon interaction in e⁺e⁻ → f₁(1285) → ηπ⁰π⁰, measuring its cross section and e⁺e⁻ branching fraction using CMD-3 data at VEPP-2000.

Motivation: To search for the C-even resonance f₁(1285) produced via two-photon interaction in e⁺e⁻ annihilation — a process sensitive to its electromagnetic structure and quantum number constraints.

Method: Performed an energy scan (1.20–1.36 GeV) and dedicated high-statistics measurement at the f₁(1285) peak (1282 MeV) using the CMD-3 detector; analyzed e⁺e⁻ → ηπ⁰π⁰ events with 109.1 pb⁻¹ (scan) + 51.7 pb⁻¹ (peak) integrated luminosity.

Abstract: A search for the $e^+ e^-\to f_1(1285)$ reaction is performed with the CMD-3 detector at the VEPP-2000 collider.
