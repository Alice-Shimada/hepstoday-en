### [1] [Matrix Product State Representatives of Symmetry-Protected Phases](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09501)
*Thomas Jackson*

Main category: math-ph

TL;DR: This paper constructs explicit SO(3)×ℤ₂^T×ℤ₂^R-invariant matrix product states (MPS) for all combinations of ℤ₂ topological indices associated with time-reversal and reflection symmetry, demonstrating their existence for integer spin—especially spin-1—with bounded injectivity length (l ≤ 8); it supports and refines existing classifications by Chen–Gu–Wen and Tasaki–Ogata.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To explicitly realize all possible ℤ₂ topological phases classified by time-reversal (ℤ₂^T) and reflection (ℤ₂^R) symmetries via concrete, symmetric matrix product states—bridging abstract classification theory with constructive, computationally verifiable models.

Method: Constructs a simple, symmetry-adapted ansatz for SO(3)×ℤ₂^T×ℤ₂^R-invariant MPS; derives exact spin-1 examples; implements a Fortran program to generate representatives for arbitrary integer spin and index triples; supplements with random sampling for small spins.

Result: Every triple of ℤ₂ topological indices (trivial/non-trivial for time-reversal, reflection, and the dihedral D₂-related Ogata index) admits a spin-1 SU(2)-, time-reversal-, and site-reflection-symmetric MPS with injectivity length l ≤ 8; existence confirmed for all integer spins via algorithmic construction and numerical sampling.

Conclusion: The explicit construction validates the completeness of the ℤ₂ classification for these symmetries, confirms consistency with Chen–Gu–Wen and Tasaki’s equivalence between hidden order and Ogata’s D₂ index, and provides a practical computational framework for generating symmetric topological MPS.

Abstract: We provide a simple ansatz for finding integer spin $SO(3)\times\Z_2^T\times\Z_2^R$-invariant matrix product states representing a phase for each triple of $\Z_2$ topological indices studied in the literature by Tasaki \cite{tasakiheistop}\cite{tasakitop} and Ogata (corresponding to time-reversal and reflection symmetries) \cite{ogatatime}\cite{ogatareflection}. Every combination of trivial and non-trivial indices has a spin-$1$ $SU(2)$, time-reversal, and site-reflection symmetric MPS representative with injectivity length $l\leq 8$. We provide some exact examples for spin-$1$ and a Fortran program which will find representatives of any triple for any integer spin, as well as some data from random sampling for small spin. The existence of such states agrees with the classification of Chen, Gu, and Wen \cite{chen}, and with the recent results of Tasaki \cite{tasakinew} equating the hidden-order and one of Ogata's indices, wherein the Ogata index is that associated to the dihedral $D_2$ symmetry.

### [2] [Absence of Absolutely Continuous Spectrum for One-Dimensional Capacitance Operators with Critical Long-Range Interactions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09582)
*Habib Ammari,Jiayu Qiu*

Main category: math-ph

TL;DR: This paper proves absence of absolutely continuous spectrum for 1D Laurent operators with multiplicative disorder and critically slow (log-corrected 1/|n|) long-range hoppings—bypassing the usual quartic decay requirement—then applies it to the capacitance operator of a 3D subwavelength resonator chain, advancing toward Anderson localization in critical long-range wave systems.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To extend spectral localization theory to physically relevant wave systems with critical long-range interactions (e.g., subwavelength capacitance coupling), where standard off-diagonal decay assumptions (e.g., |n|^{-4}) fail but slower, logarithmically corrected 1/|n| decay holds.

Method: Uses finite-difference bounds on the convolution kernel of the Laurent operator to control spectral measures; avoids reliance on high-order off-diagonal decay by leveraging regularity of the kernel in discrete difference sense; applies abstract spectral theorem to the concrete capacitance operator via verified decay and finite-difference estimates.

Result: Rigorous proof of absence of absolutely continuous spectrum for scalar 1D Laurent operators under multiplicative disorder and hoppings decaying as |n|^{-1} log^{-β}|n| (β > 1); confirmed for the capacitance operator of a periodic 3D subwavelength resonator chain.

Conclusion: The result establishes a crucial spectral prerequisite—elimination of absolutely continuous spectrum—for Anderson localization in wave systems with critical long-range coupling; the remaining spectral type (pure point vs. singular continuous) requires further analysis.

Abstract: In this paper, we establish the absence of absolutely continuous spectrum for scalar one-dimensional Laurent operators with multiplicative disorder and critical long-range hoppings. In particular, the requirement of at least quartic off-diagonal decay rate in the existing theory is bypassed by exploiting the finite-difference bound of the convolution kernel, which is available even when the interaction decays only as $|n|^{-1}\log^{-β}|n|$ with $β>1$. We then apply the abstract main result to a concrete physical model, the capacitance operator in subwavelength physics. For a chain of three-dimensional resonators with one resonator per period, we verify the required decay and finite-difference estimates and conclude the absence of absolutely continuous spectrum. These results leave open the distinction between pure point and singular continuous spectrum, but constitute a key step in proving Anderson localization in wave systems with critical long-range interactions.

### [3] [Operator Schmidt Rank and the Geometry of Schmidt Profiles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09794)
*Mohsen Kian,Mohammad Sal Moslehian*

Main category: math-ph

TL;DR: This paper establishes exact bounds on the minimal operator Schmidt rank (OSR) of a unitary transformation needed to achieve a target Schmidt rank (SR) for a given bipartite vector, and derives inequalities controlling the full cumulative Schmidt coefficient profile under such unitaries; it further provides sharp majorization-based bounds on Schatten-norm distortion and Rényi entropy change.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To quantify how unitary operations with limited entangling power—measured by operator Schmidt rank—affect the Schmidt decomposition structure of bipartite quantum states, going beyond simple rank reachability to control the entire Schmidt coefficient distribution.

Method: Analytical derivation using Schmidt decomposition, operator Schmidt rank theory, majorization techniques, and inequalities for singular values and Schatten norms; employs combinatorial optimization over unitary constraints and extremal properties of Schmidt coefficients.

Result: 1) Exact formula for minimal OSR required to map a vector of Schmidt rank p to one of Schmidt rank q; 2) Cumulative Schmidt profile inequalities involving F_r(x); 3) A majorization relation implying sharp bounds on Schatten-p norm distortion and Rényi-α entropy changes under bounded-OSR unitaries.

Conclusion: Operator Schmidt rank fully governs not only achievable Schmidt ranks but also the fine-grained redistribution of entanglement across all Schmidt coefficients; the derived majorization relation unifies and strengthens prior results on entanglement manipulation limits under low-entangling unitaries.

Abstract: The main result of the paper states that if $0\neq v\in\mathbb C^m\otimes\mathbb C^n$, $d=\min\{m,n\}$, and $p=SR(v)$, then for every $1\le q\le d$, \begin{equation*}
  \min\left\{OSR(\mathbf U):
  \begin{array}{l}
  \mathbf U\in\mathbb M_m\otimes\mathbb M_n\text{ is unitary},
  SR(\mathbf Uv)=q
  \end{array}\right\}
  =
  \left\lceil\max\left\{\frac pq,\frac qp\right\}\right\rceil . \end{equation*} Here $OSR(\mathbf U)$ denotes the operator Schmidt rank of $\mathbf U$. Furthermore, if $\mathbf U\in\mathbb M_m\otimes\mathbb M_n$ is unitary and $OSR(\mathbf U)\le k$, then, for every unit vector $x\in\mathbb C^m\otimes\mathbb C^n$ and every $1\le r\le d$, \begin{equation*}
  F_{\min\{kr,d\}}(\mathbf Ux)\ge F_r(x)
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  F_{\min\{kr,d\}}(x)\ge F_r(\mathbf Ux), \end{equation*} where $F_r(x)$ denotes the sum of the $r$ largest squared Schmidt coefficients of $x$. This result is stronger than rank reachability as it controls the entire cumulative Schmidt profile. We also establish a majorization relation and use it to obtain sharp bounds on Schatten-norm distortion and on the change of Rényi entropies under unitaries with bounded operator Schmidt rank.

### [4] [Asymptotic Neutrality for the Ionization Conjecture in the Two-Center Thomas-Fermi-Dirac-von Weizsäcker Theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09997)
*Lu Chen,Yong Wu*

TL;DR: This paper advances the ionization conjecture for the Thomas-Fermi-Dirac-von Weizsäcker model in the two-center nuclear configuration, proving an upper bound on the number of bound electrons: N ≤ Z + C Z^{153/154}(1 + log Z)^{3/8}, with C independent of nuclear positions and charges.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The ionization conjecture—determining the maximal number of electrons a nuclear configuration can bind—remains open for multi-center (molecular) systems; this work addresses the previously unsolved two-center case.

Method: The proof employs comparison with the neutral Thomas-Fermi density, spatial localization, exterior L¹-estimates, and analysis of the screened Coulomb potential.

Result: A sharp sublinear correction bound N ≤ Z + C Z^{153/154}(1 + log Z)^{3/8} is established for the maximal number N of bound electrons in a two-nucleus system of total nuclear charge Z.

Conclusion: The ionization conjecture is advanced for the two-center TFDFW model; the method is robust and extendable to K-nucleus molecules with fixed K.

Abstract: The ionization conjecture for the Thomas-Fermi-Dirac-von Weizsäcker model asks how many electrons a given nuclear configuration can bind. So far, only the single-center case has been settled by Frank, Nam, and Van Den Bosch, while the original molecular case has still remained open. We advance this conjecture in the two-center case. We prove that for two distinct nuclei of total charge $Z$, the number $N$ of bound electrons satisfies $N\le Z+C Z^{153/154}(1+\log Z)^{3/8}$, with constants independent of the nuclear positions and the nuclear charges. The proof relies on a comparison with the neutral Thomas-Fermi density and on localization and exterior $L^1$-estimates coupled through the screened potential. Our results could be extended to molecules with any fixed number $K$ of nuclei.

### [5] [Iterated Graph Systems (II): Bernoulli percolation and critical-exponent universality classes on hierarchical lattices](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10469)
*Ziyu Neroli*

Main category: math-ph

TL;DR: This paper rigorously disproves dimension-based and scale-commensurability-based universality conjectures for Bernoulli percolation on hierarchical lattices generated by edge iterated graph systems (EIGS), using analysis of eight critical exponents and the six exponentials theorem.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To resolve open questions about universality in percolation on hierarchical lattices—specifically, whether substitution-multiplicative dimensions or commensurate renormalization scales determine critical-exponent universality, as previously conjectured by Hauser and Saxena.

Method: Analytical derivation and comparison of eight critical exponents for Bernoulli percolation on EIGS-generated hierarchical lattices; application of the six exponentials theorem from transcendental number theory to assess scale commensurability; construction of counterexamples to dimension-based and scale-based universality claims.

Result: Demonstration that no finite family of substitution-multiplicative dimensions fully determines universality class; explicit disproof of the conjecture linking critical-exponent universality to commensurate discrete renormalization scales.

Conclusion: Universality in Bernoulli percolation on hierarchical lattices cannot be reduced to either dimensional invariants or scale commensurability—highlighting the necessity of more refined structural or algebraic characterizations of the underlying lattice hierarchy.

Abstract: We study Bernoulli percolation and its universality classes on hierarchical lattices generated by edge iterated graph systems (EIGS). By examining eight critical exponents, we prove that no finite family of substitution-multiplicative dimensions completely determines universality, providing a rigorous proof of the failure of dimension-based universality discussed by Hauser and Saxena [Phys. Rev. B 34 (1986), 8193-8195]. Although the six exponentials theorem provides number-theoretic criteria for scale commensurability, we further disprove the conjecture that critical-exponent universality requires commensurate discrete renormalisation scales.
