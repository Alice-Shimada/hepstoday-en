### [1] [Thermal Giant Graviton Expansion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18987)
*Kasia Budzik,Atul Sharma*

Main category: hep-th

TL;DR: This paper constructs giant graviton expansions for thermal and graded partition functions of N=4 and N=1 SYM at zero 't Hooft coupling, using self-dual chiral limits and twistor string holography; it identifies nonperturbative corrections with D-instanton/anti-D-instanton pairs and reproduces Hagedorn temperatures via resummation at infinite N.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To provide a microscopic bulk origin for the Hagedorn singularities in free super Yang-Mills theories, where standard supergravity duals fail due to non-BPS nature and vanishing coupling.

Method: Uses quiver-generalized Murthy expansion rooted in twistor string holography applied to self-dual chiral zero-coupling limits of N=4 and N=1 SYM; matches terms to bulk closed string and D-brane sectors, including D-instanton/anti-D-instanton contributions.

Result: Obtains finite- and infinite-N giant graviton expansions; identifies nonperturbative corrections as D-instanton/anti-D-instanton pairs; resummation at infinite N reproduces exact Hagedorn temperatures of free gauge theories.

Conclusion: The Hagedorn transition in free SYM has a precise bulk interpretation in terms of resummed D-instanton effects in a twistor string–inspired holographic framework, bridging non-BPS thermal observables and nonperturbative string geometry.

Abstract: We construct giant graviton expansions of the thermal and $(-1)^F$-graded partition functions of $\mathcal{N}=4$ and $\mathcal{N}=1$ super Yang-Mills theories at zero 't Hooft coupling. These observables are generically non-BPS and do not admit a direct bulk interpretation in a supergravity regime. Instead, we exploit the corresponding self-dual theories, which arise as chiral zero-coupling limits and admit twistor string holographic duals. We apply a quiver generalization of Murthy's expansion motivated by twistor strings. Notably, we obtain expansions at both finite and infinite $N$. We reproduce the terms in these expansions from bulk closed string and D-brane sectors, identifying the nonperturbative corrections with D-instanton/anti-D-instanton pairs. At infinite $N$, resumming the D-instanton sectors reproduces the Hagedorn temperatures of the free gauge theories, providing a microscopic bulk origin for their Hagedorn singularities.

### [2] [Two-Species Boltzmann--Kac Kinetics with Matsumoto--Yor Nonlinear Collisions: Conserved Energy and Conic Equilibria](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18892)
*Yoshiaki Itoh*

Main category: math-ph

TL;DR: Extends Kac's kinetic model to a two-species system on (0,∞)² using the Matsumoto–Yor involution; identifies conserved energy and invariant measure; proves an H-theorem linking zero entropy production to statistical independence of post-collision velocities; derives GIG and Gamma as non-equilibrium equilibria via state-space analysis.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To generalize Kac’s single-species kinetic framework to multi-species, non-linear interaction settings with geometric and thermodynamic structure, moving beyond transform-domain methods to physical, state-space derivations.

Method: Uses the Matsumoto–Yor rational collision involution on (0,∞)²; constructs a non-linear conserved energy E(x,y) = x + y + 1/x and invariant conic metric measure dμ = dx dy / [x(x+y)]; establishes a two-species H-theorem via logarithmic gradient transformation law and separation of variables.

Result: Entropy production vanishes iff post-collision velocities are independent (I(U;V)=0); the functional-differential system uniquely characterizes Generalized Inverse Gaussian (GIG) and Gamma distributions as stationary solutions—i.e., non-equilibrium thermodynamic equilibria.

Conclusion: The Matsumoto–Yor involution provides a physically grounded, geometrically structured kinetic framework where classical thermodynamic principles (conservation, invariance, H-theorem) extend naturally to non-linear, multi-species settings, yielding explicit equilibrium distributions without Laplace transforms.

Abstract: We extend Mark Kac's kinetic framework from single-species Euclidean spaces to a two-species, non-linear interaction space on the open cone $(0, \infty)^2$ governed by the Matsumoto--Yor rational collision involution. We identify a non-linear conserved energy $E(x,y) = x + y + 1/x$ and an invariant conic metric measure $d\mu = \frac{dx \, dy}{x(x+y)}$. By establishing a two-species $H$-theorem, we prove that entropy production vanishes if and only if post-collision velocities are statistically independent ($I(U;V)=0$). In contrast to the transform-domain proof of Letac and Wesołowski~\cite{letac2000}, which relies on joint Laplace transforms of general measures, our approach yields a direct physical derivation in state space via the transformation law for logarithmic gradients and separation of variables. This uncouples the functional-differential system to uniquely characterize the Generalized Inverse Gaussian (GIG) and Gamma distributions as the non-equilibrium thermodynamic equilibria.

### [3] [Anomalies, higher symmetries, and homotopy theory I: crossed precosheaves and a nonabelian Cech construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19004)
*Anton Kapustin,Shixiong Xu*

Main category: math-ph

TL;DR: This paper introduces a homotopy-theoretic framework for 't Hooft anomalies using crossed precosheaves and Čech–Loday classifying spaces, linking localized symmetries to higher-group structures and recovering known symmetry indices in 1D/2D quantum spin systems.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To formalize how quantum symmetries—especially higher and anomalous ones—are encoded not by ordinary groups but by homotopy types, and to provide a rigorous operator-algebraic, localization-based definition of 't Hooft anomalies that generalizes beyond global group actions.

Method: Constructs a crossed precosheaf encoding spatially localized symmetry transformations and their commutator constraints; defines the Čech–Loday classifying space as a pointed connected homotopy type associated to finite region covers; forms a pro-object in the homotopy category as covers refine; analyzes the canonical map from the ordinary classifying space and its homotopy fiber as candidate higher-symmetry classifying space.

Result: A well-defined homotopy-theoretic anomaly obstruction to gauging; recovery of the Else–Nayak index in 1D and its 2D generalizations for quantum spin chains/lattices; explicit constructions for gauge transformations (continuum) and quantum cellular automata/finite-depth circuits (lattice).

Conclusion: Higher symmetries and 't Hooft anomalies are intrinsically homotopical and spatially local; the crossed precosheaf + Čech–Loday framework provides a unifying, mathematically precise, and physically grounded description applicable across continuum QFT and lattice models.

Abstract: Symmetries of a quantum field theory or a quantum lattice model are expected to be described not by a group but by a homotopy type, whose homotopy groups are the higher symmetry groups and whose Postnikov invariants encode 't Hooft anomalies. We propose a homotopy-theoretic definition of 't Hooft anomalies based on the structure of localized symmetries in the operator-algebraic approach. The input is a crossed precosheaf: a collection of groups attached to regions of space, equipped with pairings recording that the commutator of two transformations localized in two regions is localized in their intersection. To a crossed precosheaf and a finite family of regions we attach a pointed connected homotopy type, the Cech-Loday classifying space. As the family of regions ranges over finite covers of space, the classifying spaces form a pro-object in the homotopy category. There is a canonical map from the ordinary classifying space of the symmetry group to this pro-type. Composing this map with a group action gives an anomaly whose nontriviality obstructs gauging. In favorable cases, the Cech-Loday space is essentially independent of the cover. In such cases, the homotopy fiber of the canonical map is a candidate for the classifying space of the higher group of symmetries. We give examples of crossed precosheaves arising from gauge transformations in continuum field theory and from quantum cellular automata and finite-depth circuits. For quantum spin systems in one and two dimensions, our construction recovers the Else-Nayak index and its generalizations to two dimensions.

<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To establish a direct, self-contained theoretical framework for strain-gradient effects in one-dimensional mechanical metamaterials—bypassing reliance on three-dimensional parent materials or predefined stored energy functions—while ensuring objectivity and insensitivity to rigid-body virtual displacements.

Method: A direct theory of strain-gradient special Cosserat rods is formulated via the principle of virtual work; internal virtual work is expressed intrinsically in terms of variations of classical Cosserat strains and their spatial derivatives; localization yields field equations, higher-order contact interactions, natural boundary conditions, and momentum balances; constrained variational analysis handles inextensibility/unshearability; objective constitutive laws (elastic, hyperelastic, viscoelastic) and symmetry extensions (transverse hemitropy, isotropy, flip symmetry) are systematically derived; bifurcation analysis is performed on a planar inextensible cantilever under terminal compression using linearized spectral theory and global implicit function methods.

Result: Derivation of complete dynamic field equations, higher-order contact interactions, and natural boundary conditions for strain-gradient rods; identification of gauge freedom in reactive double-force fields for constrained rods; formulation of objective constitutive laws and invariant representations of stored energies under extended symmetries; rigorous proof that eigenvalues of the linearized cantilever problem are simple and exactly coincide with bifurcation loads; existence of globally connected, locally analytic nonlinear equilibrium branches emanating from each bifurcation point.

Conclusion: The strain-gradient special Cosserat rod theory provides a geometrically exact, thermodynamically consistent, and constitutively flexible foundation for modeling advanced mechanical metamaterials; its variational structure ensures physical admissibility (objectivity, momentum balance), while its application to buckling demonstrates predictive power and mathematical rigor in both linear stability and global nonlinear analysis.

Abstract: Motivated by recent advances in the synthesis of effective one-dimensional mechanical metamaterials, we develop a direct theory of strain-gradient special Cosserat rods based on the principle of virtual work. The internal virtual work is formulated intrinsically in terms of variations of the classical Cosserat strains and their spatial derivatives, without requiring a three-dimensional parent material or a stored energy. Objectivity and insensitivity to rigid virtual displacements characterize the admissible internal-work density, while localization yields the dynamic field equations, higher-order contact interactions, natural boundary conditions, and balances of linear and angular momentum. For inextensible and unshearable rods, the constrained principle determines a unique multiplier functional, although its representation by reactive force and double-force fields possesses a gauge freedom absent from classical rod theory. We also formulate objective elastic, hyperelastic, and viscoelastic constitutive laws, extend transverse hemitropy, transverse isotropy, and flip symmetry to strain-gradient rods, and derive invariant representations of symmetric stored energies. As an application, we study a planar inextensible cantilever under a compressive terminal force. We prove that the eigenvalues of the linearized problem are simple and coincide precisely with the loads at which bifurcation from the straight state occurs. In addition, each bifurcation point generates a connected global branch of nonlinear equilibria that is locally analytic near its origin.

### [6] [On the Mathematical Structure of Helical Shear in Anelastic Continua](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19221)
*Souhayl Sadik,Giuseppe Saccomandi,Luigi Vergori*

Main category: math-ph

TL;DR: This paper analyzes helical shear in a thick-walled, dislocated, hyperelastic tube using the Bilby–Kröner–Lee decomposition and Weitzenböck geometry; it finds that dislocations induce chiral coupling between azimuthal and axial shear only when strain energy depends on the second invariant, stiffen the response for first-invariant–stiffening materials, impose strength caps in Gent-type models, and introduce mode coupling at second order in a weakly nonlinear expansion.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To understand how distributed axial screw dislocations affect the mechanical response—particularly coupling and stiffening—of thick-walled hyperelastic tubes under helical shear, extending classical universal relations to dislocated configurations.

Method: Uses the Bilby–Kröner–Lee decomposition to embed dislocations into a Weitzenböck manifold; derives equilibrium equations from a radially symmetric ansatz; solves analytically for neo-Hookean, Fung–Demiray, Gent, Knowles, and Mooney–Rivlin models and numerically for Gent–Gent; performs third-order weakly nonlinear expansion in tractions and dislocation strength.

Result: Dislocations induce chiral coupling between azimuthal and axial shear strains proportional to dislocation strength and ∂W/∂I₂; stiffen response for ∂²W/∂I₁² > 0; cap dislocation strength in Gent/Gent–Gent models; shrink admissible traction domain in subcritical Knowles (n < 1/2); linear order: no effect on shear response but residual stress; second order: chiral coupling emerges universally via I₂-dependence; third order: stiffness corrections via fourth-order modulus, mode-specific.

Conclusion: Dislocations fundamentally alter the constitutive structure of hyperelastic tubes—not merely as defects but as geometric sources that couple shear modes chirally, constrain admissibility, and modify higher-order elasticity, with implications for designing metamaterials and modeling biological or geological twisted structures.

Abstract: We study helical shear of a thick-walled tube made of an incompressible, isotropic hyper-anelastic solid carrying a radially symmetric distribution of axial screw dislocations. Through the Bilby--Kröner--Lee decomposition, the dislocations are encoded in a Weitzenböck material manifold, whose Riemannian metric alone enters the elastic response. Equilibrium reduces at each radius to an algebraic system for the azimuthal and axial shear strains, whose cross terms are proportional to the dislocation strength and to the dependence of the strain energy on the second invariant; the two shear modes therefore couple only in the presence of dislocations. Exact solutions are obtained for neo-Hookean, Fung--Demiray, Gent, Knowles, and Mooney--Rivlin materials, and numerical ones for Gent--Gent materials. The universal relations of Horgan and Saccomandi are extended to dislocated generalized neo-Hookean tubes. Dislocations stiffen the tube when the strain energy stiffens with the first invariant, couple the two shear modes chirally when it depends on the second, and may restrict admissibility: they cap the dislocation strength in Gent and Gent--Gent materials, and contract the admissible traction region in Knowles materials with power-law index below n = 1/2. A weakly nonlinear analysis to third order in the applied tractions and dislocation strength shows that, at first order (linear elasticity), the dislocations leave the shear response unaffected while carrying a residual stress. The chiral coupling enters at second order, through the dependence of the strain energy on the second invariant, so that every dislocated material couples the modes as the Mooney--Rivlin solid with the same second- and third-order moduli. At third order, no new coupling arises; the modes interact through stiffness corrections involving the fourth-order modulus, which the dislocations further correct mode by mode.

### [7] [A Nontrivial Lattice Scalar Quantum Field Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19310)
*P.A. Faria da Veiga,R. Mistry,M. O'Carroll*

Main category: math-ph

TL;DR: Introduces a stable, renormalized hybrid lattice QFT model with scaled scalar fields in d=3,4; proves thermodynamic/UV stability, OS-positivity, bounded correlations, and nonzero 4-point function via polymer expansion and perturbation theory.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To construct a well-defined, stable Euclidean quantum field theory on a hypercubic lattice that simultaneously satisfies thermodynamic and ultraviolet stability, supports Osterwalder-Schrader positivity, and admits a continuum limit — addressing foundational issues in lattice QFT for scalar fields in d ≥ 3.

Method: Uses noncanonical (wave-function) lattice scaling of zero-spin scalar fields; applies polymer expansion for correlation bounds; establishes thermodynamic and UV stability via uniform bounds on the partition function; verifies Osterwalder-Schrader positivity for small couplings; constructs the physical Hilbert space; computes truncated four-point function perturbatively.

Result: Proves uniform (in volume Λₛ and lattice spacing a ∈ (0,1]) thermodynamic and UV stability; existence of finite free energy per degree of freedom in Λₛ → ∞ (subsequential) and a → 0 limits; OS-positivity and Hilbert space construction for small κ², α; bounded correlations in thermodynamic limit; nonzero infinite-volume truncated four-point function; convergence of perturbation series in κ² and α for fixed a.

Conclusion: The hybrid model is a mathematically rigorous, stable, and physically meaningful lattice QFT in spacetime dimensions d = 3, 4 (extendable to higher d), satisfying essential axioms including stability, reflection positivity, and nontrivial interaction (nonzero 4-point function), thereby providing a viable framework for constructive QFT.

Abstract: We introduce a new Euclidean quantum model with zero-spin scaled scalar fields on a spacetime hypercubic lattice, which we refer to as the hybrid model. The model coupling parameters are the hopping parameter $\kappa^2>0$ and the field interaction coupling $\alpha>0$. Using the technique of lattice scaled fields, with a noncanonical scaling that corresponds to a partial renormalization (wave function renormalization), we show that the partition function of this model satisfies both thermodynamic and ultraviolet stability bounds, with constants which are uniform in the lattice volume $\Lambda_s$ and the lattice spacing $a\in(0,1]$. As a consequence, the model has a finite free energy per degree of freedom in the thermodynamic limit $\Lambda_s\nearrow\infty$, at least in the subsequential sense. Subsequently, we can also take the ultraviolet or continuum limit $a\searrow 0$. Besides the finiteness property of the free energy, for sufficiently small values of the model coupling parameters $\kappa^2$ and $\alpha$, we show the model satisfies Osterwalder-Schrader positivity and construct the underlying physical quantum mechanical Hilbert space, and, using a polymer expansion, we show correlations also remain bounded in the thermodynamic limit. Finally, we perturbatively compute an infinite volume truncated four-point correlation showing it is nonzero. Noting that, for fixed $a$, perturbation series in $\kappa^2$ and $\alpha$ converges, these results are sufficient to show that our hybrid model makes sense as a lattice quantum field theory in spacetime dimension $d=3,4$, with possible extensions to other higher values of $d$.

</details>
