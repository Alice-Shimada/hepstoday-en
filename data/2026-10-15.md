### [1] [Thermal Giant Graviton Expansion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18987)
*Kasia Budzik,Atul Sharma*

Main category: hep-th

TL;DR: This paper constructs giant graviton expansions for thermal and graded partition functions of N=4 and N=1 SYM at zero 't Hooft coupling using twistor string holography and a quiver-generalized Murthy expansion; it identifies nonperturbative corrections with D-instanton/anti-D-instanton pairs and microscopically explains Hagedorn singularities via resummation at infinite N.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To provide a bulk microscopic explanation for the Hagedorn singularities in free (zero-coupling) super Yang-Mills theories, whose thermal and (-1)^F-graded partition functions are generically non-BPS and lack direct supergravity duals.

Method: Uses chiral self-dual limits of N=4/N=1 SYM admitting twistor string holography; applies a quiver generalization of Murthy's giant graviton expansion; matches terms to bulk closed string and D-brane sectors—including D-instanton/anti-D-instanton pairs—as nonperturbative corrections.

Result: Obtains finite-N and infinite-N giant graviton expansions; identifies D-instanton pairs as sources of nonperturbative corrections; resummation of D-instanton sectors at infinite N reproduces the Hagedorn temperatures and gives a bulk origin for Hagedorn singularities.

Conclusion: The Hagedorn behavior in free gauge theories arises from nonperturbative D-instanton physics in their twistor string holographic duals, demonstrating that even non-BPS observables at zero coupling admit controlled bulk interpretations via giant gravitons and instanton resummation.

Abstract: We construct giant graviton expansions of the thermal and $(-1)^F$-graded partition functions of $\mathcal{N}=4$ and $\mathcal{N}=1$ super Yang-Mills theories at zero 't Hooft coupling. These observables are generically non-BPS and do not admit a direct bulk interpretation in a supergravity regime. Instead, we exploit the corresponding self-dual theories, which arise as chiral zero-coupling limits and admit twistor string holographic duals. We apply a quiver generalization of Murthy's expansion motivated by twistor strings. Notably, we obtain expansions at both finite and infinite $N$. We reproduce the terms in these expansions from bulk closed string and D-brane sectors, identifying the nonperturbative corrections with D-instanton/anti-D-instanton pairs. At infinite $N$, resumming the D-instanton sectors reproduces the Hagedorn temperatures of the free gauge theories, providing a microscopic bulk origin for their Hagedorn singularities.

### [2] [Two-Species Boltzmann--Kac Kinetics with Matsumoto--Yor Nonlinear Collisions: Conserved Energy and Conic Equilibria](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18892)
*Yoshiaki Itoh*

Main category: math-ph

TL;DR: Extends Kac's kinetic model to a two-species system on (0,∞)² using the Matsumoto–Yor involution; identifies conserved energy and invariant measure; proves an H-theorem linking zero entropy production to statistical independence of post-collision velocities; derives GIG and Gamma distributions as non-equilibrium thermodynamic equilibria via state-space analysis.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To generalize Mark Kac's kinetic framework beyond single-species Euclidean settings to multi-species, non-linear interaction spaces with physically meaningful invariants and thermodynamic structure.

Method: Uses the Matsumoto–Yor rational collision involution on (0,∞)²; constructs a non-linear conserved energy E(x,y) = x + y + 1/x and invariant conic metric measure dμ = dx dy / [x(x+y)]; establishes a two-species H-theorem via logarithmic gradient transformation law and separation of variables; avoids transform-domain methods in favor of direct state-space derivation.

Result: Entropy production vanishes iff post-collision velocities are statistically independent (I(U;V)=0); the functional-differential system uniquely characterizes Generalized Inverse Gaussian (GIG) and Gamma distributions as stationary solutions — i.e., non-equilibrium thermodynamic equilibria.

Conclusion: The Matsumoto–Yor structure supports a rigorous kinetic-theoretic interpretation with conservation laws, invariant measures, and entropy dissipation properties, positioning GIG and Gamma distributions as natural equilibria in this two-species non-linear collisional setting.

Abstract: We extend Mark Kac's kinetic framework from single-species Euclidean spaces to a two-species, non-linear interaction space on the open cone $(0, \infty)^2$ governed by the Matsumoto--Yor rational collision involution. We identify a non-linear conserved energy $E(x,y) = x + y + 1/x$ and an invariant conic metric measure $d\mu = \frac{dx \, dy}{x(x+y)}$. By establishing a two-species $H$-theorem, we prove that entropy production vanishes if and only if post-collision velocities are statistically independent ($I(U;V)=0$). In contrast to the transform-domain proof of Letac and Wesołowski~\cite{letac2000}, which relies on joint Laplace transforms of general measures, our approach yields a direct physical derivation in state space via the transformation law for logarithmic gradients and separation of variables. This uncouples the functional-differential system to uniquely characterize the Generalized Inverse Gaussian (GIG) and Gamma distributions as the non-equilibrium thermodynamic equilibria.

### [3] [Anomalies, higher symmetries, and homotopy theory I: crossed precosheaves and a nonabelian Cech construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19004)
*Anton Kapustin,Shixiong Xu*

Main category: math-ph

TL;DR: This paper introduces a homotopy-theoretic framework for 't Hooft anomalies using crossed precosheaves and Čech–Loday classifying spaces, linking localized symmetries to higher-group structures and recovering known symmetry indices in 1D/2D quantum spin systems.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To formalize how quantum symmetries—especially higher and anomalous ones—are encoded not by ordinary groups but by homotopy types, and to provide a rigorous operator-algebraic, localization-based definition of 't Hooft anomalies that generalizes beyond global group actions.

Method: Constructs a crossed precosheaf encoding spatially localized symmetry transformations and their commutator constraints; defines the Čech–Loday classifying space as a pointed connected homotopy type associated to finite region covers; forms a pro-object in the homotopy category as covers refine; uses the canonical map from the ordinary classifying space to detect anomalies via obstruction to gauging; identifies the homotopy fiber as a candidate higher-symmetry classifying space when cover-independence holds.

Result: A general homotopy-theoretic definition of 't Hooft anomalies grounded in localized operator algebras; recovery of the Else–Nayak index (1D) and its 2D generalizations for quantum spin chains/lattices; explicit constructions for gauge transformations (continuum) and quantum cellular automata/finite-depth circuits (lattice).

Conclusion: Higher symmetries and their 't Hooft anomalies are intrinsically homotopical, captured by the Čech–Loday pro-classifying space of a crossed precosheaf; this framework unifies diverse physical realizations and provides a rigorous, localization-respecting foundation for symmetry classification beyond group theory.

Abstract: Symmetries of a quantum field theory or a quantum lattice model are expected to be described not by a group but by a homotopy type, whose homotopy groups are the higher symmetry groups and whose Postnikov invariants encode 't Hooft anomalies. We propose a homotopy-theoretic definition of 't Hooft anomalies based on the structure of localized symmetries in the operator-algebraic approach. The input is a crossed precosheaf: a collection of groups attached to regions of space, equipped with pairings recording that the commutator of two transformations localized in two regions is localized in their intersection. To a crossed precosheaf and a finite family of regions we attach a pointed connected homotopy type, the Cech-Loday classifying space. As the family of regions ranges over finite covers of space, the classifying spaces form a pro-object in the homotopy category. There is a canonical map from the ordinary classifying space of the symmetry group to this pro-type. Composing this map with a group action gives an anomaly whose nontriviality obstructs gauging. In favorable cases, the Cech-Loday space is essentially independent of the cover. In such cases, the homotopy fiber of the canonical map is a candidate for the classifying space of the higher group of symmetries. We give examples of crossed precosheaves arising from gauge transformations in continuum field theory and from quantum cellular automata and finite-depth circuits. For quantum spin systems in one and two dimensions, our construction recovers the Else-Nayak index and its generalizations to two dimensions.

### [6] [On the Mathematical Structure of Helical Shear in Anelastic Continua](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19221)
*Souhayl Sadik,Giuseppe Saccomandi,Luigi Vergori*

Main category: math-ph

TL;DR: This paper analyzes helical shear in a thick-walled, dislocated, hyperelastic tube using geometric elasticity (Weitzenböck manifold) and finds that dislocations induce chiral coupling between azimuthal and axial shear modes—only when the strain energy depends on the second invariant—and affect stiffness, admissibility, and higher-order nonlinear response.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To understand how axial screw dislocations influence the nonlinear elastic response—particularly chiral coupling and stiffening—of thick-walled isotropic hyperelastic tubes under helical shear, beyond classical linear elasticity.

Method: Uses Bilby–Kröner–Lee decomposition to embed dislocations into a Weitzenböck material manifold; derives equilibrium equations from variational principles for incompressible isotropic hyperelasticity; solves analytically for neo-Hookean, Fung–Demiray, Gent, Knowles, Mooney–Rivlin models and numerically for Gent–Gent; performs weakly nonlinear expansion up to third order in tractions and dislocation strength.

Result: Dislocations: (i) leave linear (first-order) shear response unchanged but induce residual stress; (ii) introduce chiral coupling at second order via dependence on the second strain invariant; (iii) stiffen the tube if strain energy increases with first invariant; (iv) impose upper bounds on dislocation strength in Gent/Gent–Gent materials and shrink admissible traction regions in subcritical Knowles materials (n < 1/2); (v) modify third-order stiffness corrections mode-wise without introducing new couplings.

Conclusion: Geometrically encoded dislocations fundamentally alter the nonlinear shear mechanics of hyperelastic tubes—not through linear effects, but via invariant-dependent chiral coupling and higher-order stiffness modulation—revealing universal features across material models and constraints on physical realizability.

Abstract: We study helical shear of a thick-walled tube made of an incompressible, isotropic hyper-anelastic solid carrying a radially symmetric distribution of axial screw dislocations. Through the Bilby--Kröner--Lee decomposition, the dislocations are encoded in a Weitzenböck material manifold, whose Riemannian metric alone enters the elastic response. Equilibrium reduces at each radius to an algebraic system for the azimuthal and axial shear strains, whose cross terms are proportional to the dislocation strength and to the dependence of the strain energy on the second invariant; the two shear modes therefore couple only in the presence of dislocations. Exact solutions are obtained for neo-Hookean, Fung--Demiray, Gent, Knowles, and Mooney--Rivlin materials, and numerical ones for Gent--Gent materials. The universal relations of Horgan and Saccomandi are extended to dislocated generalized neo-Hookean tubes. Dislocations stiffen the tube when the strain energy stiffens with the first invariant, couple the two shear modes chirally when it depends on the second, and may restrict admissibility: they cap the dislocation strength in Gent and Gent--Gent materials, and contract the admissible traction region in Knowles materials with power-law index below n = 1/2. A weakly nonlinear analysis to third order in the applied tractions and dislocation strength shows that, at first order (linear elasticity), the dislocations leave the shear response unaffected while carrying a residual stress. The chiral coupling enters at second order, through the dependence of the strain energy on the second invariant, so that every dislocated material couples the modes as the Mooney--Rivlin solid with the same second- and third-order moduli. At third order, no new coupling arises; the modes interact through stiffness corrections involving the fourth-order modulus, which the dislocations further correct mode by mode.

### [7] [A Nontrivial Lattice Scalar Quantum Field Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19310)
*P.A. Faria da Veiga,R. Mistry,M. O'Carroll*

Main category: math-ph

TL;DR: Introduces a stable, renormalized hybrid lattice QFT model with scaled scalar fields in d=3,4; proves thermodynamic/UV stability, OS-positivity, bounded correlations, and nonzero 4-point function via polymer expansion and perturbation theory.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To construct a well-defined, ultraviolet- and thermodynamically stable Euclidean lattice quantum field theory for zero-spin scalar fields in physical spacetime dimensions (d=3,4), avoiding common instabilities and ensuring existence of a physical Hilbert space and continuum limit.

Method: Uses noncanonical (wave-function) lattice field scaling; applies thermodynamic/UV stability analysis; verifies Osterwalder-Schrader positivity; constructs Hilbert space; employs polymer expansion for correlation bounds; performs convergent perturbative computation of the infinite-volume truncated four-point function.

Result: Proves uniform (in volume Λₛ and lattice spacing a ∈ (0,1]) thermodynamic and UV stability; establishes finite free energy per degree of freedom in thermodynamic (Λₛ→∞) and continuum (a→0) limits; confirms OS-positivity and Hilbert space construction for small κ², α; shows bounded correlations in thermodynamic limit; computes nonzero truncated four-point function.

Conclusion: The hybrid model is a mathematically rigorous, stable, and physically meaningful lattice QFT in d=3,4 — it admits a well-defined continuum limit, satisfies axiomatic QFT requirements (OS-positivity, Hilbert space, local correlations), and exhibits genuine interaction (nonzero 4-point function), validating its status as a legitimate interacting scalar QFT.

Abstract: We introduce a new Euclidean quantum model with zero-spin scaled scalar fields on a spacetime hypercubic lattice, which we refer to as the hybrid model. The model coupling parameters are the hopping parameter $\kappa^2>0$ and the field interaction coupling $\alpha>0$. Using the technique of lattice scaled fields, with a noncanonical scaling that corresponds to a partial renormalization (wave function renormalization), we show that the partition function of this model satisfies both thermodynamic and ultraviolet stability bounds, with constants which are uniform in the lattice volume $\Lambda_s$ and the lattice spacing $a\in(0,1]$. As a consequence, the model has a finite free energy per degree of freedom in the thermodynamic limit $\Lambda_s\nearrow\infty$, at least in the subsequential sense. Subsequently, we can also take the ultraviolet or continuum limit $a\searrow 0$. Besides the finiteness property of the free energy, for sufficiently small values of the model coupling parameters $\kappa^2$ and $\alpha$, we show the model satisfies Osterwalder-Schrader positivity and construct the underlying physical quantum mechanical Hilbert space, and, using a polymer expansion, we show correlations also remain bounded in the thermodynamic limit. Finally, we perturbatively compute an infinite volume truncated four-point correlation showing it is nonzero. Noting that, for fixed $a$, perturbation series in $\kappa^2$ and $\alpha$ converges, these results are sufficient to show that our hybrid model makes sense as a lattice quantum field theory in spacetime dimension $d=3,4$, with possible extensions to other higher values of $d$.

</details>
