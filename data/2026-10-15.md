### [1] [Thermal Giant Graviton Expansion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18987)
*Kasia Budzik,Atul Sharma*

Main category: hep-th

TL;DR: This paper constructs giant graviton expansions for thermal and graded partition functions of N=4 and N=1 SYM at zero 't Hooft coupling, using twistor string duals of self-dual limits; it derives finite- and infinite-N expansions, identifies nonperturbative contributions as D/anti-D-instanton pairs, and microscopically explains Hagedorn singularities via instanton resummation at infinite N.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To provide a bulk (holographic) interpretation for non-BPS thermal observables in free (zero-coupling) super Yang-Mills theories—observables that lack a conventional supergravity dual—by leveraging chiral self-dual limits with known twistor string holography.

Method: Applies a quiver-generalized Murthy expansion motivated by twistor string theory to compute thermal and (-1)^F-graded partition functions; matches terms to bulk closed string and D-brane sectors—including D-instanton/anti-D-instanton pairs—and performs resummation of instanton sectors at infinite N.

Result: Obtains exact giant graviton expansions for partition functions at both finite and infinite N; identifies nonperturbative corrections with D/anti-D-instanton pairs; shows that resumming D-instantons at infinite N reproduces the Hagedorn temperature and gives a microscopic bulk origin for the Hagedorn singularity.

Conclusion: The Hagedorn behavior of free gauge theories has a precise bulk origin in nonperturbative D-brane physics—specifically, instanton–anti-instanton pairs—in a twistor-string-motivated holographic framework, bridging chiral limits, giant gravitons, and thermal phase structure.

Abstract: We construct giant graviton expansions of the thermal and $(-1)^F$-graded partition functions of $\mathcal{N}=4$ and $\mathcal{N}=1$ super Yang-Mills theories at zero 't Hooft coupling. These observables are generically non-BPS and do not admit a direct bulk interpretation in a supergravity regime. Instead, we exploit the corresponding self-dual theories, which arise as chiral zero-coupling limits and admit twistor string holographic duals. We apply a quiver generalization of Murthy's expansion motivated by twistor strings. Notably, we obtain expansions at both finite and infinite $N$. We reproduce the terms in these expansions from bulk closed string and D-brane sectors, identifying the nonperturbative corrections with D-instanton/anti-D-instanton pairs. At infinite $N$, resumming the D-instanton sectors reproduces the Hagedorn temperatures of the free gauge theories, providing a microscopic bulk origin for their Hagedorn singularities.

### [2] [Two-Species Boltzmann--Kac Kinetics with Matsumoto--Yor Nonlinear Collisions: Conserved Energy and Conic Equilibria](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18892)
*Yoshiaki Itoh*

Main category: math-ph

TL;DR: Extends Kac's kinetic model to a two-species system on (0,∞)² using the Matsumoto–Yor involution; identifies conserved energy and invariant measure; proves an H-theorem linking zero entropy production to statistical independence of post-collision velocities; derives GIG and Gamma as non-equilibrium thermodynamic equilibria via state-space analysis.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To generalize Mark Kac's kinetic framework beyond single-species Euclidean settings to multi-species, non-linear, geometrically structured interaction spaces—specifically, to uncover physically grounded characterizations of equilibrium distributions (GIG, Gamma) in non-standard collision dynamics.

Method: Uses the Matsumoto–Yor rational involution as the collision rule on (0,∞)²; constructs a non-linear conserved energy E(x,y) = x + y + 1/x and invariant conic metric measure dμ = dx dy / [x(x+y)]; establishes a two-species H-theorem by analyzing entropy production via logarithmic gradient transformation laws and separation of variables; avoids transform-domain methods (e.g., Laplace transforms) in favor of direct state-space derivation.

Result: Entropy production vanishes iff post-collision velocities U,V are statistically independent (I(U;V)=0); the only stationary solutions satisfying the H-theorem and conservation/invariance constraints are the Generalized Inverse Gaussian (GIG) and Gamma distributions; these emerge as non-equilibrium thermodynamic equilibria.

Conclusion: The Matsumoto–Yor structure admits a rigorous kinetic-theoretic interpretation: its invariant measure and conserved energy induce a well-defined H-theorem, uniquely selecting GIG and Gamma as physically consistent steady states—thereby unifying probabilistic characterization with non-equilibrium statistical mechanics.

Abstract: We extend Mark Kac's kinetic framework from single-species Euclidean spaces to a two-species, non-linear interaction space on the open cone $(0, \infty)^2$ governed by the Matsumoto--Yor rational collision involution. We identify a non-linear conserved energy $E(x,y) = x + y + 1/x$ and an invariant conic metric measure $dμ= \frac{dx \, dy}{x(x+y)}$. By establishing a two-species $H$-theorem, we prove that entropy production vanishes if and only if post-collision velocities are statistically independent ($I(U;V)=0$). In contrast to the transform-domain proof of Letac and Wesołowski~\cite{letac2000}, which relies on joint Laplace transforms of general measures, our approach yields a direct physical derivation in state space via the transformation law for logarithmic gradients and separation of variables. This uncouples the functional-differential system to uniquely characterize the Generalized Inverse Gaussian (GIG) and Gamma distributions as the non-equilibrium thermodynamic equilibria.

### [3] [Anomalies, higher symmetries, and homotopy theory I: crossed precosheaves and a nonabelian Cech construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19004)
*Anton Kapustin,Shixiong Xu*

Main category: math-ph

TL;DR: This paper introduces a homotopy-theoretic framework for 't Hooft anomalies using crossed precosheaves and Čech–Loday classifying spaces, linking localized symmetries to higher-group structures and recovering known symmetry indices in 1D/2D quantum spin systems.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To formalize how quantum symmetries—especially higher and anomalous ones—are encoded not by ordinary groups but by homotopy types, and to provide a rigorous operator-algebraic, localization-based definition of 't Hooft anomalies that generalizes beyond global group actions.

Method: Constructs a crossed precosheaf encoding spatially localized symmetry transformations and their commutator constraints; defines the Čech–Loday classifying space as a pointed connected homotopy type associated to finite region covers; forms a pro-object in the homotopy category as covers refine; uses the canonical map from the ordinary classifying space to detect anomalies via obstruction to gauging; identifies the homotopy fiber as a candidate higher-symmetry classifying space when cover-independence holds.

Result: A general homotopy-theoretic definition of 't Hooft anomalies grounded in localized operator algebras; recovery of the Else–Nayak index (1D) and its 2D generalizations for quantum spin chains/lattices; explicit constructions for gauge transformations (continuum) and quantum cellular automata/finite-depth circuits (lattice).

Conclusion: Higher symmetries and their 't Hooft anomalies are intrinsically homotopical, captured by the Čech–Loday pro-classifying space of a crossed precosheaf; this framework unifies diverse physical realizations and provides a rigorous, localization-respecting foundation for symmetry classification beyond group theory.

Abstract: Symmetries of a quantum field theory or a quantum lattice model are expected to be described not by a group but by a homotopy type, whose homotopy groups are the higher symmetry groups and whose Postnikov invariants encode 't Hooft anomalies. We propose a homotopy-theoretic definition of 't Hooft anomalies based on the structure of localized symmetries in the operator-algebraic approach. The input is a crossed precosheaf: a collection of groups attached to regions of space, equipped with pairings recording that the commutator of two transformations localized in two regions is localized in their intersection. To a crossed precosheaf and a finite family of regions we attach a pointed connected homotopy type, the Cech-Loday classifying space. As the family of regions ranges over finite covers of space, the classifying spaces form a pro-object in the homotopy category. There is a canonical map from the ordinary classifying space of the symmetry group to this pro-type. Composing this map with a group action gives an anomaly whose nontriviality obstructs gauging. In favorable cases, the Cech-Loday space is essentially independent of the cover. In such cases, the homotopy fiber of the canonical map is a candidate for the classifying space of the higher group of symmetries. We give examples of crossed precosheaves arising from gauge transformations in continuum field theory and from quantum cellular automata and finite-depth circuits. For quantum spin systems in one and two dimensions, our construction recovers the Else-Nayak index and its generalizations to two dimensions.

We also formulate objective elastic, hyperelastic, and viscoelastic constitutive laws, extend transverse hemitropy, transverse isotropy, and flip symmetry to strain-gradient rods, and derive invariant representations of symmetric stored energies. In addition, each bifurcation point generates a connected global branch of nonlinear equilibria that is locally analytic near its origin.

### [6] [On the Mathematical Structure of Helical Shear in Anelastic Continua](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19221)
*Souhayl Sadik,Giuseppe Saccomandi,Luigi Vergori*

Main category: math-ph

TL;DR: This paper analyzes helical shear in a thick-walled, dislocated, hyperelastic tube using differential geometry (Weitzenböck manifold) and nonlinear elasticity; it finds that axial screw dislocations induce chiral coupling between azimuthal and axial shear strains—only when the strain energy depends on the second invariant—and affect stiffness, admissibility, and higher-order nonlinear responses.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To understand how distributed axial screw dislocations influence the finite deformation response—particularly chiral coupling and stiffening—of thick-walled hyperelastic tubes under helical shear, beyond classical linear elasticity.

Method: Uses Bilby–Kröner–Lee decomposition to embed dislocations into a Weitzenböck material manifold; derives equilibrium equations from a radially symmetric ansatz; solves analytically for neo-Hookean, Fung–Demiray, Gent, Knowles, and Mooney–Rivlin models, numerically for Gent–Gent; extends universal relations; performs weakly nonlinear expansion up to third order in tractions and dislocation strength.

Result: Dislocations induce chiral coupling between azimuthal and axial shear only if strain energy depends on the second invariant; they stiffen the tube when energy stiffens with first invariant; impose upper bounds on dislocation strength (Gent, Gent–Gent) or shrink admissible traction regions (Knowles, n < 1/2); at linear order, dislocations produce residual stress but no shear response change; chiral coupling emerges at second order; third-order corrections involve fourth-order moduli and mode-specific dislocation effects.

Conclusion: Axial screw dislocations fundamentally alter the nonlinear shear mechanics of hyperelastic tubes—not merely as defects but as geometric sources of chirality and constraint—revealing universal coupling mechanisms tied to strain-invariant dependence and higher-order elastic moduli.

Abstract: We study helical shear of a thick-walled tube made of an incompressible, isotropic hyper-anelastic solid carrying a radially symmetric distribution of axial screw dislocations. Through the Bilby--Kröner--Lee decomposition, the dislocations are encoded in a Weitzenböck material manifold, whose Riemannian metric alone enters the elastic response. Equilibrium reduces at each radius to an algebraic system for the azimuthal and axial shear strains, whose cross terms are proportional to the dislocation strength and to the dependence of the strain energy on the second invariant; the two shear modes therefore couple only in the presence of dislocations. Exact solutions are obtained for neo-Hookean, Fung--Demiray, Gent, Knowles, and Mooney--Rivlin materials, and numerical ones for Gent--Gent materials. The universal relations of Horgan and Saccomandi are extended to dislocated generalized neo-Hookean tubes. Dislocations stiffen the tube when the strain energy stiffens with the first invariant, couple the two shear modes chirally when it depends on the second, and may restrict admissibility: they cap the dislocation strength in Gent and Gent--Gent materials, and contract the admissible traction region in Knowles materials with power-law index below n = 1/2. A weakly nonlinear analysis to third order in the applied tractions and dislocation strength shows that, at first order (linear elasticity), the dislocations leave the shear response unaffected while carrying a residual stress. The chiral coupling enters at second order, through the dependence of the strain energy on the second invariant, so that every dislocated material couples the modes as the Mooney--Rivlin solid with the same second- and third-order moduli. At third order, no new coupling arises; the modes interact through stiffness corrections involving the fourth-order modulus, which the dislocations further correct mode by mode.

### [7] [A Nontrivial Lattice Scalar Quantum Field Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19310)
*P. A. Faria da Veiga,R. Mistry,M. O'Carroll*

Main category: math-ph

TL;DR: Introduces a lattice-based Euclidean quantum field theory (hybrid model) with scaled scalar fields, proving thermodynamic and UV stability, finite free energy in both thermodynamic and continuum limits, OS-positivity, Hilbert space construction, bounded correlations, and nonzero four-point function—valid for d=3,4.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To construct a well-defined, stable, and physically meaningful lattice quantum field theory that simultaneously satisfies thermodynamic stability, ultraviolet stability, Osterwalder-Schrader positivity, and admits a continuum limit—addressing foundational challenges in rigorous QFT on hypercubic lattices.

Method: Employs noncanonical (wave-function) renormalized lattice scaling of zero-spin scalar fields; uses polymer expansion for correlation bounds; establishes stability via uniform bounds on the partition function; constructs Hilbert space from OS-positivity; computes truncated four-point function perturbatively in κ² and α.

Result: Proves uniform (in volume Λₛ and lattice spacing a ∈ (0,1]) thermodynamic and UV stability; existence of finite subsequential free energy density as Λₛ→∞; existence of continuum limit a→0; OS-positivity and Hilbert space construction for small κ², α; bounded correlations in thermodynamic limit; nonzero infinite-volume truncated four-point function.

Conclusion: The hybrid model is a mathematically rigorous, stable, and physically interpretable lattice QFT in d = 3,4 (extendable to higher d), satisfying key axiomatic requirements—including stability, positivity, continuum limit, and nontrivial interaction—as a nonperturbative foundation for scalar field theory.

Abstract: We introduce a new Euclidean quantum model with zero-spin scaled scalar fields on a spacetime hypercubic lattice, which we refer to as the hybrid model. The model coupling parameters are the hopping parameter $κ^2>0$ and the field interaction coupling $α>0$. Using the technique of lattice scaled fields, with a noncanonical scaling that corresponds to a partial renormalization (wave function renormalization), we show that the partition function of this model satisfies both thermodynamic and ultraviolet stability bounds, with constants which are uniform in the lattice volume $Λ_s$ and the lattice spacing $a\in(0,1]$. As a consequence, the model has a finite free energy per degree of freedom in the thermodynamic limit $Λ_s\nearrow\infty$, at least in the subsequential sense. Subsequently, we can also take the ultraviolet or continuum limit $a\searrow 0$. Besides the finiteness property of the free energy, for sufficiently small values of the model coupling parameters $κ^2$ and $α$, we show the model satisfies Osterwalder-Schrader positivity and construct the underlying physical quantum mechanical Hilbert space, and, using a polymer expansion, we show correlations also remain bounded in the thermodynamic limit. Finally, we perturbatively compute an infinite volume truncated four-point correlation showing it is nonzero. Noting that, for fixed $a$, perturbation series in $κ^2$ and $α$ converges, these results are sufficient to show that our hybrid model makes sense as a lattice quantum field theory in spacetime dimension $d=3,4$, with possible extensions to other higher values of $d$.
