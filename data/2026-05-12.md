### [1] [A study of multicavity concept applied to hexagonal coaxial haloscopes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08932)
*J. M. García-Barceló,Jose R. Navarro-Madrid,Alejandro Díaz-Morcillo*

Main category: hep-ex

TL;DR: This paper proposes a scalable multicavity axion haloscope design using hexagonal coaxial geometry at 30 GHz, achieving up to ×3 sensitivity gain with triple-subcavity configuration via prism-rotation tuning; quad-subcavity feasibility is theoretically confirmed under 25 mm radial constraint with optimized wall thicknesses, while addressing mode splitting, tolerances, and thermal challenges.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To increase scanning rate and sensitivity of axion haloscopes within constrained magnet bore volumes, especially in high-frequency (30 GHz) regimes where single-cavity designs are limited in effective volume and scan speed.

Method: Design and electromagnetic simulation of coaxial multicavity architectures (dual-, triple-, and quad-subcavity) based on hexagonal symmetry; implementation of a novel resonant frequency tuning mechanism via rotation of inner hexagonal prisms; theoretical analysis of frequency stability, mode splitting, and geometric constraints (e.g., 25 mm radial limit); optimization of wall thicknesses for quality factor and form factor preservation.

Result: Triple-subcavity design achieves ×3 improvement in sensitivity over single-cavity baseline; quad-subcavity system is theoretically feasible under 25 mm radial constraint with optimized wall thicknesses; rotation-based tuning maintains high Q-factor and form factor; practical limits identified in mode degeneracy, manufacturing tolerance sensitivity, and thermal management.

Conclusion: The one-port multicavity architecture with rotational tuning provides a scalable, high-Q pathway to enhance sensitive volume and scanning efficiency of high-frequency axion haloscopes—enabling more effective exploration of the axion dark matter parameter space.

Abstract: In this work, a study on the development of scalable multicavity architectures for axion haloscopes, based on a hexagonal coaxial geometry operating at $30$ GHz frequencies, is presented. To enhance the scanning rate and sensitivity within the limited volume of experimental magnet bores, the transition from a baseline single-cavity design to dual and triple-subcavity configurations is investigated. A novel tuning mechanism based on the rotation of one or two inner hexagonal prisms is implemented, providing a robust method to shift the resonant frequency while maintaining high form and quality factors. The results show that the triple-subcavity design achieves an improvement of $\times3$ over the single-cavity baseline. The scaling potential of quad-subcavity architectures under a strict radial constraint of $25$ mm is further explored. Theoretical analysis confirms that a four-subcavity system is feasible within a certain radial clearance, provided that wall thicknesses are strategically optimised to ensure frequency stability. The practical challenges associated with mode splitting, manufacturing tolerances, and thermal management in these high-order systems are also discussed. This one-port multicavity approach offers a viable path toward increasing the sensitive volume of haloscopes, enabling more efficient exploration of the axion dark matter parameter space in high-frequency regimes.
