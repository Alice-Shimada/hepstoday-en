Motivation: To enable efficient, chirally symmetric quantum simulation of Dirac fermions on quantum hardware, avoiding prohibitive resource costs of exact overlap operator implementation.

Method: Quantum signal processing (QSP) is applied to construct an approximate overlap Hamiltonian that satisfies the Ginsparg-Wilson relation up to controllable error ε_e; the method leverages QSP’s ability to implement matrix functions and reveals an emergent extra dimension analogous to domain-wall fermion construction.

Result: The QSP-based overlap Hamiltonian simulation incurs only O(log(1/ε_e)) overhead over Wilson-Dirac simulation; it has milder circuit complexity than domain-wall fermions while requiring fewer qubits; the algorithm geometrically realizes the overlap operator as a boundary mode of a higher-dimensional domain-wall-like structure.

Conclusion: Chirally symmetric fermion simulations are nearly 'free' in quantum computation—QSP enables practical overlap fermion dynamics with provable error control and resource efficiency rooted in topological field-theoretic structure.

Abstract: We give a quantum signal processing (QSP) algorithm for the overlap fermion Hamiltonian which preserves the Ginsparg-Wilson relation up to a controllable error $ε_e$. Quantum simulations of Dirac fermions with exact chiral symmetry are thus nearly free: applying the overlap Hamiltonian costs only a factor logarithmic in $ε_e$ more than the Wilson-Dirac Hamiltonian. Comparing to domain-wall fermions, a mild overhead is found in circuit complexity while reducing qubit costs. We show how QSP effectively constructs an extra dimension when simulating the overlap operator, illustrating that the scaling of quantum algorithms reflects the deeper physics of overlap fermions arising at the boundary of domain-wall fermions.
