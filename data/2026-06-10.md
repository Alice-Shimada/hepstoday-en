### [1] [Tame the Umklapp Processes in Real-Time Lattice Simulation for Hydrodynamics: An Ising Field Theory Study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09984)
*Xiaojun Yao*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper computes the real-time symmetric stress-energy tensor correlation function in a non-integrable Ising field theory with three stable scalar particles using lattice Hamiltonian simulations (exact diagonalization and MPS), revealing emergent relativistic hydrodynamic sound modes and yielding nonperturbative transport coefficients: ζ/s = 14.19 ± 0.90 and cₛ/c = 0.76 ± 0.02 at T ≈ 7.14 m₁.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To nonperturbatively study hydrodynamization and extract transport coefficients (e.g., bulk viscosity, speed of sound) in a non-integrable relativistic quantum field theory where analytical methods fail.

Method: Real-time lattice Hamiltonian simulation using classical exact diagonalization and matrix product state (MPS) tensor networks; analysis of the symmetric stress-energy tensor correlation function in the scaling regime; suppression of Umklapp processes confirmed; extraction of ζ/s and cₛ/c from long-wavelength, late-time dynamics.

Result: Emergence of relativistic hydrodynamic sound modes; bulk viscosity to entropy density ratio ζ/s = 14.19 ± 0.90; speed of sound cₛ/c = 0.76 ± 0.02 at temperature T ≈ 7.14 in units of the lightest particle mass; Umklapp processes suppressed in the scaling region.

Conclusion: Real-time lattice Hamiltonian simulations are a viable, nonperturbative tool for probing hydrodynamization and computing transport coefficients in non-integrable QFTs.

Abstract: We calculate the real-time symmetric correlation function of the stress-energy tensor for a non-integrable Ising field theory consisting of three stable scalar particles via lattice Hamiltonian simulation. Using classical exact diagonalization and the matrix product state tensor network methods, we find that in the scaling region of the lattice theory, Umklapp processes are suppressed and the sound modes of relativistic hydrodynamics emerge at long wavelength and late time. The extracted ratio of bulk viscosity to entropy density is $ζ/s=14.19\pm 0.90$ and the speed of sound is $c_s/c=0.76 \pm 0.02$ at the temperature $T\approx 7.14$ in units of the lowest stable particle's mass. Our study demonstrates the utility of real-time lattice Hamiltonian simulation for describing hydrodynamization and calculating transport coefficients nonperturbatively.

</details>


### [2] [Momentum Dependence of Heavy Quark Diffusion in a Thermal Gluonic Plasma on the Lattice](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10049)
*Harshit Pandey,Sayantan Sharma*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper introduces a lattice-based numerical method to compute momentum-dependent drag and diffusion coefficients of heavy quarks in a non-perturbative, thermal QCD plasma.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To determine the momentum dependence of heavy quark transport coefficients—drag and diffusion—in a realistic, non-perturbative, thermal non-Abelian plasma, where standard perturbative approaches fail.

Method: Uses a three-dimensional lattice discretization of an effective theory for high-temperature QCD describing soft gluons; implements a numerical strategy to simulate heavy quark dynamics across varying initial momenta in the thermalized plasma.

Result: First non-perturbative extraction of momentum-dependent heavy quark drag and diffusion coefficients in a thermal, non-Abelian plasma.

Conclusion: The proposed lattice-based approach enables quantitative, momentum-resolved transport studies of heavy quarks in strongly coupled QCD matter, bridging a key gap between theory and heavy-ion collision phenomenology.

Abstract: We study the dynamics of a heavy quark in a thermal plasma consisting of non-perturbatively interacting soft momentum gluons at high temperatures, described in terms of an effective theory of QCD discretized on a three-dimensional lattice. We propose a numerical strategy that allows us to simulate the dynamics of a heavy quark for different values of initial momenta in this thermalized plasma. This allows, for the first time, to extract the momentum dependence of the heavy quark drag and diffusion coefficients in a non-perturbatively interacting thermal, non-Abelian plasma.

### [4] [Combined Analysis of Lattice QCD and Experimental Data on the Pion Transition Form Factor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11181)
*Franziska Hagelstein,Danaheb Naomi Navarro Durán,Timon Esser,Vadim Lensky,Sotiris Pitelis,Vladyslava Sharkovska*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper proposes a global one-stage fit combining lattice QCD and experimental data on the pion transition form factor (TFF) using a modified z-expansion, improving TFF uncertainty by up to 3× in the singly-virtual limit and muon g−2 pion-pole uncertainty by 1.5×.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To improve the precision of the hadronic light-by-light scattering contribution to the muon anomalous magnetic moment (g−2), which critically depends on the pion transition form factor (TFF); current limitations arise from separate use of lattice QCD (strong at doubly-virtual kinematics) and e+e− experiments (high-precision singly-virtual data).

Method: A statistically rigorous global one-stage fitting framework based on the modified z-expansion; incorporates synthetic jackknife replicate sampling for covariance propagation and normalized χ² weighting to combine lattice QCD and experimental (e+e− scattering) datasets while respecting their complementary kinematic coverage.

Result: Inclusion of experimental data reduces TFF uncertainty by up to a factor of three in the singly-virtual limit; the pion-pole contribution to muon g−2 uncertainty improves by a factor of 1.5, limited by the g−2 integral’s strong sensitivity to low-Q² where normalization constraints already dominate precision.

Conclusion: Combined analysis of LQCD and experimental TFF data is feasible and beneficial, yielding significant gains in TFF precision; however, marginal returns for the final g−2 pion-pole uncertainty highlight the need for improved low-Q² lattice control or alternative pole modeling.

Abstract: The evaluation of the hadronic light-by-light scattering contribution to the muon anomalous magnetic moment requires precise knowledge of the pion transition form factor (TFF). In this work, we present a feasibility study for a combined analysis of lattice QCD (LQCD) and experimental data. Our methodology is driven by the goal of combining complementary datasets to leverage their respective kinematic advantages: while LQCD provides robust predictions for the doubly-virtual TFF, $e^+e^-$ scattering experiments offer high-precision singly-virtual measurements up to large momentum transfers. To ensure a statistically rigorous combination, we implement a global one-stage fitting approach based on the modified $z$-expansion, utilizing synthetic jackknife replicate sampling and a normalized $χ^2$ weighting scheme. We demonstrate that the inclusion of experimental data substantially tightens the constraints on the pion TFF, yielding up to a factor of three reduction in uncertainty in the singly-virtual limit. In contrast, the uncertainty of the resulting pion-pole contribution to the muon $g-2$ improves by a factor of $1.5$. This more modest improvement reflects the fact that the $g-2$ integral is heavily dominated by the low-$Q^2$ region, which is already well constrained by physical normalization constraints.

</details>
