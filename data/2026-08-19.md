### [2] [Computing the Critical Temperature of the Affine-Transformed $D=3$ Ising Model Using Masked Autoregressive Flow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17006)
*Kai Svenson,George T. Fleming,Richard C. Brower,Nobuyuki Matsumoto,Rohan Misra*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper compares the multiple histogram (MH) method and masked autoregressive flow (MAF) for estimating the critical temperature β_c of the affine-transformed Ising model on an FCC lattice; MAF proves computationally more efficient and scalable, with added benefits like volume extrapolation.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To construct a conformal field theory on arbitrarily curved manifolds, accurate and efficient determination of the critical temperature β_c of the Ising model on nontrivial lattices (e.g., FCC) under affine transformations is required; standard Monte Carlo interpolation/extrapolation methods face computational and accuracy challenges.

Method: Compares two methods for interpolating/extrapolating Monte Carlo observables: (1) the traditional multiple histogram (MH) method, and (2) a modern machine learning approach using masked autoregressive flows (MAF) to estimate the observable probability density function; both applied to measure β_c in the affine-transformed Ising model on the FCC lattice.

Result: MAF achieves comparable or better accuracy than MH in estimating β_c, with significantly improved computational scalability; MAF also enables reliable extrapolation in system volume—a capability not readily available in MH.

Conclusion: MAF is a viable, efficient, and flexible alternative to MH for critical-point estimation in lattice field theories, especially in contexts requiring multi-parameter extrapolation (e.g., geometry, volume), supporting future CFT construction on curved manifolds.

Abstract: The simple Ising model provides a rich environment to build and study lattice field theories. As part of an ongoing project to construct a conformal field theory (CFT) on an arbitrarily curved manifold, in this work we develop methods to measure the critical temperature $β_c$ of the affine-transformed Ising model on the face-centered cubic (FCC) lattice.
