### [1] [A Projected Semiexplicit Integrator for Dissipative Systems with Configuration-Dependent Kinetic Energy: Contact-Herglotz Formulation and Benchmarks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17198)
*Lorena Loera-Galeana,Santiago Mejía,Espartaco Alvarado,Héctor Medel-Cobaxin*

Main category: math-ph

TL;DR: This paper introduces a novel projected Pihajoki-contact integrator for non-separable contact Hamiltonian systems (e.g., damped double pendulum), combining phase-space duplication, symmetric diagonal projection, and exact Herglotz action updates; it achieves second-order accuracy, exact conformal symplecticity under constant friction (e^{−γτ} scaling of dη), O(τ³) contact-form residual, and superior long-time stability over Tao and unprojected methods—especially where frozen-coordinate splittings fail.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Standard explicit contact splitting integrators are limited to separable or diagonally kinetic Hamiltonians (e.g., spherical pendulum), failing for non-separable metrics with momentum cross-terms (e.g., double pendulum); there is a need for a robust, parameter-free, higher-order integrator for general dissipative mechanical systems governed by contact geometry.

Method: Projected Pihajoki-contact integrator: (1) duplicates phase space (q,p,η) → (q₁,p₁,η₁,q₂,p₂,η₂); (2) applies symmetric Strang-type steps including exact Herglotz updates for the action variable η; (3) enforces physical consistency via orthogonal projection onto the diagonal subspace (q₁=q₂, p₁=p₂, η₁=η₂) at every step; (4) uses constant-friction half-steps and confines nonlinear solves to the 2n projection variables; no binding parameter required.

Result: The method achieves second-order convergence on benchmark systems (damped double pendulum, spherical pendulum, torus particle); preserves the conformally symplectic identity ω → e^{−γτ}ω exactly under constant friction; yields O(τ³) one-step residual in the contact form dη + p·dq; controls long-term energy and contact drift even in coarse/stiff regimes where Tao and unprojected averages diverge; outperforms first-order alternatives for dense metrics.

Conclusion: The projected Pihajoki-contact integrator fills a critical gap for geometric integration of non-separable contact systems: it delivers guaranteed structure preservation, high-order accuracy, and numerical robustness without restrictive separability assumptions—establishing a new standard for dissipative mechanical simulation where exact contactomorphism splittings are unavailable.

Abstract: Contact Hamiltonian dynamics gives dissipative mechanics an intrinsic action variable, but explicit contact splittings reach only kinetic energies whose terms are exactly integrable: frozen-coordinate diagonal metrics (the spherical pendulum, a torus particle) are included, while dense metrics with momentum cross terms, with the double pendulum as flagship, are not. We introduce a projected Pihajoki-contact integrator for this non-separable setting, combining phase-space duplication, symmetric projection onto the physical diagonal, and constant-friction damping half-steps, with the action factor carried by an exact Herglotz update. As in the projected extended-phase-space framework it builds on, the construction needs no binding parameter, returns the copies to the diagonal at every step, and confines the nonlinear solve to the $2n$ projection variables. For constant friction the step rescales $ω=dη$ by the exact factor $e^{-γτ}$ when the projection is solved exactly (a classical conformally symplectic identity, realized here for this class), while time-symmetry, consistency, and smoothness yield an $O(τ^3)$ one-step contact-form residual, a bound not specific to the contact form. On the damped double pendulum, spherical pendulum, and torus particle the method is second-order accurate, reproduces the contact decay law, and controls long-time energy and contact drift in coarse or stiff regimes where the Tao baseline and the unprojected average lose the solution. A head-to-head with exact-contactomorphism splittings delimits the niche: where a frozen-coordinate splitting exists it preserves the contact form exactly and wins at matched cost; for the dense double-pendulum metric the realizable alternative is first-order with a prohibitive constant and the projected method prevails. The contact-form estimate is local, one-step, and constant-friction.

### [2] [Romanovski polynomials, Gegenbauer connections, and $\mathrm{su}(1,1)$ ladder structures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17221)
*José A. Vallejo,Mariana Kirchbach*

Main category: math-ph

TL;DR: This paper analyzes monic Romanovski (pseudo-Jacobi) polynomials with degree-dependent parameters, derives explicit parameter-preserving ladder operators, recovers three-term recurrence, constructs polynomials iteratively from constants, solves the connection problem with Gegenbauer polynomials (α=0 case), and reveals intrinsic su(1,1) module structure under x = cotχ substitution.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To systematically understand the algebraic and representation-theoretic structure of Romanovski polynomials with nonstandard, degree-dependent parameters β_K = −K and α_K = c/(K+1), particularly their ladder relations, recurrence properties, and connections to classical orthogonal polynomials and Lie algebra modules.

Method: Explicit derivation of first-order parameter-preserving lowering and raising operators; composition to recover three-term recurrence; iterative first-order construction of polynomials; solution of the connection problem via finite triangular transformation to the α = 0 (Gegenbauer) family; coordinate substitution x = cotχ to expose su(1,1) symmetry; analysis of weight modules on the (n,ℓ)-lattice.

Result: Parameter-preserving ladder operators with real coefficients are obtained; full iterative construction of R_n^{α,β} from constant polynomial is established; connection to Gegenbauer polynomials is solved in finite triangular form; under x = cotχ, the operators become ℓ-dependent but K-independent; dressed functions realize intrinsic lowest-weight su(1,1) modules along fixed-ℓ columns; the n = 0 row requires boundary prescription and rescaling.

Conclusion: The Romanovski polynomials with degree-dependent α_K and β_K admit a rich algebraic structure: they support canonical ladder operations, embed into the Gegenbauer family via triangular connection, and—under cotangential mapping—realize su(1,1) representation modules column-wise, revealing deep links between orthogonal polynomials, special functions, and Lie algebra theory.

Abstract: We study the monic Romanovski (pseudo-Jacobi) polynomials corresponding to the degree dependent parameters $β_K=-K$, $α_K=c/(K+1)$, with $K=n+\ell$. We write down, in explicit form, the parameter preserving first order lowering and raising relations at fixed $(α,β)$, with real proportionality constants. Combining them one recovers the standard three-term recurrence existing in any hypergeometric-type family, and iterating them one gets an ordered first order construction of $R_n^{α,β}$ starting from the unit constant polynomial. We also solve the connection problem with the $α=0$ family in a finite triangular form, identifying this last family with the Gegenbauer polynomials, and transferring the ladder relations to the $(n,\ell)$ lattice. After the substitution $x=\cotχ$, the in-level operators depend on $\ell$, but not on $K$. The resulting dressed functions support intrinsic lowest weight $\mathrm{su}(1,1)$ modules along the columns with $\ell$ fixed, while the circular row ($n=0$) requires an explicit boundary prescription and a rescaling.

### [3] [Cohomology for solutions of polygon equations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17263)
*Serban Matei Mihalache,Tomoro Mochida*

Main category: math-ph

TL;DR: This paper constructs semi-simplicial sets from set-theoretic solutions of polygon equations, defines associated (co)homology, proves an equivalence between such solutions and higher Segal semi-simplicial sets, and generalizes the Dyckerhoff–Kapranov pentagon/2-Segal correspondence.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To generalize the pentagon equation and its categorical interpretation via 2-Segal sets to broader polygon equations, and to develop a homological framework for their set-theoretic solutions.

Method: Constructs semi-simplicial sets of permitted colorings from set-theoretic polygon equation solutions; defines corresponding (co)homology groups; proves categorical equivalence with higher Segal semi-simplicial sets satisfying specific conditions using combinatorial and categorical techniques.

Result: Establishes an equivalence of categories between set-theoretic solutions of n-gon equations and (n−1)-Segal semi-simplicial sets satisfying a 'non-degeneracy' condition; recovers the Dyckerhoff–Kapranov result for n=5 as a special case; derives structural properties of the induced (co)homology.

Conclusion: Polygon equations admit a unified higher-categorical and homological treatment via higher Segal theory, extending the well-known pentagon/2-Segal correspondence to arbitrary n-gons.

Abstract: Polygon equations form a family of equations generalizing the pentagon equation. In this paper, we construct semi-simplicial sets of permitted colorings associated with set-theoretic solutions of polygon equations and use them to define the corresponding (co)homology groups. We investigate several properties of these groups and establish an equivalence of categories between set-theoretic solutions of polygon equations and higher Segal semi-simplicial sets satisfying certain conditions. As a special case, our result recovers the correspondence between bijective set-theoretic solutions of the pentagon equation and $2$-Segal semi-simplicial sets proved by Dyckerhoff--Kapranov.

</details>
