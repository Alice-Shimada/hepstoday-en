### [1] [Search for resonant production of lepton-enriched semivisible jets in proton-proton collisions at $\sqrt{s}$ = 13 TeV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05323)
*CMS Collaboration*

Main category: hep-ex

TL;DR: This paper presents the first experimental search for lepton-enriched semivisible jets (SVJs) — a signature of a strongly coupled dark sector — using 138 fb⁻¹ of 13 TeV pp collision data from CMS. It employs a dual neural network strategy (GNN for jet ID, FCNN for event-level classification) to probe two scenarios: SVJℓ (all lepton flavors) and SVJτ (tau-dominated), yielding 95% CL exclusions up to 4.7 TeV (SVJℓ) and 1.8–3.5 TeV (SVJτ) for a Z′ mediator.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To test strongly coupled dark sector models that produce semivisible jets with significant leptonic content — a theoretically motivated but previously unexplored signature bridging fully visible and fully invisible dark hadron decays.

Method: Search using CMS data (138 fb⁻¹, √s = 13 TeV); targets resonant Z′ → dark hadrons → SVJs + MET; defines two signatures (SVJℓ and SVJτ) based on lepton flavor content; applies jet–MET alignment requirement; uses graph neural network for jet-level identification and fully connected neural network for combined jet- and event-level discrimination and background estimation.

Result: Excludes Z′ mediator masses up to 4.7 TeV at 95% CL in the SVJℓ scenario, and 1.8–3.5 TeV in the SVJτ scenario, assuming gZ′qq = 0.25 and prompt dark hadron decays.

Conclusion: First experimental constraints on lepton-enriched semivisible jets are established, significantly extending the reach for strongly coupled dark sectors with leptonic decays and demonstrating the efficacy of hybrid ML techniques in complex resonance searches.

Abstract: This search targets the resonant production of lepton-enriched semivisible jets (SVJs) from a strongly coupled dark sector, using 138 fb$^{-1}$ of proton-proton collision data collected with the CMS detector at the CERN LHC at $\sqrt{s}$ = 13 TeV. Two scenarios are investigated: jets enriched in all lepton flavors (SVJ$\ell$ signature) and jets predominantly enriched in tau leptons (SVJ$τ$ signature). The analysis focuses on final states in which the missing transverse momentum is aligned with jets containing nonisolated leptons. A dual machine-learning strategy is employed, using a graph neural network for jet identification and a fully connected neural network that combines jet- and event-level information to enhance signal sensitivity and background estimation. The signal models assume a heavy Z' mediator with a benchmark coupling of 0.25 to standard model quarks, together with prompt decays of unstable dark hadrons. In the SVJ$\ell$ scenario, mediator masses up to 4.7 TeV are excluded at 95% confidence level, while masses between 1.8 and 3.5 TeV are excluded in the SVJ$τ$ scenario. These results provide the first experimental constraints on lepton-enriched semivisible jets.

*Igor Denisenko*

Main category: hep-ex

TL;DR: The Spin Physics Detector (SPD) at the NICA collider aims to probe gluon transverse-momentum-dependent (TMD) parton distribution functions (PDFs), gluon transversity, and tensor PDFs in protons and deuterons via spin asymmetries in charmonia, open charm, and prompt photon production using polarized beams up to 27 GeV and luminosity up to 10³² cm⁻²s⁻¹.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To advance understanding of nucleon spin structure—particularly the role of gluons—by exploiting NICA’s unique capability to collide polarized proton and deuteron beams at high energy and luminosity.

Method: Measure single and double spin asymmetries in charmonium, open charm, and prompt photon production processes using the SPD detector; extract gluon TMD PDFs, transversity, and tensor PDFs through QCD factorization-based analysis of spin-dependent observables.

Result: Enables first-time access to gluon TMD PDFs and deuteron gluon transversity/tensor PDFs; establishes a comprehensive spin physics program at NICA, including baseline unpolarized measurements during commissioning.

Conclusion: SPD is a key facility for precision gluon spin studies, uniquely positioned to constrain poorly known gluonic contributions to nucleon spin structure and test fundamental QCD predictions in the moderate-x, high-luminosity regime.

Abstract: The Spin Physics Detector (SPD) is a universal detector at the NICA collider under commissioning at JINR, Dubna. The SPD is intended to study the spin structure of the proton and deuteron and other spin-related phenomena using a unique possibility to operate with polarized proton and deuteron beams at a collision energies up to 27~GeV and a luminosity up to 10$^{32}$cm$^{-2}$s$^{-1}$. As the primary goal, the experiment aims to provide access to the gluon TMD PDFs in the proton and deuteron, as well as the gluon transversity distribution and tensor PDFs in the deuteron, via the measurements of relevant single and double spin asymmetries using different complementary probes such as charmonia, open charm, and prompt photon production processes. Other polarized and unpolarized physics studies are possible, especially at the first stage of NICA operation with reduced luminosity and collision energy of the proton and ion beams. The physics program of the SPD and the design of the SPD setup will be presented.
