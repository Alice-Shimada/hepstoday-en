Abstract: Understanding when gravitational collapse produces a black hole remains a central challenge in general relativity. Black-hole uniqueness results characterize the stationary endpoint under suitable assumptions, while singularity theorems establish geodesic incompleteness in the presence of trapped surfaces and appropriate energy and global conditions. Neither, however, provides a general criterion for horizon formation. The hoop conjecture offers such a criterion by relating the enclosed mass to the compactness of a matter distribution in every direction.
