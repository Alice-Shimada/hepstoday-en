### [1] [On the non-radial oscillations of realistic anisotropic neutron stars: Axial modes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06418)
*Jose F. Rodriguez-Ruiz,L. M. Becerra,F. D. Lora-Clavijo*

Main category: gr-qc

TL;DR: This paper studies axial oscillation modes of anisotropic neutron stars, showing that pressure anisotropy couples matter and metric perturbations—unlike the isotropic case—and reveals a universal scaling of scaled w-mode frequency and damping time with stellar compactness, independent of equation of state or anisotropy model.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To address the underexplored axial oscillation modes of neutron stars and investigate how pressure anisotropy—physically motivated by exotic core components—affects their non-radial perturbations and gravitational-wave signatures.

Method: Linearized Einstein field equations for axial perturbations are solved numerically for static, spherically symmetric, anisotropic neutron star models; parameterized anisotropic equations of state are used alongside realistic nuclear EoSs; polar/axial decoupling is leveraged, and scaling behavior is analyzed via compactness-dependent fits.

Result: Pressure anisotropy couples axial matter and metric perturbations; increasing mass lowers w-mode frequency and increases damping time; softer EoSs yield slightly higher frequencies; larger tangential pressure supports more massive stars with lower frequencies and longer damping times; M-scaled frequency and damping time follow a universal quadratic function of compactness M/R.

Conclusion: Axial w-modes of anisotropic neutron stars encode robust, model-independent information about stellar compactness, enabling potential use as quasi-universal probes in gravitational-wave asteroseismology—even when internal anisotropy and EoS uncertainty are present.

Abstract: Non-radial oscillation modes of neutron stars serve as diagnostics of their internal composition and relativistic structure. In this work, we investigate the perturbations of static and spherically symmetric neutron stars characterized by an anisotropic pressure. Given the background symmetry, perturbations decouple into polar and axial modes. To date, axial modes have remained less explored, primarily because matter and metric perturbations decouple in the isotropic limit. In this work, we provide a consistent treatment of axial modes and demonstrate that pressure anisotropy induces a direct coupling between matter and metric perturbations. We employ parameterized anisotropy models that ensure consistency with the treatment of matter perturbations. We numerically integrate the linearized Einstein field equations for the axial modes, employing a diverse set of realistic equations of state. Our results indicate that as the stellar mass grows, the frequency of the lower $w$-mode generally decreases, while its damping time increases. Softer equation of states typically yield slightly higher oscillation frequencies. Furthermore, larger anisotropy (i.e., when the tangential pressure exceeds the radial pressure) allows for more massive equilibrium configurations, which correspondingly leads to lower oscillation frequencies and prolonged damping times. Finally, we demonstrate that the frequency and damping time, both scaled by the stellar mass, exhibit a nearly universal quadratic dependence on the stellar compactness, remaining largely insensitive to both the underlying equation of state and the specific anisotropy model.

### [2] [Quasi-homogeneous black hole geometrothermodynamics in Einstein-Maxwell theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06602)
*Hernando Quevedo*

Main category: gr-qc

TL;DR: This paper reviews geometrothermodynamics (GTD) as a Legendre-invariant framework for thermodynamically describing non-extensive, quasi-homogeneous, self-gravitating systems—especially black holes—whose fundamental equations violate standard Euler homogeneity; it constructs equilibrium manifolds, derives curvature metrics for RN, Kerr, and Kerr-Newman black holes, and links curvature singularities to thermodynamic phase transitions via heat capacity divergences.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To extend thermodynamic formalism to gravitational systems like black holes, which are non-extensive and lack standard Euler homogeneity due to self-gravitation and quasi-homogeneity of their fundamental equations.

Method: Constructs a Legendre-invariant geometric structure (GTD) for equilibrium thermodynamics; derives Riemannian metrics on the equilibrium manifold; computes curvature scalars and identifies singularities; correlates them with heat capacity divergences as indicators of phase transitions.

Result: Demonstrates that Reissner-Nordström, Kerr, and Kerr-Newman black holes admit well-defined GTD metrics whose curvature singularities coincide exactly with known thermodynamic phase transition points (e.g., divergences in CΦ or CJ).

Conclusion: Geometrothermodynamics provides a consistent, invariant geometric description of black hole thermodynamics, where curvature singularities of the equilibrium manifold serve as intrinsic geometric signatures of thermodynamic phase transitions—bypassing reliance on extensive variables or Euler homogeneity.

Abstract: In this review, we establish the mathematical framework of geometrothermodynamics (GTD) as a formalism capable of describing non-extensive, quasi-homogeneous, self-gravitating systems in a Legendre-invariant manner. We argue that the fundamental equations of black holes are quasi-homogeneous functions, a property that invalidates the standard Euler identity of laboratory thermodynamics. We derive the metrics for the equilibrium manifold and analyze their curvature singularities for the Reissner-Nordström, Kerr, and Kerr-Newman black holes. Furthermore, we establish a direct correspondence between the curvature singularities of the equilibrium space and phase transitions, as determined by the divergences of the corresponding heat capacities.

  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To resolve the Gribov ambiguity in non-Abelian gauge theories by leveraging cohomological changes induced by spontaneous symmetry breaking.

Method: Cohomological analysis of SU(2) (and extendable to SU(N)) gauge fields under spontaneous symmetry breaking; construction of a renormalizable unitary gauge fixing via Morse-theoretic functional extremization.

Result: A specific field combination becomes cohomologically equivalent to a matter field, rendering it Gribov-free on-shell; the Gribov condition is automatically satisfied in this gauge-fixed framework.

Conclusion: The Gribov problem is circumvented on-shell through cohomological restructuring induced by spontaneous symmetry breaking, enabling consistent renormalizable unitary quantization.

Abstract: We show that, for an $SU(2)$ gauge field (the reasoning extends trivially to $SU(N)$), spontaneous symmetry breaking changes the field cohomology. This defines a new field with cohomological properties characteristic of matter fields. Consequently, the construction of a renormalizable unitary gauge fixing, following Morse's problem of functional extremization, leads to the Gribov condition being automatically solved on-shell. This result occurs because a specific combination of fields is cohomologically matter-like and therefore free of the Gribov problem.

### [5] [The Hagedorn Temperature as a Nonequilibrium Dynamical Bottleneck in String Thermodynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06497)

<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To move beyond equilibrium interpretations of the Hagedorn regime—traditionally viewed through singularities in canonical partition functions or microcanonical long-string dominance—and instead understand its dynamical, nonequilibrium consequences using a first-principles quantum thermodynamic framework that does not rely on global thermal equilibrium or ensemble definitions.

Method: Applies Steepest-Entropy-Ascent Quantum Thermodynamics (SEAQT) to string theory's state manifold: derives the exact scalar evolution equation for instantaneous (state-dependent) inverse temperature; analyzes dynamics in the commuting limit, where higher-order energy fluctuations govern response; generalizes to open systems via system–reservoir splitting of the SEAQT metric; performs diagonal Hagedorn evaluation to isolate dependence on both exponential density-of-states growth and algebraic prefactor.

Result: The Hagedorn regime acts as a dynamical bottleneck: inverse temperature evolution slows down due to higher-order fluctuation moments; reservoir coupling can induce effective Hagedorn slowing-down in subsystems; bottleneck strength depends jointly on exponential growth rate *and* algebraic prefactor of the density of states; long-string dominance is linked to intrinsic thermodynamic slowing-down and failure of effective low-energy descriptions.

Conclusion: The Hagedorn behavior is not merely an equilibrium limiting phenomenon but a fundamental nonequilibrium dynamical feature—manifesting as thermodynamic inertia—that may underlie the breakdown of semiclassical and effective-field-theory descriptions in quantum gravity.

Abstract: The Hagedorn regime of string theory is usually understood as an equilibrium limiting phenomenon: the exponential growth of the density of states makes the canonical partition function singular at the Hagedorn temperature, while in the microcanonical description additional energy is absorbed predominantly by highly excited long-string configurations. In this work we revisit this regime from a nonequilibrium perspective using Steepest-Entropy-Ascent Quantum Thermodynamics (SEAQT), where thermodynamic evolution is formulated directly on the state manifold and does not require a globally well-defined canonical ensemble. The inverse temperature is treated as an instantaneous, state-dependent quantity, and we derive its exact scalar evolution equation. In the commuting limit, this dynamics is controlled by higher-order fluctuation moments, showing that the Hagedorn regime may act as a dynamical bottleneck for the response of the effective intensive variable. We then extend the construction to an open-system setting through a system--reservoir splitting of the SEAQT metric and show that reservoir coupling can drive the subsystem toward effective Hagedorn slowing-down. A diagonal Hagedorn evaluation further shows that the strength of this bottleneck depends not only on the exponential density of states, but also on its algebraic prefactor. These results provide a nonequilibrium interpretation of Hagedorn behavior and suggest a connection between long-string dominance, thermodynamic slowing-down, and the breakdown of effective descriptions in quantum gravity.

### [8] [From Baby Universes to Narain Moduli: Topological Boundary Averaging in SymTFTs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06653)
*Xingyang Yu*

Main category: hep-th

TL;DR: This paper proposes a SymTFT-based interpretation of ensemble averaging in low-dimensional holography, where averaging is performed over topological boundary conditions—yielding distinct absolute completions of the same relative theory—rather than over arbitrary local dynamics; it formalizes this using cap functionals and groupoid/Haar measures, and demonstrates it in Marolf–Maxfield (Poisson/Bell moments) and Narain (Zamolodchikov-measured moduli) examples.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To provide a principled, topological-field-theoretic foundation for ensemble averaging in holography—replacing ad hoc statistical ensembles with structured averages over physically meaningful topological completions of relative theories.

Method: Uses symmetric topological field theory (SymTFT) framework: fixes SymTFT and physical boundary condition, averages over topological boundary conditions at the opposite end of the SymTFT slab; employs cap functionals and groupoid or Haar-type measures to define the ensemble; identifies topological boundaries with finite sets (Marolf–Maxfield) and maximal isotropic subgroups (Narain BF theory).

Result: Reproduces Poisson/Bell-polynomial moments in Marolf–Maxfield closed-string sector via groupoid sum over finite-set-labelled boundaries; recovers Narain moduli space average with Zamolodchikov measure via averaging over maximal isotropic subgroups of an ℝ-valued BF SymTFT.

Conclusion: Ensemble averaging in low-dimensional holography admits a natural SymTFT interpretation as averaging over topological completions—i.e., topological boundary conditions—of a fixed relative theory; this unifies disparate ensemble constructions and suggests generalizations to JT gravity, RMT, Virasoro T(Q)FT, and 3D gravity.

Abstract: We propose a SymTFT interpretation of ensemble averaging in low-dimensional holography. The central operation is to keep fixed both the SymTFT and the physical boundary condition, while averaging over topological boundary conditions at the other end of the SymTFT slab. Each such boundary condition gives an absolute completion of the same relative theory, so the ensemble is interpreted as an average over topological completions rather than over arbitrary local dynamics. In the closed-string sector of the Marolf--Maxfield model, topological boundary conditions are labelled by finite sets, and the groupoid sum reproduces the Poisson/Bell-polynomial moments. In the Narain case, compact topological boundary conditions of an $\mathbb{R}$-valued BF SymTFT are identified with maximal isotropic subgroups, so that topological-boundary averaging becomes the usual Narain moduli average with Zamolodchikov measure. We also discuss possible extensions to JT gravity, random matrix theory, Virasoro T(Q)FT, and 3D gravity.

</details>
