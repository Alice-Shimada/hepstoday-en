### [1] [On the non-radial oscillations of realistic anisotropic neutron stars: Axial modes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06418)
*Jose F. Rodriguez-Ruiz,L. M. Becerra,F. D. Lora-Clavijo*

Main category: gr-qc

TL;DR: This paper studies axial oscillation modes of anisotropic neutron stars, showing that pressure anisotropy couples matter and metric perturbations—unlike the isotropic case—and reveals universal scaling of w-mode frequency and damping time with compactness.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Axial modes of neutron stars have been underexplored due to decoupling of matter and metric perturbations in isotropic models; this work addresses that gap by incorporating pressure anisotropy, which breaks the decoupling and enables new probes of internal composition and relativistic structure.

Method: The authors perform linear stability analysis of static, spherically symmetric neutron star backgrounds with anisotropic pressure; they derive and numerically integrate the coupled axial perturbation equations (Einstein + matter) using parameterized anisotropy models consistent with conservation laws and a suite of realistic equations of state.

Result: Pressure anisotropy induces matter–metric coupling in axial modes; increasing stellar mass lowers w-mode frequency and increases damping time; softer EOS yields slightly higher frequencies; larger tangential pressure supports more massive stars with lower frequencies and longer damping times; frequency and damping time, when scaled by mass, follow a universal quadratic function of compactness, insensitive to EOS or anisotropy model.

Conclusion: Axial w-modes of anisotropic neutron stars encode robust, universal signatures of compactness—enabling equation-of-state–agnostic asteroseismology—and highlight the critical role of pressure anisotropy in neutron star oscillations and structure.

Abstract: Non-radial oscillation modes of neutron stars serve as diagnostics of their internal composition and relativistic structure. In this work, we investigate the perturbations of static and spherically symmetric neutron stars characterized by an anisotropic pressure. Given the background symmetry, perturbations decouple into polar and axial modes. To date, axial modes have remained less explored, primarily because matter and metric perturbations decouple in the isotropic limit. In this work, we provide a consistent treatment of axial modes and demonstrate that pressure anisotropy induces a direct coupling between matter and metric perturbations. We employ parameterized anisotropy models that ensure consistency with the treatment of matter perturbations. We numerically integrate the linearized Einstein field equations for the axial modes, employing a diverse set of realistic equations of state. Our results indicate that as the stellar mass grows, the frequency of the lower $w$-mode generally decreases, while its damping time increases. Softer equation of states typically yield slightly higher oscillation frequencies. Furthermore, larger anisotropy (i.e., when the tangential pressure exceeds the radial pressure) allows for more massive equilibrium configurations, which correspondingly leads to lower oscillation frequencies and prolonged damping times. Finally, we demonstrate that the frequency and damping time, both scaled by the stellar mass, exhibit a nearly universal quadratic dependence on the stellar compactness, remaining largely insensitive to both the underlying equation of state and the specific anisotropy model.

### [2] [Quasi-homogeneous black hole geometrothermodynamics in Einstein-Maxwell theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06602)
*Hernando Quevedo*

Main category: gr-qc

TL;DR: This paper introduces geometrothermodynamics (GTD) as a Legendre-invariant framework for thermodynamically describing non-extensive, quasi-homogeneous, self-gravitating systems—especially black holes—whose fundamental equations violate the standard Euler identity; it constructs equilibrium manifold metrics and links their curvature singularities to thermodynamic phase transitions via heat capacity divergences.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Standard thermodynamic formalism (e.g., Euler identity, extensive scaling) fails for self-gravitating, non-extensive systems like black holes; a geometric, Legendre-invariant approach is needed to consistently describe their equilibrium thermodynamics and phase structure.

Method: Develops geometrothermodynamics (GTD) by constructing Legendre-invariant Riemannian metrics on the equilibrium thermodynamic manifold; derives metric tensors for Reissner-Nordström, Kerr, and Kerr-Newman black holes; analyzes curvature singularities of these metrics and correlates them with heat capacity divergences.

Result: Demonstrates that black hole fundamental equations are quasi-homogeneous (not homogeneous), invalidating the standard Euler identity; obtains explicit GTD metrics for three classical black hole families; shows one-to-one correspondence between curvature singularities in the equilibrium manifold and second-order phase transitions signaled by heat capacity divergences.

Conclusion: Geometrothermodynamics provides a consistent, geometric, and Legendre-invariant thermodynamic description for black holes and other quasi-homogeneous gravitational systems; curvature singularities in the equilibrium manifold serve as intrinsic geometric indicators of thermodynamic phase transitions.

Abstract: In this review, we establish the mathematical framework of geometrothermodynamics (GTD) as a formalism capable of describing non-extensive, quasi-homogeneous, self-gravitating systems in a Legendre-invariant manner. We argue that the fundamental equations of black holes are quasi-homogeneous functions, a property that invalidates the standard Euler identity of laboratory thermodynamics. We derive the metrics for the equilibrium manifold and analyze their curvature singularities for the Reissner-Nordström, Kerr, and Kerr-Newman black holes. Furthermore, we establish a direct correspondence between the curvature singularities of the equilibrium space and phase transitions, as determined by the divergences of the corresponding heat capacities.

### [3] [Non-abelian field cohomology, its relation with spontaneous symmetry breaking and Morse's Theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06159)
*V. E. R. Lemes*

Main category: hep-th

TL;DR: Spontaneous symmetry breaking in SU(2) gauge theory alters field cohomology, yielding a matter-like field combination that bypasses the Gribov ambiguity on-shell within a renormalizable unitary gauge-fixing framework derived from Morse-theoretic functional extremization.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To resolve the Gribov ambiguity in non-Abelian gauge theories by leveraging cohomological changes induced by spontaneous symmetry breaking.

Method: Cohomological analysis of SU(2) (and generalizable to SU(N)) gauge fields under spontaneous symmetry breaking; application of Morse-theoretic functional extremization to construct a renormalizable unitary gauge fixing.

Result: A specific field combination acquires matter-like cohomological properties, rendering it immune to the Gribov problem on-shell; the Gribov condition is automatically satisfied in this gauge-fixing scheme.

Conclusion: The Gribov ambiguity is intrinsically avoided on-shell when gauge fields acquire matter-like cohomology via spontaneous symmetry breaking, enabling a consistent, renormalizable, and unitary quantization framework.

Abstract: We show that, for an $SU(2)$ gauge field (the reasoning extends trivially to $SU(N)$), spontaneous symmetry breaking changes the field cohomology. This defines a new field with cohomological properties characteristic of matter fields. Consequently, the construction of a renormalizable unitary gauge fixing, following Morse's problem of functional extremization, leads to the Gribov condition being automatically solved on-shell. This result occurs because a specific combination of fields is cohomologically matter-like and therefore free of the Gribov problem.

### [5] [The Hagedorn Temperature as a Nonequilibrium Dynamical Bottleneck in String Thermodynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06497)
*Cesar Damian,Oscar Loaiza-Brito*

Main category: hep-th

TL;DR: This paper reinterprets the Hagedorn regime in string theory from a nonequilibrium thermodynamic perspective using Steepest-Entropy-Ascent Quantum Thermodynamics (SEAQT), deriving an exact evolution equation for instantaneous inverse temperature and revealing the Hagedorn density of states as a dynamical bottleneck—dependent on both exponential growth and algebraic prefactor—that causes thermodynamic slowing-down and challenges effective descriptions in quantum gravity.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To move beyond equilibrium interpretations of the Hagedorn regime and understand its dynamical role in nonequilibrium quantum thermodynamics, especially in contexts where canonical ensembles fail (e.g., quantum gravity).

Method: Applies Steepest-Entropy-Ascent Quantum Thermodynamics (SEAQT) to formulate thermodynamic evolution intrinsically on the state manifold; derives exact scalar evolution equation for instantaneous, state-dependent inverse temperature; analyzes commuting limit involving higher-order fluctuation moments; extends framework to open systems via system–reservoir splitting of the SEAQT metric; performs diagonal Hagedorn evaluation to quantify bottleneck strength.

Result: Derives exact evolution law for inverse temperature in the Hagedorn regime; shows that dynamics is governed by higher-order energy fluctuations in the commuting limit, inducing a bottleneck; demonstrates reservoir coupling can induce effective Hagedorn slowing-down in subsystems; reveals bottleneck strength depends jointly on exponential density-of-states growth rate and its algebraic prefactor.

Conclusion: The Hagedorn regime is not merely an equilibrium singularity but a nonequilibrium dynamical bottleneck causing thermodynamic slowing-down; this links long-string dominance, breakdown of effective thermal descriptions, and foundational issues in quantum gravity.

Abstract: The Hagedorn regime of string theory is usually understood as an equilibrium limiting phenomenon: the exponential growth of the density of states makes the canonical partition function singular at the Hagedorn temperature, while in the microcanonical description additional energy is absorbed predominantly by highly excited long-string configurations. In this work we revisit this regime from a nonequilibrium perspective using Steepest-Entropy-Ascent Quantum Thermodynamics (SEAQT), where thermodynamic evolution is formulated directly on the state manifold and does not require a globally well-defined canonical ensemble. The inverse temperature is treated as an instantaneous, state-dependent quantity, and we derive its exact scalar evolution equation. We then extend the construction to an open-system setting through a system--reservoir splitting of the SEAQT metric and show that reservoir coupling can drive the subsystem toward effective Hagedorn slowing-down. A diagonal Hagedorn evaluation further shows that the strength of this bottleneck depends not only on the exponential density of states, but also on its algebraic prefactor. These results provide a nonequilibrium interpretation of Hagedorn behavior and suggest a connection between long-string dominance, thermodynamic slowing-down, and the breakdown of effective descriptions in quantum gravity.

### [8] [From Baby Universes to Narain Moduli: Topological Boundary Averaging in SymTFTs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06653)
*Xingyang Yu*

Main category: hep-th

TL;DR: This paper introduces a SymTFT-based interpretation of ensemble averaging in low-dimensional holography, where averaging is performed over topological boundary conditions—yielding absolute completions of a fixed relative theory—rather than over arbitrary local dynamics; it formalizes this via cap functionals and groupoid/Haar measures, and demonstrates it in Marolf–Maxfield (Poisson/Bell moments) and Narain (Zamolodchikov measure) examples.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To provide a principled, topological-field-theoretic foundation for ensemble averaging in holography—reconciling it with symmetry topological field theory (SymTFT) and clarifying whether ensembles average over physical theories or mathematical completions.

Method: Uses SymTFT slab geometry with fixed SymTFT and physical boundary condition; averages over topological boundary conditions at the opposite end using cap functionals and groupoid or Haar-type measures; identifies topological boundaries with finite sets (Marolf–Maxfield) or maximal isotropic subgroups (Narain BF theory).

Result: Reproduces Poisson/Bell-polynomial moments in Marolf–Maxfield closed-string sector via groupoid sum over finite-set-labelled boundaries; recovers Narain moduli space average with Zamolodchikov measure via averaging over compact maximal isotropic subgroups of an R-valued BF SymTFT.

Conclusion: Ensemble averaging in low-dimensional holography is naturally interpreted as averaging over topological completions (absolute theories) of a fixed relative theory, governed by SymTFT structure and encoded in cap functionals and appropriate categorical measures—offering a unifying framework extendable to JT gravity, RMT, Virasoro T(Q)FT, and 3D gravity.

Abstract: We propose a SymTFT interpretation of ensemble averaging in low-dimensional holography. The central operation is to keep fixed both the SymTFT and the physical boundary condition, while averaging over topological boundary conditions at the other end of the SymTFT slab. Each such boundary condition gives an absolute completion of the same relative theory, so the ensemble is interpreted as an average over topological completions rather than over arbitrary local dynamics. We formulate this construction in terms of cap functionals and their natural groupoid or Haar-type measures, and illustrate it in two examples. In the closed-string sector of the Marolf--Maxfield model, topological boundary conditions are labelled by finite sets, and the groupoid sum reproduces the Poisson/Bell-polynomial moments. In the Narain case, compact topological boundary conditions of an $\mathbb{R}$-valued BF SymTFT are identified with maximal isotropic subgroups, so that topological-boundary averaging becomes the usual Narain moduli average with Zamolodchikov measure. We also discuss possible extensions to JT gravity, random matrix theory, Virasoro T(Q)FT, and 3D gravity.
