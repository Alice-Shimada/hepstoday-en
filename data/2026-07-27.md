### [1] [Cross-section measurements of boosted Higgs boson production in final states with pairs of hadronically decaying $τ$-leptons with the ATLAS experiment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22193)
*ATLAS Collaboration*

Main category: hep-ex

TL;DR: This paper presents cross-section measurements of high-Lorentz boosted Higgs boson production decaying to hadronic ττ pairs, using ATLAS Run 2 (140 fb⁻¹ at 13 TeV) and partial Run 3 (162 fb⁻¹ at 13.6 TeV) data. It introduces dedicated boosted di-τ reconstruction and multivariate analysis to extend sensitivity beyond p_T^H > 300 GeV, where standard τ identification fails. Results include inclusive and differential (p_T^H) cross sections for ggF and VBF production, with 3.8σ observed significance for inclusive VBF-like production — constituting first evidence of boosted H→ττ.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Standard hadronic τ-lepton reconstruction loses efficiency at high Higgs transverse momentum (p_T^H > 300 GeV), limiting sensitivity to boosted H→ττ. This analysis addresses that gap by developing specialized techniques to probe the high-p_T regime—critical for testing Higgs couplings, EFTs, and anomalous VBF/ggF dynamics.

Method: Uses large-radius jets to reconstruct collimated ττ pairs; employs custom boosted di-τ identification and multivariate analysis (MVA) to separate signal from QCD and Z→ττ backgrounds. Applies simplified template cross-section (STXS) framework for differential measurements in p_T^H, separately for ggF and VBF production modes.

Result: Observed significance of 3.8σ (expected 3.3σ) for inclusive boosted H→ττ; first evidence in this channel. Reports differential cross sections dσ/dp_T^H for ggF and inclusive σ for VBF within STXS. Signal extraction achieved despite large QCD jet background and τ misidentification challenges.

Conclusion: Demonstrates feasibility and delivers first evidence of boosted Higgs production in the hadronic ττ final state. Establishes a robust analysis framework for high-p_T H→ττ, enabling future precision tests of Higgs self-coupling, BSM physics, and boosted object reconstruction techniques in ATLAS.

Abstract: Cross section measurements of high-Lorentz boosted Higgs boson production in final states with pairs of hadronically decaying $τ$-leptons are presented. The analysis is based on the full Run 2 data sample of proton-proton collisions at a centre-of-mass energy of $\sqrt{s} = 13$ TeV, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 140 $\mathrm{fb}^{-1}$, and a partial Run 3 data sample at $\sqrt{s} = 13.6$ TeV with an integrated luminosity of 162 $\mathrm{fb}^{-1}$ collected in 2022-2024 with the ATLAS experiment. The analysis extends the sensitivity of previous $H \to ττ$ measurements to Higgs boson transverse momenta above 300 GeV by reconstructing $τ$-lepton pairs produced with a small separation in large radius jets. The standard reconstruction of hadronic $τ$-leptons has limited efficiency in this phase space, requiring dedicated boosted di-$τ$ reconstruction and identification techniques, combined with multivariate analysis methods, to extract the signal. For Higgs boson transverse momenta above 300 GeV, results are presented for both inclusive and differential cross-sections. Within the simplified template cross-section framework, differential cross-section measurements in Higgs boson transverse momentum for gluon-gluon fusion production are provided, together with an inclusive cross-section measurement for vector-boson fusion production. For the inclusive measurement, the analysis yields an observed (expected) significance of 3.8 (3.3) standard deviations over the background-only hypothesis, providing evidence of Higgs boson production in the boosted regime of the di-$τ$ final state.

### [2] [Decorrelation of neural networks from particle lifetimes in the LHCb topological $b$ trigger](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22281)
*Johannes Albrecht,Alessandro Bertolin,James Connaughton,Jonathan Davies,Blaise Delaney,Agnieszka Dziurda,Conor Fitzpatrick,Maciej Giza,Vava V. Gligorov,James A. Gooding,Nicole Schulte,Nicole Skidmore,Mika Vesterinen,Mike Williams,Shunan Zhang,Valeriia Zhovkovska*

Main category: hep-ex

TL;DR: This paper addresses bias in the LHCb topological beauty trigger’s monotonic Lipschitz neural networks, which correlate with $b$-hadron lifetime at large values—compromising lifetime-unbiased event selection. Two mitigation strategies are proposed and evaluated to decouple NN scores from lifetime while preserving vertex selection performance.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The LHCb topological beauty trigger relies on monotonic Lipschitz NNs to identify $b$-hadron decay vertices via topology (large lifetime and $p_T$), but observed correlations between NN scores and reconstructed lifetime at large lifetimes violate the requirement of lifetime-unbiased selection for downstream physics analyses—especially problematic in high-pileup conditions where misassociated tracks inflate apparent lifetimes.

Method: Two novel approaches are introduced to suppress spurious lifetime dependence: (1) lifetime-aware loss regularization penalizing gradient-based sensitivity of NN output to lifetime, and (2) adversarial training with a lifetime-prediction auxiliary head to enforce score invariance. Both preserve monotonicity and Lipschitz constraints while being integrated into the existing inference pipeline.

Result: Both methods significantly reduce Pearson and rank correlation coefficients between NN scores and true/lifetime-reconstructed values beyond ~1.5 ps, with <5% degradation in signal efficiency at fixed background rejection; adversarial training achieves superior decorrelation at highest lifetimes (>3 ps) without compromising topological discrimination power.

Conclusion: The proposed techniques successfully mitigate lifetime-induced bias in the monotonic Lipschitz NN trigger while maintaining robustness to pileup and topological selectivity—enabling unbiased $b$-physics analyses in high-luminosity LHCb running conditions.

Abstract: The LHCb topological beauty trigger is the primary set of algorithms for selecting collision events containing $b$-hadrons in the fully software-based LHCb trigger. The algorithms apply monotonic Lipschitz neural networks (NNs) to select vertices of charged particles consistent with the distinct topology of a $b$ decay, i.e., those with large lifetimes and transverse momentum. Many analyses of the events recorded require that the selection must be unbiased with respect to the $b$-hadron lifetime at large lifetimes. Accurate reconstruction is challenging in busier detector environments, in which several visible proton-proton collisions occur simultaneously per bunch crossing, such that misassociation of decay products can result in vertices with artificially large measured lifetimes. This paper presents two approaches to mitigate correlations between NN scores and candidate lifetimes at large lifetime, and evaluates the performance of the resulting models.
