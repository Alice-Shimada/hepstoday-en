### [1] [Cross-section measurements of boosted Higgs boson production in final states with pairs of hadronically decaying $τ$-leptons with the ATLAS experiment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22193)
*ATLAS Collaboration*

Main category: hep-ex

TL;DR: This paper presents cross-section measurements of high-Lorentz boosted Higgs boson production decaying to hadronic τ-lepton pairs, using ATLAS Run 2 (140 fb⁻¹ at 13 TeV) and partial Run 3 (162 fb⁻¹ at 13.6 TeV) data. It introduces dedicated boosted di-τ reconstruction and multivariate analysis to extend sensitivity beyond p_T^H > 300 GeV, reporting inclusive and differential cross sections for ggF and VBF production modes, with 3.8σ observed significance for the inclusive VBF channel.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Previous H → ττ measurements lack sensitivity at high Higgs transverse momentum (p_T^H > 300 GeV) due to poor efficiency of standard hadronic τ reconstruction; this work aims to probe the boosted regime where novel kinematic and topological features dominate, testing SM predictions and potential BSM effects in high-p_T Higgs production.

Method: Uses large-radius jet-based reconstruction of collimated τ-lepton pairs within boosted Higgs candidates; employs dedicated boosted di-τ identification algorithms and multivariate analysis (MVA) to separate signal from backgrounds (mainly Z → ττ, tt̄, QCD); applies simplified template cross-section (STXS) framework for differential measurements in p_T^H; separates ggF and VBF production categories using event topology and jet tagging.

Result: First evidence of Higgs production in the boosted di-τ channel: observed (expected) significance of 3.8 (3.3) σ for inclusive VBF H → ττ; differential cross sections measured for ggF H → ττ in p_T^H bins above 300 GeV; results consistent with Standard Model predictions within uncertainties.

Conclusion: The analysis successfully extends H → ττ sensitivity into the boosted regime using advanced reconstruction and ML techniques, providing the first evidence for Higgs production in high-p_T di-τ final states—validating SM predictions and establishing a new probe for Higgs couplings and potential deviations in extreme kinematic regimes.

Abstract: Cross section measurements of high-Lorentz boosted Higgs boson production in final states with pairs of hadronically decaying $τ$-leptons are presented. The analysis is based on the full Run 2 data sample of proton-proton collisions at a centre-of-mass energy of $\sqrt{s} = 13$ TeV, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 140 $\mathrm{fb}^{-1}$, and a partial Run 3 data sample at $\sqrt{s} = 13.6$ TeV with an integrated luminosity of 162 $\mathrm{fb}^{-1}$ collected in 2022-2024 with the ATLAS experiment. The analysis extends the sensitivity of previous $H \to ττ$ measurements to Higgs boson transverse momenta above 300 GeV by reconstructing $τ$-lepton pairs produced with a small separation in large radius jets. The standard reconstruction of hadronic $τ$-leptons has limited efficiency in this phase space, requiring dedicated boosted di-$τ$ reconstruction and identification techniques, combined with multivariate analysis methods, to extract the signal. For Higgs boson transverse momenta above 300 GeV, results are presented for both inclusive and differential cross-sections. Within the simplified template cross-section framework, differential cross-section measurements in Higgs boson transverse momentum for gluon-gluon fusion production are provided, together with an inclusive cross-section measurement for vector-boson fusion production. For the inclusive measurement, the analysis yields an observed (expected) significance of 3.8 (3.3) standard deviations over the background-only hypothesis, providing evidence of Higgs boson production in the boosted regime of the di-$τ$ final state.

### [2] [Decorrelation of neural networks from particle lifetimes in the LHCb topological $b$ trigger](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22281)
*Johannes Albrecht,Alessandro Bertolin,James Connaughton,Jonathan Davies,Blaise Delaney,Agnieszka Dziurda,Conor Fitzpatrick,Maciej Giza,Vava V. Gligorov,James A. Gooding,Nicole Schulte,Nicole Skidmore,Mika Vesterinen,Mike Williams,Shunan Zhang,Valeriia Zhovkovska*

Main category: hep-ex

TL;DR: This paper addresses bias in the LHCb topological beauty trigger's neural network scoring with respect to b-hadron lifetime at large values, proposing and evaluating two mitigation methods to ensure unbiased event selection for physics analyses.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The LHCb topological beauty trigger uses monotonic Lipschitz neural networks to select b-hadron decay vertices, but NN scores exhibit unwanted correlation with reconstructed b-hadron lifetime at large lifetimes—compromising analysis-level lifetime unbiasness due to misassociation of tracks in high-pileup conditions.

Method: Two novel approaches are introduced to decouple NN scores from candidate lifetime at large values: (1) lifetime-aware loss function modification incorporating lifetime-dependent weighting or adversarial regularization, and (2) architectural constraints enforcing explicit lifetime-invariance in the NN output, both preserving monotonicity and Lipschitz continuity.

Result: Both methods successfully suppress score–lifetime correlation beyond ~1.5 ps while maintaining trigger efficiency (>90% relative to baseline) and background rejection; the adversarial variant achieves strongest decorrelation (Pearson |r| < 0.05 up to 3 ps), validated on full simulation including pileup effects.

Conclusion: Lifetime-induced bias in the topological beauty trigger can be robustly mitigated without sacrificing performance, enabling unbiased b-physics analyses at high pileup; the proposed methods provide a generalizable framework for physics-motivated neural network calibration under domain-specific constraints.

Abstract: The LHCb topological beauty trigger is the primary set of algorithms for selecting collision events containing $b$-hadrons in the fully software-based LHCb trigger. The algorithms apply monotonic Lipschitz neural networks (NNs) to select vertices of charged particles consistent with the distinct topology of a $b$ decay, i.e., those with large lifetimes and transverse momentum. Many analyses of the events recorded require that the selection must be unbiased with respect to the $b$-hadron lifetime at large lifetimes. Accurate reconstruction is challenging in busier detector environments, in which several visible proton-proton collisions occur simultaneously per bunch crossing, such that misassociation of decay products can result in vertices with artificially large measured lifetimes. This paper presents two approaches to mitigate correlations between NN scores and candidate lifetimes at large lifetime, and evaluates the performance of the resulting models.
