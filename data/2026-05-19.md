### [1] [Feasibility of Low-Energy True Muonium Photoproduction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16557)
*Ivo Schulthess,Benjamin Banto Oberhauser,Paolo Crivelli*

Main category: hep-ex

TL;DR: This paper proposes a feasibility study for producing true muonium (μ⁺μ⁻ bound state) via near-threshold photoproduction using the Gamma Factory at CERN, demonstrating viable signal-to-background discrimination and outlining future precision QED and BSM physics measurements.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: True muonium is an unobserved, purely leptonic exotic atom whose observation would provide a clean test of bound-state QED and sensitivity to physics beyond the Standard Model.

Method: Feasibility study including Monte Carlo simulations of signal (γ + nucleus → μ⁺μ⁻ + X) and dominant backgrounds, photon flux and energy requirements estimation, gamma-source assessment (focusing on CERN's Gamma Factory), and cut-based background suppression analysis.

Result: The Gamma Factory can deliver sufficient photon energy (~1–2 GeV) and flux (~10¹⁷ γ/s) for observable true muonium production; optimized kinematic cuts reduce backgrounds (e.g., μ⁺μ⁻ from π/κ decays, Drell-Yan) below the signal level.

Conclusion: Near-threshold photoproduction at the Gamma Factory is a viable path to first observation of true muonium; subsequent high-statistics runs would enable precision measurements of its lifetime, hyperfine splitting, and Lamb shift—probing QED and potential new physics.

Abstract: True muonium, the bound state of a muon and an antimuon, is a theoretically well-understood but experimentally unobserved exotic atom. Its purely leptonic nature makes it a sensitive probe for bound-state quantum electrodynamics and possible physics beyond the Standard Model. We present a feasibility study of low-energy true muonium production via near-threshold photoproduction on a fixed target. The study includes simulations of the signal and dominant background processes, estimates of the required photon fluxes, and an overview of possible gamma sources. We show that the proposed Gamma Factory at CERN could provide the necessary photon energies and rates for the production of true muonium. Furthermore, cut-based selections are shown to suppress the background below the expected signal level. Finally, we discuss possible physics opportunities beyond the first observation, including measurements of its lifetime, hyperfine splitting, and Lamb shift.

### [2] [Search for the nonresonant production of a pair of additional Higgs bosons in the Type-X two-Higgs-doublet model in proton-proton collisions at $\sqrt{s}$ = 13 TeV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16655)
*CMS Collaboration*

Main category: hep-ex

TL;DR: A CMS search for two additional Higgs bosons produced via off-shell Z decay and decaying to ττ pairs finds no significant deviation from SM background; it excludes the Type-X 2HDM alignment scenario as a solution to the muon g−2 anomaly.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To test whether the Type-X two-Higgs-doublet model (2HDM) — particularly its alignment limit — can resolve the long-standing discrepancy between the measured and Standard Model predicted values of the muon anomalous magnetic moment (g−2).

Method: Search for resonant production of two additional neutral Higgs bosons (H/A → ττ) in association with an off-shell Z boson (Z* → ℓℓ/νν), using 138 fb⁻¹ of 13 TeV pp collision data collected by the CMS detector; employs τ-lepton pair reconstruction, multivariate analysis, and background estimation via data-driven and simulation-based techniques.

Result: No statistically significant excess observed over SM background expectations; upper limits set on the product of cross section and branching fraction for Z* → H/A + X → ττ + X in the Type-X 2HDM alignment limit.

Conclusion: The Type-X 2HDM alignment scenario is excluded at 95% CL for relevant parameter space (e.g., tan β ≳ 10–50 depending on mass), ruling it out as an explanation for the muon g−2 anomaly.

Abstract: A search is presented for the production of two additional Higgs bosons from an off-shell Z boson, where both additional particles decay to $τ$ lepton pairs. The search is performed with a data sample collected with the CMS detector from proton-proton collisions at the LHC at $\sqrt{s}$ = 13 TeV, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 138 fb$^{-1}$. No deviation from the standard model background is observed. Exclusion limits are set on the Type-X two-Higgs-doublet model alignment scenario. These results rule out this model as an explanation to the potential tension between the experimental and theoretical values of the muon anomalous magnetic moment.

*Guihong Huang*

Main category: hep-ex

TL;DR: This paper investigates how discrepancies in reactor antineutrino spectral predictions—particularly between the Huber-Müller model and Daya Bay–measured spectra—affect global fits of solar neutrino oscillation parameters (Δm²₂₁, θ₁₂), finding that using empirical spectra reduces tension between KamLAND and JUNO results.

Method: Constructs a model-weak global analysis framework combining Daya Bay’s experimentally measured ²³⁵U and ²³⁹Pu fission antineutrino spectra with KamLAND public data; performs two fits: one using the Huber-Müller model (benchmark), and another substituting it with Daya Bay’s measured spectra; compares resulting best-fit Δm²₂₁ and tan²θ₁₂.

Conclusion: Reactor antineutrino flux model dependence—specifically the Huber-Müller prediction—is a non-negligible systematic affecting Δm²₂₁ and θ₁₂ extractions; using empirical fission spectra mitigates the KamLAND–JUNO tension, supporting the need for model-independent or data-driven spectral inputs in precision oscillation analyses.
