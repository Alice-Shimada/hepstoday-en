### [1] [Two Regularized Determinants of Laplacian through Resurgence theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03960)
*Wen Shen,Shanzhong Sun*

Main category: math-ph

TL;DR: This paper applies resurgence theory to regularize the determinant of the Laplacian on Riemann manifolds, deriving closed formulas via Borel–Laplace resummation incorporating singularities of a Theta series related to the square-root Laplacian spectrum; it recovers classical results (Poisson and Selberg trace formulas) and establishes asymptotic (1-Gevrey) behavior and equivalence of derivatives between logarithmic and exponential deformations at vanishing deformation parameter.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To develop rigorous regularization methods for the determinant of the Laplacian—ill-defined due to infinite eigenvalue product—using resurgence theory, bridging spectral geometry, analytic continuation, and asymptotic analysis.

Method: Borel–Laplace resummation applied to formal series arising from the logarithmic derivative and exponential deformation of det(Δ); analysis of the analytic continuation and singularities of the Theta series Θ̂_{D_X}, defined via spectrum of √Δ; comparison with heat kernel trace and use of Poisson and Selberg trace formulas for verification.

Result: Closed-form expressions for both regularized determinants; 1-Gevrey asymptotics for the exponential deformation at infinity with coefficients given by heat kernel traces; equivalence of first derivatives of both regularizations as the deformation parameter → 0.

Conclusion: Resurgence theory provides a unifying framework for Laplacian determinant regularization on Riemann manifolds; the logarithmic and exponentially deformed regularizations are intimately linked through their derivative behavior at zero deformation, reinforcing consistency across spectral and asymptotic perspectives.

Abstract: We study two types of regularizations of the determinant of Laplacian on Riemann manifold from the viewpoint of resurgence theory. One is the formal logarithmic derivative of the determinant, and the other is its exponential deformation. Under appropriate conditions, the close formulas for both regularized determinant are established through Borel-Laplace resummation which takes into account the contribution of the singularities along the analytic continuation of Theta series $\hatΘ_{D_X}$. The series resembles the trace of the heat kernel, but is defined via the spectrum of the square-root of the Laplacian. As applications, we revisit the well known formal logarithmic derivative of determinant on $S^1$ and compact Riemann surface with higher genus ($\geq2$) corresponding to the Poisson summation formula and Selberg trace formula respectively. Furthermore, the 1-Gevrey asymptotic behavior of the exponential deformation regularization at infinity is considered whose coefficients are determined by the trace of the heat kernel. In the end, we establish the relationship between the two regularized determinants. In fact, they have the same derivatives when the deformation parameter tends to $0$ in exponentially deformed regularization.

</details>


### [2] [Pade Approximants for Geodesy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04027)
*Ovidiu Costin,Gerald V. Dunne,Crichton Ogle*

Main category: math-ph

TL;DR: This note investigates Padé approximants for extending spherical harmonic expansions (SHEs) beyond their convergence radius—particularly for downward continuation—and for locating complex singularities of the gravitational potential.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Spherical harmonic expansions (SHEs) converge only outside the Brillouin sphere in general, limiting their use for downward continuation and singularity analysis; improved analytic continuation is needed, especially for synthetic planetary models with analytic topography/density.

Method: Application and analysis of Padé approximants to SHEs—treated as expansions in 1/r about infinity—to analytically continue beyond the standard radius of convergence and detect complex singularities of the gravitational potential.

Result: Padé approximants enable effective downward continuation beyond the Brillouin sphere; convergence region of SHEs can expand for synthetic models with analytic topography and density, and the expansion deviation shrinks as planetary structural complexity increases.

Conclusion: Padé approximants are a viable and powerful tool for analytic continuation of SHEs and singularity detection in gravitational potential modeling, particularly when planetary structure admits analytic representations.

Abstract: In this note we analyze the use of Padé approximants for downward continuation beyond the radius of convergence of spherical harmonic expansions (SHEs), and for identifying the complex singularities of the gravitational potential. SHEs are, in essence, expansions in 1/r, i.e., expansions about the point at infinity. Their domain of convergence is generically the exterior of the Brillouin sphere. However, for synthetic models with analytic topography and density the region of convergence may be larger, with the deviation decreasing as the structural complexity of the planet increases.

</details>
