### [1] [From Modal Memory to Topological Relaxation in a Driven Holographic Superfluid](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01455)
*Yan Peng,Bo-Xuan Ge*

Main category: hep-th

TL;DR: This paper investigates how different driving histories affect the topological relaxation dynamics of a holographic superfluid, finding that late-time behavior—particularly residence time in the penultimate winding sector—is strongly history-dependent and governed by modal imbalance entering the amplification stage.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To understand whether and how the dynamical relaxation of topological defects in holographic superfluids retains memory of prior driving protocols, despite converging to identical final states.

Method: Constructing distinct driving histories that lead to identical present conditions and future protocols in a holographic superfluid model; analyzing nonlinear topological relaxation via holographic duality; quantifying modal imbalance and its correlation with residence times across winding sectors.

Result: Late-time relaxation dynamics—specifically residence time in the penultimate winding sector—varies by ~3.7× across histories; this variation is systematically controlled by modal imbalance at the onset of amplification; histories with matched modal imbalance (but different shapes) yield nearly identical relaxation times; same behavior observed for two independent mode pairs.

Conclusion: Topological relaxation in holographic superfluids exhibits robust history dependence encoded in modal imbalance, indicating non-Markovian memory in the nonlinear dynamics—even when macroscopic endpoints coincide.

Abstract: We study how driving history affects the nonlinear topological relaxation of a holographic superfluid. Different histories are constructed to reach the same present driving conditions and then follow the same future protocol. Although they relax to the same final winding sector, their late-time dynamics remain strongly history-dependent: the residence time of the penultimate winding sector changes by about a factor of \(3.7\). This variation is organised by the modal imbalance carried into the common amplification stage. Histories with different shapes but matched modal imbalance produce nearly identical relaxation times. The same qualitative behaviour is found for a second pair of competing modes.

### [2] [On-shell renormalization of sine-Gordon by the quantum inverse scattering method](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01571)
*Francesco Beccarini,Claudio Conti*

Main category: hep-th

TL;DR: This paper introduces a renormalization scheme for the sine-Gordon model within the quantum inverse scattering method on a lattice, where cut-off dependence is fully absorbed into a single length-rescaling factor $Z_L$, tied to the scaling dimension of the vertex operator; neither field nor coupling $\beta$ is renormalized, and mass renormalization is finite and fixed by breather mass matching; the scheme preserves integrability, yields exact mass ratios directly, and generalizes to ultralocal massive integrable theories.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To formulate a renormalization scheme consistent with integrability—specifically, one that respects the quantum inverse scattering method's structure on the lattice and avoids ad hoc subtractions or running couplings while correctly recovering physical masses and ratios in the continuum limit.

Method: Rescaling the box length $L \to Z_L L$ supplemented by an on-shell condition; using eigenvalues of the monodromy matrix to extract soliton and breather masses; fixing $Z_L$'s exponent via the scaling dimension of the vertex operator; imposing finite mass renormalization by requiring the lightest breather mass matches the linearized boson mass.

Result: A cut-off-independent, integrability-preserving renormalization scheme where only the length scale is multiplicatively renormalized ($L \mapsto Z_L L$); exact mass ratios emerge directly from the regularized lattice construction; $\beta$ and the field remain unrenormalized; the physical scale is encoded entirely in $Z_L$, eliminating need for explicit lattice-to-continuum calibration.

Conclusion: The proposed scheme is a natural reparameterization of the sine-Gordon theory that makes the synergy between integrability and renormalization manifest; it extends beyond sine-Gordon to ultralocal massive integrable models—including potentially asymptotically free ones admitting ultralocal lattice regularization—and provides a viable alternative to Coleman's normal ordering and conformal perturbation theory.

Abstract: In the quantum inverse scattering method, the sine-Gordon model is solved on a lattice, and its continuum and infinite-volume limits become uniform only after the length of the box is rescaled. We show that this rescaling, supplemented by an on-shell condition, defines a renormalization scheme. The whole dependence on the cut-off is absorbed into a factor $Z_L$ multiplying the length of the box, with an exponent fixed by the scaling dimension of the vertex operator; neither the field nor the coupling $β$ is renormalized, no subtraction scale is introduced, and the mass parameter receives a finite renormalization, fixed by requiring that the lightest breather has the mass of the boson in the linearized theory. The soliton and breather masses follow from the eigenvalues of the monodromy operator as functions of parameters that do not run, and reduce to the classical and semiclassical results as $β\rightarrow0$. In particular, the mass ratios are produced directly from the regularized construction, while the relation between the lattice parameters and the physical scale is absorbed into $Z_L$ and never needs to be computed. The scheme is a reparameterization of the theory which makes explicit the interplay between integrability and renormalization. We compare it with Coleman's normal ordering and with conformal perturbation theory, and we argue that it extends to ultralocal integrable theories with a massive spectrum, possibly including asymptotically free models that admit an ultralocal lattice regularization.

Motivation: To determine whether spatial overlap between neighboring subcritical bubbles—typically treated as isolated fluctuations—can modify their thermodynamic stability and dynamical evolution during weak first-order phase transitions.

Method: Combined analytic free-energy calculations for composite Gaussian bubble profiles with Langevin simulations of overlapping subcritical bubble configurations.

Result: Spatial overlap lowers the free-energy cost and generally increases the lifetime of subcritical bubbles; enhanced persistence may feed back on their abundance.

Abstract: Subcritical bubbles can form an appreciable population during weak first-order phase transitions, but are usually treated as isolated fluctuations. This raises the question of whether spatial overlap between neighboring subcritical bubbles can modify their evolution. We address this question by combining analytic free-energy calculations for composite Gaussian profiles with Langevin simulations of overlapping configurations. We find that the overlap lowers the free-energy cost and generally increases the lifetime of subcritical bubbles, with the enhanced persistence potentially feeding back on their abundance. Such overlap can therefore generate collective effects and should be incorporated into kinetic descriptions of subcritical-bubble populations.
