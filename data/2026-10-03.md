### [1] [From Modal Memory to Topological Relaxation in a Driven Holographic Superfluid](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01455)
*Yan Peng,Bo-Xuan Ge*

Main category: hep-th

TL;DR: This paper investigates how different driving histories affect the topological relaxation dynamics of a holographic superfluid, finding that late-time relaxation is strongly history-dependent due to modal imbalance carried into an amplification stage — despite converging to the same final winding sector.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To understand whether and how the dynamical relaxation of topological defects in holographic superfluids depends on prior driving history, beyond initial and boundary conditions.

Method: Constructing distinct driving protocols that converge to identical present conditions and subsequent future protocols; analyzing nonlinear topological relaxation using holographic duality; quantifying modal imbalance and its role in determining residence times in penultimate winding sectors.

Result: Late-time relaxation dynamics (specifically residence time in the penultimate winding sector) varies by ~3.7× across different histories; this variation is governed by modal imbalance — histories with matched imbalance yield nearly identical relaxation times, even with different shapes; similar behavior observed for two distinct mode pairs.

Conclusion: Topological relaxation in holographic superfluids exhibits nontrivial memory of driving history encoded in modal imbalance, indicating that the system's dynamical pathway—not just endpoints—critically determines late-time behavior.

Abstract: We study how driving history affects the nonlinear topological relaxation of a holographic superfluid. Different histories are constructed to reach the same present driving conditions and then follow the same future protocol. Although they relax to the same final winding sector, their late-time dynamics remain strongly history-dependent: the residence time of the penultimate winding sector changes by about a factor of \(3.7\). This variation is organised by the modal imbalance carried into the common amplification stage. Histories with different shapes but matched modal imbalance produce nearly identical relaxation times. The same qualitative behaviour is found for a second pair of competing modes.

### [2] [On-shell renormalization of sine-Gordon by the quantum inverse scattering method](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01571)
*Francesco Beccarini,Claudio Conti*

Main category: hep-th

TL;DR: This paper introduces a renormalization scheme for the sine-Gordon model within the quantum inverse scattering method, where cut-off dependence is fully absorbed into a length-rescaling factor $Z_L$, fixed by the scaling dimension of the vertex operator; no field or coupling renormalization occurs, and mass renormalization is finite and physically constrained—yielding exact soliton/breather masses and mass ratios directly from the lattice construction, without needing explicit lattice-to-continuum calibration.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To reconcile integrability-preserving lattice regularization with consistent renormalization in the sine-Gordon model, avoiding ad hoc subtractions or running couplings while preserving exact solvability and physical mass conditions.

Method: Uses quantum inverse scattering on a lattice, imposes uniform continuum/infinite-volume limits via box-length rescaling $L \to Z_L L$, enforces an on-shell condition to define a renormalization scheme; derives masses from monodromy eigenvalues parameterized by non-running quantities; compares with Coleman's normal ordering and conformal perturbation theory.

Result: A cut-off-independent, integrability-compatible renormalization scheme where $Z_L$ absorbs all UV dependence, $\beta$ and field remain bare, mass receives finite renormalization fixing the lightest breather mass, and exact mass ratios emerge directly; scheme extends to ultralocal massive integrable QFTs, possibly asymptotically free ones with ultralocal lattice regularization.

Conclusion: The proposed scheme is a physically transparent reparameterization that unifies integrability and renormalization—demonstrating that exact spectral data can be obtained without conventional RG flow or scale-dependent couplings, offering a framework for UV-complete constructions of massive integrable QFTs.

Abstract: In the quantum inverse scattering method, the sine-Gordon model is solved on a lattice, and its continuum and infinite-volume limits become uniform only after the length of the box is rescaled. We show that this rescaling, supplemented by an on-shell condition, defines a renormalization scheme. The whole dependence on the cut-off is absorbed into a factor $Z_L$ multiplying the length of the box, with an exponent fixed by the scaling dimension of the vertex operator; neither the field nor the coupling $β$ is renormalized, no subtraction scale is introduced, and the mass parameter receives a finite renormalization, fixed by requiring that the lightest breather has the mass of the boson in the linearized theory. The soliton and breather masses follow from the eigenvalues of the monodromy operator as functions of parameters that do not run, and reduce to the classical and semiclassical results as $β\rightarrow0$. In particular, the mass ratios are produced directly from the regularized construction, while the relation between the lattice parameters and the physical scale is absorbed into $Z_L$ and never needs to be computed. The scheme is a reparameterization of the theory which makes explicit the interplay between integrability and renormalization. We compare it with Coleman's normal ordering and with conformal perturbation theory, and we argue that it extends to ultralocal integrable theories with a massive spectrum, possibly including asymptotically free models that admit an ultralocal lattice regularization.

  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To construct a unified cosmological framework spanning inflation and late-time acceleration without ad hoc transitions or fine-tuned parameters—specifically, avoiding arbitrary Gauss-Bonnet couplings, conventional reheating, and incompatibility of quartic potentials with CMB constraints in cold inflation.

Method: Develops Einstein-Gauss-Bonnet gravity coupled to three scalar fields sharing one potential and one coupling function; allows dynamical Gauss-Bonnet coupling dependent on field content; identifies a two-phase inflationary dynamics ending via geometric stabilization failure of a waterfall field; models post-inflation radiation production via coherent oscillations of the waterfall condensate; implements warm inflation with temperature-dependent dissipation Γφ ∝ T³; computes scalar/tensor power spectra including Gauss-Bonnet corrections; analyzes consistency of spectral index ns and tensor-to-scalar ratio r with Planck data; introduces a late-time mechanism where a third scalar’s release near matter-radiation equality generates large effective coupling from small mass via inverse seesaw logic.

Result: A self-consistent unified model: (i) realizes geometrically terminated two-phase inflation; (ii) eliminates need for separate reheating; (iii) yields ns within Planck 1σ and suppressed r; (iv) renders quartic potential viable under warm inflation with shear-viscosity-regulated fluctuations; (v) constrains model to narrow band in parameter combination (n−1)Q; (vi) produces late-time dark energy from persistent inflaton; (vii) generates large coupling from tiny mass scale via inverse seesaw at late times.

Conclusion: A single theoretical framework—Einstein-Gauss-Bonnet gravity with three scalars, unified potential/coupling, and warm inflation dynamics—successfully describes both early-universe inflation and late-time cosmic acceleration while resolving longstanding issues (reheating, quartic potential exclusion, fine-tuning), with predictions tightly constrained by CMB and internal consistency.

Abstract: This work presents a unified inflationary and late-time cosmology, developed within Einstein-Gauss-Bonnet gravity coupled to three scalar fields, governed by a single potential and coupling function throughout cosmic history. The initial super-inflationary stage ends dynamically when the geometrically induced stabilization of the waterfall field fails, at a field value determined by the combination $(n-1)Q$. Coherent oscillations of the waterfall condensate serve as the initial radiation source, replacing conventional reheating and establishing the thermal bath necessary for the subsequent warm phase. Utilizing a two-stage dissipation mechanism with $Γ_φ\propto T^3$, the scalar and tensor spectra are derived while retaining Gauss-Bonnet contributions, demonstrating that the growth of inflaton fluctuations is regulated by the shear viscosity of the bath, which is essential for model viability. A quartic potential, previously excluded in the cold scenario, becomes viable, with $n_s$ within the Planck $1σ$ band and $r$ suppressed. Agreement with the spectral index is generic, while internal consistency confines the model to a narrow band in $(n-1)Q$. Finally, the release of the third scalar near matter-radiation equality transforms a very small mass scale into a very large coupling by a logic inverse to the seesaw mechanism. At late times, the inflaton persists as dark energy.

Method: The authors promote hidden zeros to exact splitting formulas of the form A = Σ_α R_α J_{α,L} J_{α,R} J_{α,out}, where R_α encodes the zero locus condition and J's are lower-point amplitudes/currents; they apply this to Tr(φ³), NLSM, and cosmological wavefunctions.

Abstract: Hidden zeros impose constraints on scattering amplitudes beyond their familiar factorization properties. We explore their structure in two related directions. We first promote hidden zeros to explicit splitting formulas that organize full amplitudes at generic kinematics. Ordered $\mathrm{Tr}(φ^3)$ amplitudes, nonlinear sigma model (NLSM) amplitudes, and cosmological wavefunctions share the same defining exact splitting structure $\mathcal A=\sum_αR_α\,
  {\mathcal J}_{α,L}{\mathcal J}_{α,R}
  {\mathcal J}_{α,\mathrm{out}}$, adapted to any hidden zero. Each prefactor $R_α$ is a condition of the hidden zero locus, so this decomposition manifests the hidden vanishing term by term. The remaining factors are lower point amplitudes or currents. Using these representations, we can systematically find new zero loci beyond the usual ones. In particular, we obtain five new all-multiplicity zero families. We also find continuous families of zeros: the coefficients of their defining equations can vary continuously while the amplitude remains zero.

Motivation: To determine whether spatial overlap between neighboring subcritical bubbles—typically treated as isolated fluctuations—can modify their thermodynamic stability and dynamical evolution during weak first-order phase transitions.

Method: Combined analytic free-energy calculations for composite Gaussian bubble profiles with Langevin simulations of overlapping subcritical bubble configurations.

Result: Spatial overlap lowers the free-energy cost and generally increases the lifetime of subcritical bubbles; enhanced persistence may feed back on their abundance, enabling collective behavior.

Abstract: Subcritical bubbles can form an appreciable population during weak first-order phase transitions, but are usually treated as isolated fluctuations. We address this question by combining analytic free-energy calculations for composite Gaussian profiles with Langevin simulations of overlapping configurations. We find that the overlap lowers the free-energy cost and generally increases the lifetime of subcritical bubbles, with the enhanced persistence potentially feeding back on their abundance. Such overlap can therefore generate collective effects and should be incorporated into kinetic descriptions of subcritical-bubble populations.
