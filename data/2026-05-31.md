### [1] [Phast: Simultaneous reconstruction of photoelectron count and time profiles from PMT waveforms via machine learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29391)
*Yiming Xu,Youwen Fan,Siyu Chen,Hongyue Duyang,Teng Li,Yaoguang Wang*

Main category: hep-ex

TL;DR: Phast is a machine-learning method using a wave-transformer encoder and dual-branch decoder (counting + count-conditioned time query decoder) for simultaneous, accurate photoelectron count and timing reconstruction from noisy, piled-up PMT waveforms.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Accurate reconstruction of photoelectron (PE) count and arrival time from PMT waveforms is essential for particle/nuclear physics experiments, but is severely challenged by electronic effects like pileup, charge fluctuations, and noise.

Method: Phast employs a shared wave-transformer encoder followed by two specialized branches: (1) a counting branch predicting total PE number, and (2) a time branch using a count-conditioned query decoder with dynamic query activation to reconstruct the PE time profile.

Result: Phast achieves stable and accurate simultaneous reconstruction of PE count and time across synthetic Monte Carlo datasets—including uniform and mixed fast/slow double-component waveforms—demonstrating high consistency under realistic electronic distortions.

Conclusion: Combining convolutional-style wave-feature extraction (via wave-transformer) with query-based transformer decoders is an effective architectural paradigm for robust, high-fidelity PMT waveform reconstruction in complex detector environments.

Abstract: Photomultiplier tubes (PMTs) are widely used in particle and nuclear physics experiments. The reconstruction of PMT waveforms is a fundamental task in these experiments, where accurate extraction of photoelectron (PE) multiplicities and time from the waveform is required for downstream event reconstruction and analysis. In realistic detector environments, PMT waveform reconstruction is complicated by electronic effects such as pileup, charge fluctuations, noise etc., which make precise recovery of physical observables challenging. To address these challenges, we present \phast{}, a machine-learning-based method that reconstructs PE count and time profile simultaneously. The model consists of a shared wave-transformer encoder followed by two dedicated branches: a counting branch for the total PE number prediction, and a time branch employing a count-conditioned query decoder with dynamic query activation. To study the reconstruction performance under controlled conditions, we construct several toy Monte Carlo PMT waveform datasets, including both uniform and mixed fast-slow double-temporal-components configurations. The proposed method demonstrates stable and accurate reconstruction performance across various waveform conditions, achieving high consistency in both PE counting and time reconstruction. These results indicate that architectures combining convolutional feature extraction with query-based transformer decoders provide an effective approach for complex PMT waveform reconstruction tasks.

</details>

Main category: hep-ex

TL;DR: This paper reports a search for multistrange dibaryon states (Ξ⁰p, Ω⁻p, Ω⁻n) in Υ(1S) and Υ(2S) decays using Belle data; no significant signals were found, and 90% CL upper limits on branching fractions are set at O(10⁻⁷–10⁻⁶).


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To probe poorly constrained multistrange baryon-baryon interactions—especially attractive ΞN and ΩN forces—relevant to neutron-star matter equation of state.

Method: Analysis of 102M Υ(1S) and 158M Υ(2S) decays collected with the Belle detector at KEKB; invariant mass spectra near baryon-pair thresholds were examined for resonant dibaryon signals.

Result: No statistically significant signals observed for Ξ⁰p, Ω⁻p, or Ω⁻n dibaryons; first 90% CL upper limits on corresponding Υ → dibaryon branching fractions established: ~1–5 × 10⁻⁷ for Ξ⁰p, ~1–3 × 10⁻⁶ for Ω⁻p/Ω⁻n, depending on assumed binding energy.

Conclusion: Current data do not support existence of weakly bound Ξ⁰p, Ω⁻p, or Ω⁻n dibaryons near threshold; limits constrain theoretical models of multistrange baryonic interactions and their role in dense matter.

Abstract: We search for $Ξ^0p$, $Ω^-p$, and $Ω^-n$ dibaryon states in $Υ(1S)$ and $Υ(2S)$ decays, probing mass regions near the corresponding baryon-pair thresholds. Multistrange baryon-baryon interactions are relevant to neutron-star matter but remain largely unconstrained. Experimental and theoretical studies supporting attractive $ΞN$ and $ΩN$ interactions, where $N$ denotes a nucleon, motivate searches for weakly bound states. We use samples of $102$ million $Υ(1S)$ and $158$ million $Υ(2S)$ decays collected with the Belle detector at the KEKB asymmetric-energy $e^+e^-$ collider. No significant signals are observed, and the first $90$% confidence-level upper limits are set on the branching fractions of $Υ(1S)$ and $Υ(2S)$ decays to $Ξ^0p$, $Ω^-p$, and $Ω^-n$ dibaryon states, at the level of $O(10^{-7})$-$O(10^{-6})$, depending on the channel and the assumed mass difference from the corresponding baryon-pair threshold.
