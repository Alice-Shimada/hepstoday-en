### [1] [The Landau-Dirac operator with shell interactions: self-adjointness and clustering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20665)

<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To understand how singular perturbations (electrostatic + Lorentz-scalar delta interactions) supported on a smooth closed curve affect the spectral structure — especially discrete eigenvalue accumulation — of the two-dimensional Dirac operator under a constant magnetic field, distinguishing non-critical vs. critical coupling regimes and connecting to physically relevant boundary conditions (e.g., infinite-mass).

Method: Functional-analytic approach combining self-adjoint extension theory for symmetric operators, spectral analysis of perturbed Dirac operators, pseudodifferential calculus applied to boundary integral operators (e.g., single- and double-layer potentials), and capacity-theoretic estimates (logarithmic capacity of the curve); analysis separates non-critical (defect indices preserved) and critical (resonant) coupling regimes.

Result: 1) Self-adjoint realizations exist in both non-critical and critical cases; 2) In the non-critical case, essential spectrum = Landau-Dirac levels (unchanged), discrete eigenvalues accumulate at each level at rate determined by the logarithmic capacity of the curve; 3) In the critical case, an additional interval of essential spectrum appears in the gap containing zero; 4) Accumulation occurs from above or below depending on whether coupling is sub- or super-critical — a novel sign flip; 5) Exterior boundary value problems with confining couplings yield eigenvalue clusters, recovering infinite-mass boundary condition as limit.

Conclusion: The interplay between geometry (curve’s logarithmic capacity), coupling strength (criticality), and relativistic quantum mechanics (Dirac operator with magnetic field) leads to rich spectral phenomena: robust Landau-level structure under mild perturbations, emergence of new essential spectrum at criticality, and directional eigenvalue accumulation governed by geometric capacity — with implications for graphene-like systems and quantum confinement models.

Self-adjointness is studied in the so called non critical and critical cases. Our main result concerns the discrete spectrum in the non-critical case: using the pseudodifferential properties of the involved boundary integral operators, we show that the eigenvalues accumulate at each Landau-Dirac level at a rate governed by the logarithmic capacity of the curve. ### [2] [On quantum channels: extreme points, topology, and categorical properties](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20689)
*J. M. S. T. da Silva*

Main category: math-ph

TL;DR: This thesis mathematically analyzes quantum channels—specifically CPTP and UCPTP (unital) maps—focusing on extremality, tensor products, categorical structure (products/coproducts), and topology of extreme-point closures; it constructs extreme maps of various ranks, disproves general extremality preservation under tensoring for UCPTP maps, shows binary products exist only trivially, derives necessary dimensions for coproducts, and obtains a partial CW-like decomposition resembling complex projective space.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To rigorously understand the geometric, topological, and categorical structure of quantum channels—particularly the convex sets of CPTP and UCPTP maps—with emphasis on extremal elements and their behavior under fundamental operations like tensoring and categorical composition.

Method: Convex geometry (extreme point characterization), functional analysis (CPTP/UCPTP map construction), category theory (semicartesian structure, existence proofs for products/coproducts), and algebraic topology (CW decompositions, topological invariants, closure analysis of extreme-point sets).

Result: 1) Explicit constructions of extreme CPTP maps of all ranks and extreme UCPTP maps of rank 2 in all dimensions, plus many rank ≥3 cases; 2) Tensor product of extreme CPTP maps remains extreme, but not for UCPTP (counterexamples provided); 3) Binary categorical products exist only trivially; coproduct dimensions are constrained if they exist; 4) Topological analysis reveals the closure of extreme CPTP maps admits a partial CW-like decomposition analogous to ℂℙⁿ.

Conclusion: The convex sets of CPTP and UCPTP maps exhibit rich yet highly constrained structure: extremality is broadly constructible but not tensor-stable for unital maps; their categorical semantics are degenerate beyond trivial cases; and their topological geometry—while intricate—admits projective-space-inspired decompositions, suggesting deeper links between quantum channel geometry and classical algebraic topology.

Abstract: In this thesis, quantum channels are studied from the point of view of Mathematics. We studied the CPTP (completely positive trace preserving) and UCPTP channels, the latter being the unital channels. In both cases, the sets are compact convex, so they are the closure of their extreme points. We constructed extreme CPTP maps for every possible rank. We also constructed extreme UCPTP maps with rank 2 for each possible dimension. For ranks of at least 3, we constructed extreme UCPTP maps for many dimensions and ranks. It is known that the category of CPTP maps is semicartesian. We add to this a classification of binary products. We proved that products only exist in trivial cases. For binary coproducts, we obtained the dimensions they should have, if they exist. In both cases, topological techniques were used. For this reason, we also investigated the topology of the closure of the set of extreme points of the CPTP maps. Topological invariants can be computed from decompositions of a space such as CW structures. These are a type of decomposition in which the space is expressed as a gluing of balls. In the search for a CW structure for the closure of the set of extreme CPTP maps, we obtained a partial decomposition that resembles a CW structure of the complex projective space.

Motivation: To probe the properties and hadronic decay dynamics of higher charmonium states—particularly the radially excited η_c(2S) and P-wave χ_cJ states—via their decays to baryon-antibaryon + meson final states, which are sensitive to QCD multipole expansion, final-state interactions, and possible exotic contributions.
