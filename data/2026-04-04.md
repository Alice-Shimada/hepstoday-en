### [1] [Axial gravitational perturbations and echo-like signals of a hairy black hole from gravitational decoupling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02164)
*Yi Yang,Ali Ovgun,Gaetano Lambiase,Dong Liu,Zheng-Wen Long*

Main category: gr-qc

TL;DR: This paper investigates axial gravitational perturbations of a hairy black hole obtained via gravitational decoupling, showing that echo-like late-time signals emerge dynamically from a double-peaked effective potential—without ad hoc near-horizon reflectivity—and clarifies that echo-supporting parameter regions need not satisfy the weak energy condition globally.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To understand the geometric origin of echo-like late-time signals in hairy black hole spacetimes and assess their viability as observational probes of black hole hair beyond the no-hair theorem.

Method: Derivation of the odd-parity master equation and effective potential for axial perturbations; computation of quasinormal modes using both frequency-domain (e.g., continued fraction) and time-domain (e.g., evolution of wave packets) methods; analysis of potential morphology, trapping cavity formation, and energy condition compliance.

Result: A double-peaked structure in the axial effective potential arises in a specific parameter regime, enabling quasi-bound states and producing echo-like late-time waveforms; echoes occur dynamically from geometry—not imposed boundary conditions—and the echo-supporting region does not necessarily satisfy the weak energy condition everywhere outside the horizon.

Conclusion: Echoes in this hairy black hole model are intrinsically geometric and provide a physically motivated, testable deviation from Kerr predictions; they offer a framework for constraining black hole hair with gravitational-wave ringdown observations, contingent on careful interpretation of energy condition violations.

Abstract: We study axial gravitational perturbations of a hairy black hole constructed in the framework of gravitational decoupling and investigate the geometric origin of echo-like late-time signals in this spacetime. We derive the odd-parity master equation and the corresponding effective potential, and we compute the quasinormal-mode spectrum by using frequency-domain and time-domain methods. We show that, in a suitable region of parameter space, the axial potential develops a double-peak structure that supports a trapping cavity and gives rise to echo-like late-time waveforms. Rather than imposing near-horizon reflectivity by hand, the delayed pulses therefore arise dynamically from the geometry of the effective potential. We also clarify that the parameter region exhibiting echoes need not coincide with the region in which the weak energy condition is satisfied everywhere outside the event horizon, and this distinction must be taken into account when interpreting the physical status of the solution. Our results provide a useful framework for probing black-hole hair through gravitational-wave ringdown and for exploring possible observational departures from the standard no-hair paradigm.

### [2] [Quadratic gravity corrections to scalar QNMs of rapidly rotating black holes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02214)
*Stef J. B. Husken,Tom van der Steen,Simon Maenaut,Kelvin Ka-Ho Lam,Maxim D. Jockwer,Adrian Ka-Wai Chung,Thomas Hertog,Tjonnie G. F. Li,Nicolás Yunes*

Main category: gr-qc

TL;DR: This paper computes leading-order deviations from general relativity in scalar quasinormal mode spectra of rapidly rotating black holes in scalar Gauss-Bonnet and dynamical Chern-Simons gravity, using numerical high-spin black-hole solutions and pseudo-spectral methods—achieving high accuracy up to a/M = 0.99.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Previous calculations of quasinormal mode corrections in quadratic-curvature, scalar-tensor gravity were limited to moderate black-hole spins due to reliance on spin expansions; the motivation is to extend these calculations to near-extremal spins using newly available numerical solutions.

Method: Uses recently constructed numerical rotating black-hole solutions valid for large spin (up to a/M = 0.99) in scalar Gauss-Bonnet and dynamical Chern-Simons gravity, solves linearized scalar perturbation equations via pseudo-spectral collocation methods.

Result: Obtains quasinormal-mode corrections with accuracy ≲ 10⁻³ for l=m=0 mode and ≲ 10⁻⁶ for higher multipoles; finds that for a/M > 0.9, certain mode corrections increase by orders of magnitude.

Conclusion: High-spin effects dramatically enhance deviations in scalar quasinormal spectra in quadratic-curvature scalar-tensor theories, implying that near-extremal black holes offer heightened sensitivity to beyond-GR physics in gravitational-wave tests.

Abstract: In an effective-field-theory framework for gravity, black-hole quasinormal mode spectra acquire corrections in quadratic-curvature, scalar-tensor extensions of general relativity. Previous calculations of such corrections were limited to moderate spins, since the corresponding background solutions relied on expansions in the spin parameter. Using recently constructed numerical black-hole solutions valid for large spin, we compute the leading-order deviations from general relativity in the scalar quasinormal mode spectrum of rotating black holes in scalar Gauss-Bonnet and dynamical Chern-Simons gravity. We solve the resulting perturbation equations with pseudo-spectral collocation methods, allowing us to determine the quasinormal-mode corrections for dimensionless spins up to $a/M=0.99$, with accuracy better than $\lesssim 10^{-3}$ for the $l=m=0$ mode and $\lesssim 10^{-6}$ for higher multipoles. For spins $a/M>0.9$, the corrections to certain modes can increase by orders of magnitude.

### [3] [Fluid perturbations from expanding bubbles in first-order phase transitions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02240)
*Chiara Caprini,Antonino S. Midiri,Simona Procacci,Alberto Roper Pol*

Main category: gr-qc

TL;DR: This paper refines the velocity power spectrum template for first-order phase transitions in the radiation-dominated era, identifying discontinuity positions—not bubble size or sound shell thickness—as the origin of spectral breaks; it clarifies scale-dependent asymptotics, analyzes wall-velocity and nucleation-time distribution dependencies, and integrates results into the public CosmoGW package.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To improve gravitational wave predictions from cosmological first-order phase transitions by accurately modeling the velocity field power spectrum at the onset of the sound-wave regime—specifically addressing inconsistencies in prior spectral break interpretations and their dependence on bubble dynamics and nucleation statistics.

Method: Analytical derivation of the velocity power spectrum using single-bubble profiles and Fourier analysis; distinction between large-scale (integral-dominated) and small-scale (discontinuity-dominated) asymptotics; parametric study of wall velocity (especially near Chapman-Jouget speed), bubble nucleation timing (exponential vs. simultaneous), and spectral break locations; implementation and validation within the CosmoGW Python package.

Result: The velocity power spectrum breaks are determined by velocity profile discontinuities—not bubble radius or sound shell thickness—leading to a more pronounced intermediate slope and wider separation of breaks for supersonic deflagrations near the Chapman-Jouget speed; large-scale spectrum follows k^{-2} scaling from integrated profiles, small-scale follows k^{-1} from discontinuities; nucleation distribution strongly affects break positions and slope.

Conclusion: A physically grounded, refined velocity spectrum template is established, resolving prior ambiguities in spectral feature origins; this enables more accurate computation of anisotropic stresses and gravitational wave spectra, especially for non-standard bubble wall velocities and nucleation histories, and is now publicly available in CosmoGW.

Abstract: We study the power spectrum of the velocity field induced during a first-order phase transition occurring in the radiation-dominated era. We focus on the phase of bubble expansion, assuming that it ends with the onset of the sound-wave regime. The main result we present is a refined template for the velocity spectrum at the beginning of the sound-wave phase, which can be used for studying the resulting anisotropic stresses and gravitational wave production. In particular, we find that the breaks in the velocity spectrum are not associated to the bubble size and the sound shell thickness, as previously proposed, but to the position of the discontinuities. This distinction is particularly relevant for supersonic deflagrations, as it implies that the intermediate slope is more pronounced and the two breaks are more separated when the wall velocity approaches the Chapman-Jouget speed, instead of the sound speed. We also show that the asymptotic branches of the velocity power spectrum are determined by the integral over the single-bubble profiles at large scales, and by the discontinuities of the velocity profiles at small scales. Furthermore, we study the dependence of the two breaks and the intermediate slope on the distribution function of the times of bubble nucleation (exponential and simultaneous). All the results presented in this work have been included in the public Python package CosmoGW.

</details>
