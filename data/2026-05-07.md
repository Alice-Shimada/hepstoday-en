Motivation: To probe QCD dynamics and baryon production mechanisms in heavy-baryon decays, and test theoretical models (e.g., factorization, diquark, or final-state interaction models) via a pure baryonic, charmless decay forbidden at tree level in the Standard Model.

Method: Analysis of 6.0 fb⁻¹ of pp collision data at √s = 13 TeV collected by LHCb; signal extraction via unbinned maximum-likelihood fit to invariant mass distributions; suppression of charmonium backgrounds (e.g., J/ψ, ψ(2S)) using m(p p̄) < 2.85 GeV and m(K⁺K⁻) < 2.85 GeV vetoes; normalization to the topologically similar control channel Λ⁰_b → Λ K⁺K⁻.

Result: Observation of Λ⁰_b → Λ p p̄ at 5.1σ significance; first measurement of its relative branching fraction: (5.1 ± 1.3_stat ± 0.3_syst) × 10⁻².

Conclusion: Confirms nonperturbative QCD contributions dominate this penguin- and annihilation-dominated decay; provides a benchmark for theoretical predictions of baryonic B decays and tests of CP violation potential in future analyses.

Abstract: A search for the charmless purely baryonic decay $\mathinner{\mathitΛ^0_b\!\to \mathitΛ p \overline{p}}$ is performed using proton-proton collision data recorded by the LHCb experiment at a centre-of-mass energy of $\sqrt{s}=13\,\text{TeV}$ and corresponding to an integrated luminosity of $6.0\,\text{fb}^{-1}$. The signal decay is observed with a significance of 5.1 standard deviations. Its branching fraction is measured for the first time, relative to that of the topologically similar decay $\mathinner{\mathitΛ^0_b\!\to \mathitΛ K^+ K^-}$. Contributions from intermediate charmonium resonances decaying to the $p \overline{p}$ and $K^+ K^-$ final states are explicitly excluded with a requirement on the invariant mass of the companion hadron system, $m(h\bar{h}) < 2.85\,\text{GeV}$, where $h$ stands for a proton or a charged kaon. ### [53] [Quantum Dynamics: A Dilation-Based Approach](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04096)
*Caleb A. Mickelson*

Main category: math-ph

TL;DR: This thesis explores dilation-based modeling of finite-dimensional open quantum systems, representing reduced dynamics via unitary evolution on an extended system (system + ancilla), using Stinespring dilations of channel-valued dynamical curves; it establishes exact dilation results for analytic curves, analyzes singular behavior at t=0, and proves approximate finite-dimensional dilations for Lipschitz-continuous curves.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To provide a mathematically rigorous alternative to the standard reduced-dynamics framework by adopting a dilation-based viewpoint—emphasizing unitary embeddings into larger Hilbert spaces—as a means to reformulate and potentially gain new insight into problems in open quantum systems theory.

Method: Formulates quantum dynamics as channel-valued dynamical curves; applies Stinespring dilation theorem to such curves; derives exact dilations for analytic curves using complex-analytic techniques; analyzes differentiability and singularity structure at t=0; constructs explicit approximate dilations for Lipschitz-continuous curves via piecewise constant approximations and finite-dimensional ancilla extensions.

Result: Exact finite-dimensional Stinespring dilations are obtained for analytic quantum dynamical curves; singular behavior (e.g., non-differentiability or unbounded generator) at t=0 is characterized; Lipschitz-continuous dynamical curves are shown to admit arbitrarily accurate finite-dimensional Stinespring approximations with bounded ancilla dimension depending on Lipschitz constant and time interval.

Conclusion: Dilation-based modeling offers a viable and insightful alternative perspective on open quantum dynamics in finite dimensions; exact dilations require analyticity, but robust approximation results extend applicability to broader classes of physically relevant dynamics, supporting the utility of this approach for theoretical formulation and analysis.

Abstract: In the study of open quantum systems, one commonly describes the evolution of a system of interest through reduced dynamics, obtained by treating the environment indirectly rather than as a part of the full model. This thesis presents an expository account of an alternative, dilation-based viewpoint in the finite-dimensional setting, where a family of reduced dynamics is represented through unitary evolution on a larger system consisting of the original system together with an ancillary environment. After reviewing the reduced-dynamics perspective and the language of quantum channels, we formulate finite-dimensional quantum dynamics as channel-valued dynamical curves and use this framework to discuss Stinespring dilations of such curves. We then present exact dilation results for analytic dynamical curves, explain the singular behavior that can arise at t=0, and describe approximation results showing that Lipschitz-continuous dynamical curves admit approximate finite-dimensional Stinespring dilations. The thesis therefore provides a mathematically focused introduction to dilation-based modeling of quantum dynamics and argues that a change of perspective can lead to new ways of formulating problems in the theory of open quantum systems.

### [54] [Anchored random clusters and SLE excursions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04395)
*Federico Camia,Valentino F. Foit,Rongvoram Nivesvivat*

Main category: math-ph

TL;DR: This paper offers a pedagogical review of conformal field theory (CFT) methods to compute SLE observables in the upper half-plane, deriving known results (e.g., Schramm’s left-passage probability, SLE Green’s functions, anchored percolation densities) and new results for pivotal point densities in critical FK clusters.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To provide a unified, accessible CFT-based framework for computing geometric SLE observables—especially those expressible as bulk-boundary correlation functions involving degenerate boundary operators—and to extend it to new quantities like pivotal point densities in FK clusters.

Method: Uses boundary CFT techniques: maps SLE observables to bulk-boundary correlation functions with degenerate boundary operators; exploits null-state conditions to derive and solve BPZ-type differential equations in the upper half-plane.

Result: Recovers Schramm's left-passage probability, SLE Green's functions, and Kleban–Simmons–Ziff anchored percolation densities; derives novel closed-form expressions for the densities of pivotal points separating critical Fortuin-Kasteleyn clusters.

Conclusion: CFT provides a powerful, systematic, and computationally effective approach to SLE observables, unifying prior results and enabling new predictions—particularly for geometric and connectivity-related quantities in critical statistical models.

Abstract: We provide a pedagogical review of CFT techniques to compute certain Schramm-Loewner Evolution (SLE) observables in the upper half-plane. The approach relies on the ability to express the observables as bulk-boundary correlation functions that involve degenerate boundary operators and, therefore, obey certain differential equations. In particular, we recover Schramm's left-passage probability for SLE, the SLE Green's functions, and the generalized densities of ``anchored'' critical percolation clusters first obtained by Kleban, Simmons, and Ziff. We also obtain new formulas corresponding to the densities of pivotal points between critical Fortuin-Kasteleyn (FK) clusters.

### [55] [Time-Dependent Dynamical Dimensional Transmutation in the $SU(2)$ Gross-Neveu Model with Time-Dependent Interaction Strength](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05111)
*Parameshwar R. Pasnoori*

Main category: math-ph

TL;DR: This paper establishes integrability of a time-dependent SU(2) Gross-Neveu model by mapping its time evolution to the renormalization group (RG) flow of the static model—identifying physical time t with ln Λ—and demonstrates dynamical dimensional transmutation and asymptotic freedom leading to the SU(2)₁ WZNW UV fixed point.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To extend integrability and RG concepts to time-dependent quantum field theories, and to uncover a precise dynamical realization of RG flow via time evolution in a solvable fermionic model.

Method: Applies the generalized Bethe ansatz to the time-dependent SU(2) Gross-Neveu model; imposes integrability condition by matching g(t) to the RG beta-function trajectory of the static model; analyzes exact time-dependent wavefunctions, identifies adiabatic vs. fast-drive regimes, and compares mass gap scaling and UV asymptotics with known fixed points.

Result: 1) Integrability is achieved iff g(t) follows the static model’s RG flow: dg/dt = β(g); 2) In the adiabatic regime (t ∼ t₀), a time-dependent mass gap emerges: m(Δt) = m₀ e⁻ᵖⁱᵃ⁰Δᵗ, mirroring dimensional transmutation; 3) For t ≫ t₀ or αt ≫ α₀t₀, the system flows to the SU(2)₁ WZNW CFT—the UV fixed point—establishing asymptotic freedom.

Conclusion: Time evolution in the driven SU(2) Gross-Neveu model is an exact, solvable realization of Wilsonian RG flow: physical time t parametrizes the RG scale ln Λ, and universal IR/UV behavior maps directly onto dynamical adiabatic/fast regimes.

Abstract: In this work we consider the time-dependent $SU(2)$ Gross-Neveu model, which is a quantum field theory of fermions which interact with each other through spin exchange interaction with time-dependent coupling strength $g(t)$. Using the recently formulated generalized Bethe ansatz framework, we show that the system is integrable provided the time-dependent coupling strength is such that its trajectories in time are exactly same as that of the renormalization group (RG) flow equations corresponding to the static model, where time `$t$' of the time-dependent model is identified with the logarithm of the cutoff `$\ln Λ$' of the static model. In the scaling regime $Λ\rightarrow\infty$, the above relation between time and the logarithm of the cutoff provides a characteristic time scale $t_0$. We analyze the exact time-dependent wavefunction in the case of coupling strength decreasing with time and show that in the adiabatic regime, which corresponds to $t\sim t_0$ for drive rate $α_0=1$, the system exhibits a time-dependent dynamical dimensional transmutation where a time dependent mass gap is generated, which at time $t=t_0+Δt$ is given by $m(Δt)=m_0 e^{-πα_0Δt}$, where $m_0=Λe^{-πα_0 t_0}$. Comparing this with the mass gap of the static model, we identify the adiabatic regime of the time-dependent model with the scaling regime of the static model. In the case of very large time scales $t\gg t_0$ for drive rate $α_0$ or for very fast drive rates $α$ such that $αt \gg α_0t_0$, for any $t<L$, we argue that the system is asymptotically free and approaches the $SU(2)_1$ Wess-Zumino-Novikov-Witten (WZNW) model, which corresponds to the UV fixed point of the $SU(2)$ Gross-Neveu model. Hence we establish that progression of time in the time-dependent model is equivalent to RG flow in the corresponding static model.

</details>
