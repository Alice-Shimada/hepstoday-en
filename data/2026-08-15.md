### [1] [Effects of Quantum Spin-Connection Foam in the Solar System, Galaxies, and the Universe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12404)
*Igor V. Kanatchikov,Valery A. Kholodnyi*

Main category: gr-qc

TL;DR: This paper proposes that quantum spin-connection foam—arising from precanonical quantization of GR—explains both the cosmological constant and MOND-like dynamics via a fundamental acceleration scale a_* = 8πGℏκ, linking κ to the hadronic mass gap; it derives a modified Newtonian potential, recovers Milgromian MOND with a distinct interpolating function, and suggests testable implications in Solar System and lab settings.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To reconcile quantum gravity effects (via precanonical quantization of GR) with observed astrophysical anomalies—the small cosmological constant and flat galactic rotation curves—without dark matter, by identifying a quantum-originated fundamental acceleration scale.

Method: Uses precanonical quantization of General Relativity to model quantum gravity as spin-connection foam; derives a modified Newtonian potential incorporating a fundamental acceleration scale a_* = 8πGℏκ; connects κ to the pure Yang-Mills mass gap; performs nonrelativistic mean-field approximation to recover MOND phenomenology with a theoretically determined interpolating function.

Result: Derives a quantum-gravity-motivated modification of Newtonian dynamics yielding flat rotation curves; obtains a_* ≈ 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s² consistent with MOND’s empirical a₀; predicts Λ ∼ a_*² matching observed cosmological constant; identifies testable signatures in Solar System ephemerides and precision laboratory experiments.

Conclusion: Quantum spin-connection foam provides a unified, first-principles explanation for both Λ and MOND-like dynamics, bridging quantum gravity, particle physics (Yang-Mills mass gap), and galactic-scale observations—suggesting MOND may be an effective manifestation of quantum gravitational structure rather than a phenomenological modification of gravity.

Abstract: We argue that effects of the quantum spin-connection foam, which describes quantum gravity according to the precanonical quantization of General Relativity, may already be observed in the form of the small cosmological constant and a modification of Newtonian dynamics at small accelerations, manifested in the flat rotation curves of galaxies. We obtain a modification of the Newtonian potential that takes into account the existence of a fundamental small acceleration scale, $a_* = 8πG\hbar\varkappa$, where $\varkappa$ is a parameter with the dimensions of inverse spatial volume that appears on dimensional grounds. The connection between $\varkappa$ and the hadronic scale of the mass gap in the pure Yang-Mills sector of the Standard Model leads to an estimated value of $a_*$ compatible with the Milgromian acceleration scale in MOND. The connection between $a_*^2$ and the cosmological constant leads to a realistic value of the latter. Milgromian MOND, together with a theoretically distinct interpolating function, is derived under the assumption that classical dynamics is modified by the mean-field acceleration calculated from the simplest solution of precanonical quantum gravity in the nonrelativistic approximation. We also indicate that the effects of Newtonian dynamics modified by the spin-connection foam may be observable in the Solar System and even in laboratory experiments.

### [2] [Dynamical uncertainty geometry for nonlinear transport](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12521)
*Giacomo Tommei*

Main category: math-ph

TL;DR: DUG is a framework that disentangles posterior uncertainty, future observations, and model-induced transport in nonlinear dynamical systems by indexing credible sets and transport-conditioned subsets via a common mass coordinate; it provides stability guarantees for mass-indexed persistence modules under density and measure perturbations, and demonstrates utility in orbit determination and three-body problem settings.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To address the conflation of posterior uncertainty, future observations, and model-induced transport in nonlinear dynamical systems—where transport structures organizing uncertainty are not evident from posterior geometry alone.

Method: Introduces Dynamical Uncertainty Geometry (DUG), integrating: (i) a mass-ranked credible filtration, (ii) Fisher-geometric characterization of future experiments via induced probability laws, and (iii) a labelled transport skeleton encoding dynamically distinct outcomes; establishes stability of mass-indexed persistence modules under simultaneous perturbations of posterior density and probability measure (continuum, refinement, atomic cases).

Result: Quantitative stability bounds for persistence modules under numerical approximations and weighted grids; empirical validation on two benchmarks: (1) short-arc orbit determination—revealing dynamically distinct return classes within a connected credible region and exposing divergence between information-based vs. local Fisher observation design; (2) Earth-Moon PCR3BP—identifying coexisting first-hit transport outcomes within one credible region and providing resolution diagnostics.

Conclusion: DUG enables physically interpretable, multi-scale tracking of uncertainty classes across credibility levels—not just at fixed thresholds—and shows that coupling topological summaries of posterior geometry with transport labels and experimental design yields a more complete uncertainty characterization than posterior probabilities or dynamical classifications in isolation.

Abstract: Uncertainty in nonlinear dynamical systems is often organized by transport structures that are not apparent from posterior geometry alone. We introduce Dynamical Uncertainty Geometry (DUG), a framework that separates three ingredients that are frequently conflated: posterior uncertainty, future observations, and model-induced transport. DUG indexes posterior credible sets and transport-conditioned subsets by a common enclosed-probability coordinate, allowing physically meaningful classes to be tracked across credibility levels rather than examined at a single threshold. The framework combines a mass-ranked credible filtration, a description of future experiments through their induced probability laws and Fisher geometry, and a labelled transport skeleton representing dynamically distinct outcomes. We establish stability results for the resulting mass-indexed persistence modules under simultaneous perturbations of posterior density and probability measure, including continuum, refinement, and finite atomic formulations. These results provide quantitative control of persistence computed from numerical approximations and weighted grids. Two benchmark problems illustrate the framework. In a short-arc orbit-determination setting, DUG identifies dynamically distinct return classes within a connected uncertainty region and highlights a difference between information-based and local Fisher-based observation-design criteria. In the Earth-Moon planar circular restricted three-body problem, DUG reveals multiple first-hit transport outcomes coexisting within a connected credible region and provides diagnostics for assessing their numerical resolution. Together, these examples show how topological summaries of posterior geometry, when coupled to transport labels and future experiments, yield a richer description of uncertainty than either posterior probabilities or dynamical classifications alone.

### [5] [Edge physics and the Casimir interaction in Maxwell--Chern--Simons theory on a strip](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12622)

<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>

Conclusion: Boundary-locality and gauge symmetry fully constrain the allowed boundary dynamics in Maxwell-Chern-Simons theory on a strip; the scattering determinant representation cleanly separates topological boundary data (anomaly inflow, current algebra levels) from bulk dynamics (dispersion, mixing, Casimir interaction), enabling analytic control across asymptotic regimes.

Abstract: We study Maxwell--Chern--Simons theories on a strip $\mathcal M=\mathbb R^{1,1}\times[0,h]$ within the Symanzik framework for quantum field theory with boundaries. We add the most general local boundary functional within the quadratic one-tangential-derivative truncation adopted throughout the paper and derive the admissible boundary conditions from locality and the variational principle, with gauge symmetry implemented through boundary Ward identities. The strip supports two boundary $U(1)$ current algebras, with opposite levels fixed by the Chern--Simons coupling $κ$ and by boundary orientation, while edge velocities depend on local boundary couplings. They are equal and opposite on the flip-symmetric branch, which is the natural choice for a spatially symmetric strip. The same boundary data determine the reflection coefficients for bulk modes and yield a closed determinant (scattering) representation of the finite interaction energy. In the Maxwell limit ($κ=0$) the inter-edge interaction is long-ranged and produces a power-law Casimir force, whereas in Maxwell--Chern--Simons theory the topological mass $m=κg^2$ generates Yukawa suppression at large separations $mh\gg1$. The determinant form also gives analytic control of the asymptotic regimes and separates topological boundary data (levels and anomaly inflow) from dynamical bulk effects (dispersion, boundary mixing and Casimir interaction).

Motivation: Previous amplitude analyses of B → Kππ decays left unresolved questions regarding the modeling of S-wave contributions—particularly the relation between the K*₀(1430)⁰ resonance and the non-resonant scalar K⁺π⁻ component—and relied on unitarity-based assumptions that may bias interpretations; the motivation is to test and improve the theoretical description of scalar dynamics in charmless B decays.

Method: Amplitude analysis of B⁺ → K⁺π⁺π⁻ using unbinned maximum-likelihood fit to the full 3 fb⁻¹ LHCb dataset; key methodological innovation is relaxing fixed unitarity constraints between the K*₀(1430)⁰ pole and the broad scalar K⁺π⁻ amplitude, allowing independent parameterization; includes coupled-channel treatment of π⁺π⁻ scalar amplitudes using established resonances (e.g., f₀(500), f₀(980), f₀(1370)) instead of the phenomenological f_X(1300).

Result: Improved goodness-of-fit and significantly better agreement between model and data upon relaxing unitarity assumptions; updated B⁺ → K*₀(1430)⁰π⁺ branching fraction contradicts prior experimental consensus that K*₀(1430)π dominates B → Kππ phase space; consistency with QCD factorisation predictions (not pQCD); elimination of f_X(1300) component; identification and characterization of ten distinct intermediate resonant decay pathways (e.g., B⁺ → K*⁺(892)ρ⁰, B⁺ → K⁺f₀(980), etc.).

Conclusion: The standard unitarity-motivated coupling assumption between K*₀(1430)⁰ and the K⁺π⁻ S-wave is overly restrictive and biases amplitude models; a more flexible, data-driven treatment of scalar amplitudes resolves tensions with theory, aligns with QCD factorisation expectations, removes need for unphysical ad-hoc states, and enables robust identification of multiple intermediate resonant contributions—establishing a new benchmark for B → Kππ amplitude analyses.

Abstract: An amplitude analysis of $B^+ \!\to K^+ π^+ π^-$ decays is presented, based on $pp$ collision data recorded with the LHCb detector in 2011--2012, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of $3\,\text{fb}^{-1}$. Previous studies of the $B \!\to K ππ$ sector have left key unresolved questions concerning the model of the S-wave contributions. A pivotal finding is that relaxing unitarity-based assumptions about the relation between the $K^*_0(1430)^0$ resonance and the slowly varying scalar part in $K^+π^-$ leads to considerably better agreement between the model and data. The $B^+ \!\to K^*_0(1430)^0 π^+$ branching fraction now challenges the experimental consensus that $B \!\to K^*_0(1430) π$ decays dominate the $B \!\to K ππ$ phase space, aligning with the predictions of QCD factorisation rather than perturbative QCD, thus reversing the agreement found in previous measurements. With this increased flexibility, it also becomes possible to model the scalar $π^+ π^-$ amplitude using established states, eliminating the need for the ad-hoc ``$f_X(1300)$'' component included in previous analyses of the $B \!\to Kππ$ sector. These advances facilitate the discovery of ten intermediate decays.
