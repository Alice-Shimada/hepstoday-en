Method: Constructed a high-resolution (0.2°×0.2°) local crustal model by integrating SWChinaCVM-2.0 with CRUST1.0; computed geoneutrino fluxes using this reference model and compared with ECM1 and CRUST1.0 alone; simulated inverse beta decay event spectra assuming 500 p.e./MeV energy scale and 500 t×1500 days exposure; performed spectral fitting to disentangle ²³⁸U, ²³²Th, and reactor antineutrino components.

Result: Refined geoneutrino flux predictions for JNE with quantified crustal contributions; spectrally resolvable separation of ²³⁸U, ²³²Th, and reactor antineutrino signals; validated input for extracting the mantle geoneutrino component and discriminating among BSE models.

Conclusion: The high-resolution local crustal model significantly improves geoneutrino prediction fidelity at Jinping, enabling more precise mantle signal isolation and stronger constraints on Earth's radiogenic heat budget and compositional models.

Abstract: We present refined predictions of the geoneutrino flux for the 500-tonne Jinping Neutrino Experiment (JNE). We construct a local crustal model with a resolution of \(0.2^\circ\times0.2^\circ\) based on the SWChinaCVM-2.0 seismic velocity model and integrate it with CRUST1.0 to establish our reference model. For comparison, we also calculate the geoneutrino flux using ECM1 and CRUST1.0 as standalone global crustal models. Assuming an energy scale of 500~p.e./MeV and an exposure of 500~t$\times$1500~days, we predict the inverse beta decay event spectrum, including both the geoneutrino signal and the reactor antineutrino background. The distinct spectral shapes of the $^{238}$U, $^{232}$Th, and reactor antineutrino components allow their respective contributions to be obtained through spectral fitting. These predictions provide inputs for the JNE data analysis, particularly for extracting the mantle geoneutrino signal and testing competing bulk silicate Earth models.
