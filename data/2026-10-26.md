Motivation: Defect-field interaction critically governs system behavior, yet existing methods lack rigorous dynamical models for *simultaneous*, conservative evolution of defect density and background fields; ambiguities in defect density definition further hinder progress.

Method: Integrates Euler-Poincaré variational formalism (for conservative continuum dynamics) with gauge-theoretic description of topological defects (to unambiguously define defect density and incorporate topological constraints); applied to Gross-Pitaevskii and Ericksen-Leslie systems under no-reconnection assumption.

Result: A consistent, conservative field-theoretic model for coupled defect–field evolution is derived, enabling well-defined defect density and physically admissible dynamics without requiring ad hoc closure assumptions or phenomenological couplings.

Conclusion: The proposed framework provides a geometrically grounded, variational foundation for modeling topological defect dynamics in ordered media, extending applicability beyond standard tracking methods and offering a pathway toward incorporating defect creation/annihilation in future extensions.

Abstract: While defect dynamics in condensed matter systems is widely studied and several methods are available to track the defect position over time, the formulation of dynamical models for the coupled evolution of both defect and background field quantities seems to be more challenging. Yet, the overall behavior of a system containing moving defects is strongly influenced by this defect-field interaction. Here, we propose a modeling framework for the conservative coupled evolution of the defect density and the background fields upon focusing on two relevant cases of study: the Gross-Pitaevskii theory of quantum fluids and the Ericksen-Leslie model of nematic liquid crystals. To achieve the coupled evolution, we combine the Euler-Poincaré variational setting of continuum dynamics with the gauge theory of topological defects. While the latter allows us to overcome certain ambiguities in definition of the defect density, the Euler-Poincaré method provides a unified modeling approach in the absence of defect reconnection.
