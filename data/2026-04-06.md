<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To clarify whether reported signatures of symmetric mass generation (SMG) in staggered-fermion lattice QCD genuinely realize SMG, and to identify robust theoretical criteria and observable consequences—particularly distinguishing type-II SMG.

Method: Analytical verification of SMG criteria (e.g., anomaly cancellation without spontaneous symmetry breaking, mass generation while preserving chiral symmetry) applied to the staggered fermion action and its Lee–Sharpe continuum limit; interpretation of numerical lattice results to propose renormalization group (RG) flow near the SMG transition; identification of low-energy effective degrees of freedom (tetraquark Goldstones) via symmetry analysis.

Result: The staggered fermion action—including its Lee–Sharpe continuum limit—satisfies all necessary conditions for SMG; numerical evidence supports an SMG transition; a plausible RG flow is proposed; Goldstone tetraquark mesons are identified as a definitive low-energy signature of the type-II SMG phase.

Conclusion: Staggered fermions provide a valid lattice realization of SMG, with type-II SMG characterized by emergent tetraquark Goldstone bosons; this bridges lattice observations with continuum effective field theory and offers testable phenomenology for future QCD simulations.

Abstract: Signatures of symmetric mass generation (SMG) have recently been reported in lattice QCD calculations employing staggered fermions. We discuss the general criteria for SMG, and demonstrate that these conditions are indeed met by the staggered fermion action, in particular by the continuum action derived by Lee and Sharpe. We propose possible RG flow around the SMG transition, guided by the numerical results.
