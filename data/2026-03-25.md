Motivation: To test the Vector Meson Dominance (VMD) mechanism in radiative decays of charmed mesons to axial-vector mesons — a theoretically unexplored regime where VMD predictions for D → γK₁ transitions remain unverified experimentally.

Method: An event-by-event maximum-likelihood fit is performed on 20.3 fb⁻¹ of e⁺e⁻ collision data at √s = 3.773 GeV, collected with the BESIII detector at BEPCII; signal candidates are reconstructed via K₁ → K⁺π⁻π⁰ (for K₁⁰) and K₁ → Kₛ⁰π⁺π⁰ (for K₁⁺), with photon detection in the electromagnetic calorimeter; background suppression employs kinematic constraints (e.g., energy-momentum conservation, M²(Kππ) window), particle identification, and sideband subtraction; upper limits are derived using a Bayesian approach with uniform prior and Gaussian systematic uncertainty treatment.

Result: No statistically significant signals are observed for either decay channel; upper limits at 90% confidence level on the branching fractions are determined as ℬ(D⁰→γK̄₁(1270)⁰) < 7.7×10⁻⁴ and ℬ(D⁺→γK₁(1270)⁺) < 3.9×10⁻⁵; systematic uncertainties (dominated by tracking efficiency, π⁰ reconstruction, and K₁ line shape modeling) are evaluated and incorporated.

Conclusion: The non-observation sets the first experimental constraints on radiative D → γK₁ decays, providing benchmark limits for testing VMD-based theoretical models and effective field theories describing charm-quark radiative transitions to axial-vector mesons; it highlights the need for higher-statistics datasets or alternative production mechanisms to probe these suppressed channels.
