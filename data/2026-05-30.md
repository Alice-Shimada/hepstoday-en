### [10] [Connection Factorization in Constrained Quantum Mechanics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29241)
*A. Nuramatov*

Main category: math-ph

TL;DR: This paper develops a geometric framework for constrained quantum motion on curves and surfaces using connection factorization of Laplacians, revealing Riccati-type geometric potentials and spinorial reductions where the standard Jensen–Koppe–da Costa term cancels, exposing hidden nilpotent differential complexes.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To rigorously formulate quantum mechanics on curved submanifolds by exploiting differential-geometric structures—specifically connections and frame bundles—to unify and generalize known geometric potential effects (e.g., da Costa term) and uncover deeper algebraic structures (e.g., nilpotent complexes).

Method: Uses orthonormal moving frames and connection factorization: decomposes the Laplace–Beltrami operator into a half-connection form involving Darboux rotational connections; applies structure equations for Dirac reduction; analyzes Fermi-coordinate-based constraints and spinor representations; derives effective Hamiltonians via geometric elimination of first-order terms.

Result: 1) Exact half-connection factorization of Laplacians via Darboux connection one-forms; 2) Emergence of quadratic geometric invariants analogous to supersymmetric Riccati potentials; 3) Cancellation of the scalar Jensen–Koppe–da Costa term in Fermi-reduced Dirac dynamics, leaving a first-order spinorial derivative; 4) Indication of underlying nilpotent covariant differential complexes tied to geometric connections.

Conclusion: Constrained quantum dynamics on submanifolds is governed not only by scalar curvature potentials but also by intrinsic connection-driven first-order structures and hidden cohomological (nilpotent) geometries—suggesting a richer algebraic–differential framework beyond standard effective Hamiltonian approaches.

Abstract: We investigate constrained quantum motion on curves and surfaces using connection factorization methods. We show that Laplace operators admit an exact half-connection factorization generated by connection one-forms. The first-order part of the Laplacian is identified with the Darboux rotational connection of the orthogonal frame. Elimination of this rotational connection naturally produces quadratic geometric invariants analogous to supersymmetric Riccati potentials. Using orthonormal moving frames, we derive effective geometric contributions for planar curves, spatial curves, and embedded surfaces. We further analyze Dirac reductions using structure equations and show that for Fermi-type reductions the scalar Jensen--Koppe--da Costa contribution is cancelled in the reduced Dirac sector, leaving a residual first-order spinorial derivative structure. The resulting framework suggests the existence of hidden nilpotent covariant differential complexes naturally generated by geometric connection structures.

### [11] [Multi-Loop Negative Geometries](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28926)
*Lance J. Dixon,Umut Oktem,Shruti Paranjape,Jaroslav Trnka,Yongqun Xu,Shun-Qing Zhang*

Main category: hep-th

TL;DR: This paper studies the IR-finite quantity F(g,z) — the one-loop-integrated logarithm of the 4-point planar N=4 SYM amplitude — using amplituhedron and negative geometry frameworks; it computes all three-loop negative geometries, links diagram cycle count to polylog depth, extracts the cusp anomalous dimension Γ_cusp via z-integration, separates odd/even zeta contributions to show higher-cycle suppression, and performs all-loop resummations of one-cycle diagrams.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To understand the infrared-finite structure of scattering amplitudes in planar N=4 SYM through geometric (amplituhedron/negative geometry) principles, particularly for the Lagrangian-insertion Wilson loop observable F(g,z), which encodes universal IR-safe information and connects weak/strong coupling regimes.

Method: Systematic construction and classification of negative geometries corresponding to the integrand of F(g,z); explicit computation of all three-loop negative geometry contributions; analysis of internal cycle topology versus polylogarithmic weight/depth; z-integration to extract Γ_cusp; decomposition into odd/even zeta-valued sectors; all-order resummation of infinite families of one-cycle diagrams using geometric and combinatorial techniques.

Result: Complete enumeration and evaluation of three-loop negative geometries for F(g,z); demonstration that the number of internal cycles determines the maximal polylogarithmic depth; analytic extraction of Γ_cusp at three loops from z-integration; evidence that higher-cycle diagrams are suppressed in odd/even zeta sectors; closed-form all-loop resummations for certain infinite series of one-cycle contributions, revealing novel analytic structure and convergence properties.

Conclusion: Negative geometries provide a natural, IR-finite, and combinatorially transparent framework for computing F(g,z); the cycle structure of diagrams governs transcendental weight and controls perturbative organization; separating zeta parity enables effective truncation; all-loop resummations of simple topologies expose underlying simplicity beyond fixed-order expansion.

Abstract: Scattering amplitudes in planar ${\cal N}=4$ supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory are dual to expectation values of null polygonal Wilson loops. The Amplituhedron provides a geometric construction for the all-loop integrand as the canonical form on the geometric region in the Grassmannian defined by a certain set of inequalities. For a closely related object, the logarithm of the scattering amplitude, the integrand is reproduced in a similar way using negative geometries. When integrated over all loop momenta except one, the result is infrared (IR) finite and equal to the expectation value of a certain Wilson loop with a Lagrangian insertion. At four points, this quantity, ${\cal F}(g,z)$ only depends on a single cross ratio $z$ and the 't Hooft coupling $g$. At weak coupling, it is known up to three loops from perturbative Wilson loop computations and at strong coupling through the AdS/CFT correspondence at leading order. In this paper, we explore this object further through the lens of the Amplituhedron and negative geometries, which provide very natural IR finite building blocks. We perform an explicit three-loop computation of all negative geometries and show that the number of internal cycles in the diagram is closely linked to the depth of polylogarithms. We calculate the cusp anomalous dimension $Γ_{\rm cusp}$ by integrating ${\cal F}(g,z)$ over $z$. We show that the higher-cycle diagrams are suppressed if we consider separate odd and even zeta contributions. Furthermore, we focus on certain convergent infinite series of one-cycle diagrams, perform all-loop order resummations of such contributions, and discuss various features of the result.

### [12] [Analytic Bootstrap for $O(N)$ Boundary Conformal Field Theories with Interacting Boundaries](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28933)
*Xinyu Sun,Shao-Kai Jian,Hong Yao*

Main category: hep-th

TL;DR: This paper uses analytic bootstrap and RG methods to study O(N) boundary CFTs in d=4−ε and d=3−ε, uncovering new boundary fixed points (including non-unitary ones) and computing conformal data for the tricritical O(N) model for the first time.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To develop a unified framework for boundary CFTs with interacting boundaries and to systematically determine bulk and boundary operator expansions using universal constraints.

Method: Analytic conformal bootstrap combined with perturbative renormalization-group analysis in dimensional regularization (d = 4−ε and d = 3−ε).

Result: Derivation of universal constraints enabling infinite operator expansions to be expressed via finite inputs; discovery of novel boundary fixed points (including non-unitary) in d=4−ε driven by cubic boundary coupling; first computation of boundary conformal data for the tricritical O(N) model in d=3−ε.

Conclusion: The combined analytic bootstrap–RG approach provides a powerful, systematic, and unified prescription for solving interacting BCFTs, significantly simplifying the determination of conformal data and operator spectra.

Abstract: We investigate $O(N)$ boundary conformal field theories (BCFTs) with boundary interactions in $d=4-ε$ and $d=3-ε$ employing the analytic bootstrap. By deriving universal constraints on conformal data, we show that infinitely many operator expansions can be expressed in terms of a finite set of inputs. Complementing the analytic bootstrap with a perturbative renormalization-group analysis, we identify totally new boundary fixed points in $d=4-ε$, including non-unitary ones, generated by a boundary cubic coupling, and compute their conformal data to leading order. Moreover, we leverage our solution in $d=3-ε$ to extract, for the first time, the boundary conformal data for the tricritical $O(N)$ model. Altogether, our approach provides a unified prescription for BCFTs with interacting boundaries and streamlines the determination of bulk and boundary operator expansions.

### [13] [Gravity Decoupling and Axionic Shift Symmetries](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28938)
*Christian Aoufia,Gonzalo F. Casas,Fernando Marchesano*

Main category: hep-th

TL;DR: This paper studies how approximate axionic shift symmetries and associated axionic strings constrain effective field theories in type II Calabi-Yau compactifications, particularly in gravity-decoupling limits; it shows that vector fields derived from string tensions split into orthogonal subsets—some decoupling from gravity—and links tension Laplacians to divergent moduli space curvature.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To understand how approximate axionic shift symmetries and their associated topological defects (axionic strings) govern the structure of effective field theories in weak-gravity limits of string compactifications, especially regarding gravity decoupling, kinetic mixing, and moduli space geometry.

Method: Analysis of axionic shift symmetries and their associated axionic strings in type II Calabi-Yau compactifications; derivation of constraints on Kähler potential gradients from string tension; construction and geometric analysis of vector fields on moduli space analogous to BPS mass vectors; derivation of inner-product bounds to identify orthogonal subsets; computation of Laplacian of string tensions and its relation to moduli space curvature.

Result: Vector fields defined by gradients of axionic string tensions decompose into mutually orthogonal subsets in asymptotic limits; one subset decouples from gravity; the Laplacian of certain string tensions diverges precisely where moduli space curvature diverges.

Conclusion: Approximate axionic shift symmetries encode universal structural constraints on EFTs in gravity-decoupling limits; orthogonality of tension-gradient vectors reflects sectoral decoupling, and divergence of their Laplacian signals breakdown of effective geometry due to strong curvature.

Abstract: We analyse the rôle of approximate axionic shift symmetries in gravity-decoupling limits arising in type II Calabi-Yau compactifications. Associated with each shift symmetry there is an axionic string whose tension constrains the gradient of the Kähler potential, as expected in regimes where gravity becomes weakly coupled. The gradients of these tensions define vector fields on moduli space, analogous to those associated with BPS particle masses. Together, they characterise the evolution of different effective field theory sectors along asymptotic limits, encoding both their coupling to gravity and their kinetic mixing. By deriving upper bounds on the inner products of these vector fields, we show that they split into mutually orthogonal subsets, one of which decouples from gravity. Finally, we relate the Laplacian of certain axionic string tensions to a divergent moduli space curvature.

</details>
