### [1] [Searching for Anomalies with Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23593)
*Vinicius Mikuni,Benjamin Nachman*

Main category: hep-ex

TL;DR: This paper investigates anomalous behavior observed in a mass sideband when applying the OmniLearned foundation model to CMS experiment data, performs a full background estimation analysis, and finds that while the background model fits validation regions well, it fails in the signal region—prompting calls for further scrutiny.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To understand unexpected behavior observed in a mass sideband during application of the OmniLearned foundation model to CMS data, and to rigorously assess whether it indicates new physics or a modeling deficiency.

Method: Full analysis including data-driven background estimation applied to the phase space selected by the large foundation model; validation in control (sideband) regions and evaluation in the signal region.

Result: Background estimation accurately describes data in validation regions but fails in the signal region, revealing a discrepancy that is not yet explained.

Conclusion: The observed discrepancy in the signal region remains unexplained by current background modeling; the results warrant independent verification and deeper investigation into both physics interpretations and model limitations.

Abstract: Foundation models have the potential to extend the discovery reach for anomaly detection searches. When studying the large OmniLearned foundation model on data from the CMS experiment, unexpected behavior was observed in a mass sideband. The purpose of this paper is to perform a full analysis, including a complete background estimate, on the phase space picked out by the large model. We find that the background estimation describes the data well in validation regions, but is unable to accurately model the signal region. We invite further scrutiny of these events and our methods.

</details>

TL;DR: This paper measures the collinear and transverse-momentum-dependent jet fragmentation functions and radial profiles for $B^\pm$ mesons in jets using LHCb data at 13 TeV, finding increasing gluon fragmentation contribution to $B^\pm$ production with rising jet $p_T$.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To complement recent heavy-flavor jet fragmentation measurements and probe the underlying hadronization mechanisms—particularly the relative roles of quark vs. gluon fragmentation—in $B^\pm$ meson production within jets.

Method: Analysis of $B^\pm \to J/\psi(\to \mu^+\mu^-)K^\pm$ decays in pp collisions at $\sqrt{s}=13$ TeV, using 5.4 fb$^{-1}$ of LHCb data (2016–2018); extraction of collinear and TMD fragmentation functions and jet radial profiles via standard jet reconstruction (anti-$k_T$), $B^\pm$ invariant mass fitting, and differential kinematic unfolding.
